Founder Decisions

01 501(c)(3) Name MODERATE ▼

The Question

What should the 501(c)(3) nonprofit entity be called? The name appears on the Articles of Incorporation, Form 1023, all grant applications, and public-facing materials.

Options

A — Mission-focused, no MedEdPrep branding (e.g. "Foundation for Out-of-Hospital Care"): maximum independence optics for IRS Form 1023, broadest programmatic flexibility, but no brand transfer.
B — MedEdPrep-adjacent ("MedEdPrep Foundation"): instant recognition, but increases IRS scrutiny of private benefit and looks like a "house nonprofit."
C — Hybrid ("MedEd Access Foundation"): moderate proximity, but the "MedEd" prefix may still trigger association concerns.

Precedent

Gary Sinise Foundation and Firehouse Subs Public Safety Foundation show mission-first names build stronger independent identities. NASM Foundation (tied to for-profit NASM) is the weaker model.
Recommendation: Option A — mission-focused name with no MedEdPrep branding. The formation design doc explicitly warns against "shell nonprofit" perception; the three program areas span a broader mission than MedEdPrep's products. Urgency: Moderate — needed before Articles are filed (Week 1-2). Hard to reverse once filed.
02 Board Recruitment — 3 Independent Directors BLOCKING ▼

The Question

Confirm 3 independent directors (4 total with founder) to proceed with 501(c)(3) formation. Board composition is the single largest factor the IRS evaluates for governance on Form 1023.

Options

A — Minimum viable (1 founder + 2 independent = 3): fastest, but only 67% independence with zero margin.
B — 4 directors (1 + 3 independent): 75% independence, comfortable margin, covers legal/governance, finance/grants, and EMS-education gaps.
C — 5 directors (1 + 4 independent): 80% independence, all four expertise gaps covered, but slowest to recruit.

Where To Look

Seat 2 (Legal): Georgia Center for Nonprofits board matching. Seat 3 (Finance): CPAs with nonprofit clients. Seat 4 (EMS): non-MedEdPrep EMS educators, NAEMSE members in Georgia.
Recommendation: Option B — file with 4 directors (75% independence), recruit the 5th (community/hospice) within 6 months. Do not delay filing for a perfect 5-person board. Urgency: Blocking now — the single item gating all 501(c)(3) formation past Week 2.
03 Certification Body Brand (Company C) BLOCKING ▼

The Question

What should Company C (the certification body) be called? It needs a separate brand identity from MedEdPrep.

Why It Matters

NCCA Standard 3 requires a structural firewall between certification and education. Using "MedEdPrep" in the certification body's name would immediately raise NCCA auditor concerns about independence. The brand will appear on certificates, state regulations, and NCCA accreditation documents for decades, and must signal credibility, independence, and psychometric rigor to state EMS boards.
Status: Brand selection open — tracked alongside the Company C formation decision (Q4 2026). Currently referenced as "[TBD Brand] Certification" across the corpus; must be resolved before any external-facing certification documents are produced. See founder-decisions-research.md for the full option analysis.

Fix: State definitively that IBSC is NCCA-accredited (CP-C, CCP-C, FP-C, TP-C); remove the parenthetical; cite the NAEMT August 2018 announcement.
