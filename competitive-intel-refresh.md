## 1. NREMT Exam Redesign (Fully Live April 7, 2025)

### What Changed

The National Registry of Emergency Medical Technicians completed the largest exam restructuring in its history. Both BLS-level (EMR, EMT) and ALS-level (AEMT, Paramedic) certification exams were redesigned simultaneously -- affecting every EMS candidate in the United States.

### New Domain Structure

The legacy content areas (Airway/Trauma/Cardiology/Medical/OB-Pediatrics) were replaced with a new four-domain framework:

| Domain | Weight (EMT) | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| **Primary Assessment** | Heaviest -- nearly half the EMT exam | Scene size-up, primary survey, initial clinical decision-making |
| **Medical/Trauma Management** | Significant | Condition-specific assessment and treatment |
| **Resuscitation & Ventilation** | Moderate | Airway management, cardiac arrest, ventilation strategies |
| **EMS Operations** | Moderate | Scene management, triage, transport decisions, crew resource management |

**Pediatric content is now integrated throughout all domains** rather than isolated in a separate category. This reflects real clinical practice -- pediatric patients present across all four domains, not as a discrete topic.

### Technology-Enhanced Items (TEIs)

TEIs are now deployed at **all certification levels** -- EMR, EMT, AEMT, and Paramedic. This is a significant change from the legacy exam, which relied almost exclusively on traditional multiple-choice.

New item types include:
- **Drag-and-drop** -- ordering steps, categorizing information
- **Table classification** -- sorting data into categories within a grid
- **Build-a-list** -- constructing an ordered sequence from available options
- **Multiple-select** -- selecting all correct answers from a set (not traditional single-best-answer)

### Why This Matters for MedEdPrep

**Content mapping.** Every item in MedEdPrep's 10,600+ question bank must map to the new four-domain structure. Legacy Airway/Trauma/Cardiology tags are no longer sufficient for diagnostic reporting. The new domain taxonomy is the standard that programs, accreditors, and students will evaluate against.

**TEI validation.** MedEdPrep's Pulse platform supports 10 distinct question types -- including drag-and-drop, ordering, multi-select, and classification items. The NREMT's TEI expansion directly validates this investment. Competitors still offering only traditional multiple-choice are now visibly misaligned with the certification exam format.

**Diagnostic reporting must reflect new domains.** The "aha moment" for instructors is MedEdPrep's multi-dimensional diagnostic reports. Those reports must now speak the language of Primary Assessment / Medical-Trauma Management / Resuscitation & Ventilation / EMS Operations. Programs evaluating diagnostic tools will expect this alignment.

**Competitive gap widens.** Neither Platinum/EMSTesting, Fisdap, nor MyLab Brady has published documentation confirming alignment with the new domain structure. Fisdap's unit exams were historically structured around the legacy domains (Airway, Cardiology, Trauma, Medical, OB/Pediatric, Operations). Whether they have been remapped is unclear and unconfirmed. This creates a window where MedEdPrep can be the first platform demonstrably aligned to the new exam.

## 2. ALS Psychomotor Exam Discontinued

### What Changed

NREMT permanently discontinued the ALS (AEMT and Paramedic) psychomotor examination effective **July 1, 2024**. This was not a pandemic-era temporary suspension -- it is a permanent structural change.

AEMT and Paramedic candidates now earn NREMT certification through **cognitive examination only**. BLS-level psychomotor testing (EMR, EMT) remains in place, administered by state EMS offices.

### Why This Matters for MedEdPrep

**Cognitive assessment is the entire certification pathway for ALS.** With psychomotor testing eliminated, the cognitive exam is the sole gatekeeper for AEMT and Paramedic certification. This elevates the importance of cognitive diagnostic feedback -- understanding not just whether a candidate will pass, but precisely where their cognitive gaps are.

**Strengthens MedEdPrep's core value proposition.** MedEdPrep's differentiator is diagnostic cognitive feedback: multi-dimensional reporting by competency domain, cognitive level, and confidence. When cognitive testing was one of two gates (cognitive + psychomotor), diagnostic feedback was valuable but not singular. Now that cognitive testing is the only gate, diagnostic feedback becomes the primary tool for readiness assessment.

**Competitors offering generic "study more cardiology" remediation are further behind.** Fisdap's "learning prescriptions" -- topic-level recommendations without diagnostic specificity -- were already a weakness. With cognitive testing as the sole certification pathway, the gap between generic remediation and targeted diagnostic-driven remediation becomes more consequential for programs.

## 4. IBSC Correction

### What Changed (From Earlier Research)

Previous MedEdPrep research incorrectly identified the International Board of Specialty Certification (IBSC) as lacking NCCA accreditation. **This was wrong. IBSC IS NCCA-accredited.**

### Corrected Understanding

**IBSC credentials:**
- **CP-C** (Certified Paramedic - Critical Care)
- **CCP-C** (Critical Care Paramedic - Certified)
- **FP-C** (Flight Paramedic - Certified)
- **TP-C** (Tactical Paramedic - Certified)

**IBSC is NCCA-accredited**, meaning its certification exams meet the National Commission for Certifying Agencies' standards for personnel certification programs.

### Revised Strategic Angle

The Company C play is **not** an accreditation gap argument. It is two distinct gaps:

**1. Scope integration gap.** IBSC certifies specialty/advanced practice providers -- critical care, flight, tactical. It does not certify base-level EMTs or Paramedics (that is NREMT's domain). There is no single certification body that covers the full scope from EMR through specialty practice. A platform that provides diagnostic assessment across the entire scope -- NREMT base certification AND specialty competency domains -- fills a structural gap that neither NREMT nor IBSC addresses.

**2. Diagnostic feedback gap.** IBSC provides pass/fail certification decisions. It does not provide diagnostic reports identifying specific competency gaps. A provider who fails the FP-C exam knows they failed; they do not know whether they failed because of ventilator management, pharmacology, or flight physiology. MedEdPrep's diagnostic engine can provide this specificity -- not as a replacement for IBSC certification, but as a complementary preparation and remediation tool.

## 5. CE Market Landscape

### The Competitive Vacuum

**ZERO competitors offer adaptive or psychometric CE.** This finding was verified across 40+ EMS CE providers during the 2026-04-14 market mapping exercise (interactive dashboard at `entity-ce-market.html`). Every provider in the market sells CE hours -- video courses, reading modules, or test-only activities -- without any adaptive delivery, IRT-based assessment, or diagnostic competency reporting.

This is not a marginal differentiation. This is an open field.

### Market Structure

**The Lexipol/Vector duopoly (60-70% of CE revenue):**

Lexipol (which acquired Vector Solutions) captures the majority of EMS CE revenue through enterprise B2B agency bundles. Their model is straightforward: sell agency-wide training subscriptions that include CE compliance tracking, LMS delivery, and content libraries. Agencies buy because it solves a compliance problem (ensuring every provider has their CE hours), not because it solves a competency problem.

This creates the market's central tension: **the dominant providers optimize for compliance, not competency.** MedEdPrep's positioning is the inverse -- optimize for competency, and compliance follows.

**The "good enough" tier ($35-72/yr):**

| Provider | Price | Model |
|----------|-------|-------|
| Recert.com (PSG/Ascend, NAEMT-partnered) | $61.95/yr | 200+ scenario-based online courses |
| DistanceCME | ~$35-50/yr | Online CE courses, multiple disciplines |
| CE Solutions | ~$50-72/yr | Online CE, "test out" option (but not adaptive) |
| Guardian CME | Free (CAPCE) | Free courses, ad-supported or lead-gen model |

**This tier is the real competitive threat** -- not free CE. Providers in this range have established that $35-72/year is the "good enough" price point for individual EMS professionals who want to check the CE box without spending much time or money. MedEdPrep's CE product must justify a premium over this tier through demonstrable value (diagnostic reports, competency validation, remediation targeting) rather than competing on price.

**Pricing norms across the market:**
- Individual providers: $35-160/year
- Agency/institutional seats: $50-100/seat
- Premium individual platforms (FOAMfrat, Prehospital Prep): $100-160/year
- Free tier (Guardian CME, limited Bound Tree EDU): exists but limited

### TAM

- ~1M active EMS professionals in the U.S.
- 557K NREMT-certified (CE required for recertification)
- Market valued at $50-150M annually
- Growth driven by: online CE limit removal (2023), state-level CE mandates, increasing agency compliance requirements

### Structural Tailwind: NREMT Online CE Limit Removal (2023)

NREMT permanently removed caps on how much CE can be completed online. Prior to 2023, providers were limited in the number of online CE hours that counted toward recertification. This limit was temporarily lifted during COVID and then permanently removed.

This is a structural tailwind for any online CE provider -- it removes the regulatory ceiling on digital CE delivery. Combined with CAPCE's confirmation that assessment-only CE is allowed (no video or lecture required), the path is clear for MedEdPrep's adaptive CE model.

### Key Market Corrections

Common errors encountered in external research that should not be repeated:

- **Bound Tree Medical** is owned by Sarnova / $989M / Patricia Industries / Investor AB -- NOT Henry Schein
- **JEMS Training** (Clarion Events / Blackstone) and **Ally by EMS World** (HMP Global) are competitors, not sister products
- **Bound Tree EDU** is NOT fully free -- 5 free courses, then $159.99/year
- **GA EMS Academy** ($40/yr, not CAPCE-accredited, website vintage 2007) is the Georgia local incumbent -- underserving the market MedEdPrep is entering first

## 6. NASEMSO Policy Trajectory

### The Institutional Direction

The National Association of State EMS Officials has been publishing a clear institutional trajectory for two decades:

**EMS Education Agenda 2000** established the framework for competency-based EMS education. **EMS Agenda 2050** extended this vision toward a future where EMS education and continuing competency are measured by demonstrated ability, not hours counted.

The trajectory is explicit: **move away from hour-counting toward demonstrated competency.**

### Why This Matters for MedEdPrep

**MedEdPrep implements NASEMSO's published vision. It is not proposing something new.** This is the most important framing for policy conversations. When MedEdPrep presents adaptive competency assessment as an alternative to traditional CE hour-counting, it is implementing what NASEMSO has been publishing for 25+ years. The argument is not "we have a novel idea" -- the argument is "we built the technology to deliver what your policy documents already describe."

### Joe House and Kansas

**Joe House** served as NASEMSO Immediate Past President from 2023-2025. He is not a regional figure -- he has working relationships with state EMS directors nationwide.

Joe House is actively championing "Rethinking Continuing Education" at the Kansas Board of Emergency Medical Services (KBEMS Board Task 2024AS0001). His vision aligns precisely with MedEdPrep's model:

- **Adaptive exams as a formal state assessment instrument** -- if someone demonstrates competency via adaptive exam, they should not need blanket CE hours
- **Exam identifies deficiencies** (e.g., pediatric trauma) followed by **targeted remediation** rather than generic hours
- **"Competency Education"** -- same CE acronym, fundamentally different philosophy

Kansas statutory framework (K.S.A. 65-6110, 65-6111) gives KBEMS broad authority to set qualifications by regulation without prescribing specific CE methods -- meaning this does not require legislative action.

### The Full State Stack

The Kansas opportunity represents MedEdPrep's full product suite deployed at scale:

| Product | Role in State Stack |
|---------|-------------------|
| MedEdPrep adaptive exams | State testing / competency verification |
| Pulse CE | Targeted remediation for identified gaps |
| CoAssist | Clinical competency tracking, school/program oversight |
| State admin dashboard | Unified state-level visibility |

Kansas as a showcase state, combined with NASEMSO sponsorship ($20K top-tier, targeting 2027 conference), creates a pipeline to other states. Success here is the proof point for the national "competency education" model.

## 8. Implications for MedEdPrep

### Content: Map to New NREMT Domains

All items, diagnostic reports, and content organization must reflect the new four-domain structure (Primary Assessment, Medical/Trauma Management, Resuscitation & Ventilation, EMS Operations) with pediatric content integrated throughout. Legacy domain tags should be retained for historical data but new reporting surfaces the new structure.

### Product: TEI Investment Validated

Pulse's 10 question types are aligned with NREMT's TEI expansion. This should be a prominent marketing point: "Practice with the same item types you'll see on the real exam." Competitors offering only multiple-choice are now visibly behind the exam format.

### Positioning: Cognitive-Only Certification Strengthens Diagnostic Feedback

With the ALS psychomotor exam gone, cognitive assessment is the entire certification pathway for AEMT and Paramedic. Diagnostic cognitive feedback is no longer "nice to have" -- it is the primary tool for readiness assessment. Marketing and sales messaging should reflect this shift.

### CE: Wide Open for Adaptive Approach

Zero competitors offer adaptive CE. CAPCE has confirmed assessment-only CE is allowed. NREMT has permanently removed online CE limits. The structural conditions are all favorable. The constraint is CAPCE organizational accreditation (imminent) and production velocity, not market readiness.

### Policy: Competency-Based Positioning Aligns with National Trajectory

MedEdPrep is not proposing a new idea. It is implementing NASEMSO's 25-year published vision with technology that did not previously exist in EMS. The Kansas/Joe House partnership is the proof point. NASEMSO sponsorship in 2027 is the amplification mechanism. Frame every policy conversation as "we built what you've been asking for."

### Timing: The Window Is Real but Finite

Every month of adoption -- programs using MedEdPrep, students generating diagnostic data, agencies running CE through the platform, states evaluating the competency model -- builds switching costs and data moats that competitors cannot replicate quickly. The psychometric moat (10,600+ calibrated items, 1.55M responses, IRT parameters, metacognition dataset) deepens with every response collected. The data advantage is compounding.

**Move fast. The structural conditions have never been better aligned.**
