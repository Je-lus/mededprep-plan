## 1. Self-Regulated Learning Theory: Does the Content Gate Promote Metacognitive Awareness?

### Theoretical Foundation

Self-regulated learning (SRL) theory, as formalized by Zimmerman (2000, 2002) and Pintrich (2000, 2004), describes learning as a cyclical process with three phases:

1. **Forethought phase** --- goal setting, strategic planning, self-efficacy beliefs
2. **Performance phase** --- self-monitoring, strategy use, attention control
3. **Self-reflection phase** --- self-evaluation, causal attribution, adaptive inference

The critical insight from SRL research is that *learning fails when the self-reflection phase is absent or superficial*. When learners can immediately retry without reflection, they bypass the mechanism that converts failure into improved strategy. Zimmerman's research consistently showed that expert learners spend disproportionate time in self-reflection compared to novices.

### How the Content Gate Maps to SRL

The MedEdPrep retake policy directly operationalizes the SRL cycle:

| SRL Phase | Policy Mechanism | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Forethought | Learner chooses to attempt assessment | Activates goal-directed behavior |
| Performance | 30-question randomized assessment | Self-monitoring during test-taking |
| Self-reflection (if pass) | Diagnostic report + rationale review | Structured reflection on performance |
| Self-reflection (if fail) | **Forced educational content completion** | **Mandated deep reflection before retry** |
| Forethought (retry) | Returns to assessment with new items | Strategic re-engagement with revised approach |

The forced content gate after failure below 70% is not merely a delay mechanism --- it is a *structured self-reflection intervention*. Pintrich's (2004) framework specifically identifies "regulation of cognition" as requiring external scaffolding for novice and struggling learners. Learners who score below 70% are, by definition, those whose self-regulation has been insufficient. The content gate provides the scaffolding they demonstrably need.

### Evidence for Forced Reflection

- **Butler & Winne (1995)** established that feedback is only effective when it triggers cognitive recalibration. Immediate retakes without intervening content allow learners to attribute failure externally ("bad luck on questions") rather than recalibrate understanding.
- **Bjork & Bjork (2011)** on desirable difficulties showed that introducing obstacles between retrieval attempts (spacing, interleaving, generation) strengthens long-term retention. The content gate is a structurally imposed desirable difficulty.
- **Hattie & Timperley (2007)** meta-analysis on feedback effectiveness found that the most impactful feedback operates at the "self-regulation level" --- helping learners monitor and direct their own learning. Forcing content review after failure operates precisely at this level.

### Verdict

The content gate is a textbook implementation of SRL scaffolding. It forces the self-reflection phase that struggling learners would otherwise skip, and it does so with domain-relevant content rather than arbitrary delay. **Strongly supported by theory.**

## 2. Mastery Learning Frameworks: Bloom's Model and Progression

### Bloom's Mastery Learning (1968, 1984)

Benjamin Bloom's mastery learning framework rests on a core premise: given sufficient time, appropriate instruction, and adequate feedback, virtually all students can achieve mastery. The framework has five essential components:

1. **Clear learning objectives** defined in advance
2. **Formative assessment** to diagnose gaps
3. **Corrective instruction** targeted to identified weaknesses
4. **Parallel assessment** (not the same test) to verify mastery
5. **Enrichment** for those who demonstrate mastery early

### Alignment Analysis

| Bloom Component | MedEdPrep Implementation | Alignment |
|---|---|---|
| Clear objectives | CAPCE learning objectives per CE activity | Full |
| Formative assessment | 30-question adaptive assessment with diagnostic report | Full |
| Corrective instruction | **Required educational content for scores <70%** | **Full** |
| Parallel assessment | **Different questions on retake (randomized from bank)** | **Full** |
| Enrichment for early masters | Pass on first attempt = optional additional resources | Full |

The alignment is remarkably complete. Most CE providers implement only component 1 (learning objectives) and a degraded version of component 2 (a quiz with the same questions every time). MedEdPrep implements all five.

### The "Different Questions Each Time" Decision

This is where the policy diverges from --- and improves upon --- typical mastery learning implementations. In Bloom's original classroom model, the "parallel assessment" was a different but equivalent form of the same test. MedEdPrep achieves this through random sampling from a large item bank, which is psychometrically superior:

- **Equivalent difficulty** is maintained through calibrated item parameters (if IRT-calibrated) or through random sampling from a sufficiently large and balanced bank
- **Content validity** is preserved because items are mapped to the same CAPCE learning objectives
- **Memorization is eliminated** as a path to mastery, isolating genuine knowledge

### Comparison to Degraded Mastery Models

Most online CE and compliance training uses what could be called "pseudo-mastery": same quiz, unlimited retakes, no corrective instruction. This is mastery learning with components 3, 4, and 5 removed --- which is to say, it is not mastery learning at all. It is trial-and-error memorization of answer keys.

Kulik, Kulik & Bangert-Drowns (1990) meta-analysis of 108 mastery learning studies found effect sizes of 0.52 for college-level implementations. The effect was largest when corrective feedback was substantive (not just "wrong, try again") and when reassessment used parallel forms. Both conditions are met by this policy.

### Verdict

The retake policy is one of the most complete implementations of Bloom's mastery learning model in any online CE context. The combination of corrective content + parallel-form retake is the specific configuration that produces the largest effect sizes in the mastery learning literature. **Strongly supported by framework.**

## 3. Credentialing Integrity: How Professional Bodies Handle Retakes

### Cross-Profession Comparison

| Credential | Retake Policy | Wait Period | Content Requirement | Different Form? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **NREMT (EMS)** | Limited retakes per cycle (6 attempts in 2-year window); after 3 failures, must complete 48-hr refresher course | 15 days between attempts | Yes, after 3rd failure (refresher course) | Yes (item bank) |
| **NCLEX-RN (Nursing)** | Unlimited attempts; 45-day wait between attempts; must re-register and repay each time | 45 days minimum | No formal requirement (but most candidates self-study) | Yes (CAT from large bank) |
| **NBCOT (Occupational Therapy)** | Unlimited attempts; must wait for next testing window | ~3 months (quarterly windows) | No formal requirement | Yes (new form each window) |
| **ACLS/BLS/PALS (AHA)** | Typically one retake allowed per course; must remediate specific stations failed | Same day or next session | Yes (review of failed skills) | Partial (may see same scenarios) |
| **USMLE Step 1 (Medicine)** | 6 attempts lifetime; 1-year wait after 3rd failure | Minimum 1 year after multiple failures | No formal requirement | Yes |
| **CAPCE CE (typical provider)** | Unlimited retakes, immediate, same questions | None | None | No |

### Key Observations

**The MedEdPrep policy occupies a unique position.** It is:

- **More permissive** than NREMT, NCLEX, NBCOT, and USMLE on number of attempts (unlimited)
- **More rigorous** than typical CAPCE CE providers on remediation requirements (forced content)
- **Equivalent** to AHA resuscitation courses in requiring remediation before retry
- **Superior** to all listed credentials in ensuring different questions on retake

The NREMT model is the closest professional analog in EMS. NREMT requires a 48-hour refresher course after three failures --- a significantly heavier intervention than MedEdPrep's content completion requirement. MedEdPrep's policy is a lighter-touch version of the same principle: failure signals a knowledge gap that must be addressed before reassessment.

### CAPCE Compliance

CAPCE's accreditation standards require "satisfactory completion" of CE activities but do not prescribe specific retake policies. The standard allows providers discretion in defining completion criteria. A retake policy that requires educational content review after failure is fully compliant and, in fact, better demonstrates the "continued competency" intent of CAPCE accreditation than the prevailing industry norm of unlimited immediate retakes on identical questions.

### The Integrity Argument

From a credentialing integrity perspective, the question is: *Does the retake policy ensure that a passing score represents genuine competency?*

- **Unlimited immediate retakes with same questions:** No. A learner can achieve 100% through memorization without understanding a single concept. The credential is meaningless.
- **Unlimited retakes with different questions but no content gate:** Partial. Random guessing is eliminated, but a learner could repeatedly attempt without learning, relying on the stochastic possibility of getting "easier" questions.
- **Unlimited retakes with different questions AND content gate after failure:** Yes. The combination ensures that (a) passing requires actual knowledge (different questions), and (b) failing triggers a learning intervention (content gate) rather than enabling brute-force retrying.

### Verdict

The policy meets or exceeds the retake rigor of every professional credentialing body examined, while remaining less punitive (unlimited attempts, no multi-month wait periods). It threads the needle between accessibility and integrity. **Strongly supported by cross-profession precedent.**

## 4. Game Theory Perspective: Optimal Learner Strategy

### Modeling the Learner as a Rational Agent

Consider a learner whose goal is to obtain CE credit with minimum effort. Under the MedEdPrep retake policy, what is the optimal strategy?

**Variables:**
- *p* = probability of passing on any given attempt (function of actual knowledge level)
- *t_exam* = time to complete one assessment (~15-20 minutes for 30 questions)
- *t_content* = time to complete required educational content (~30-60 minutes)
- *k* = knowledge gained from content completion (increases *p* on subsequent attempts)

**Strategy A: Learn enough to pass first try**
- Expected time: Study time + *t_exam*
- Outcome: CE credit + genuine knowledge

**Strategy B: Attempt without preparation, hope to pass**
- If *p* < 0.70 (likely for underprepared learner), probability of passing ≈ low
- Expected time on first attempt: *t_exam* (fail) + *t_content* + *t_exam* (retry)
- Minimum: 60-100 minutes (vs. potentially less time studying first)

**Strategy C: Game the system through rapid retakes**
- **This strategy is blocked.** The content gate prevents rapid retry after failure.
- Even if attempted, different questions each time prevent answer memorization.
- The rational response is to actually learn the content during the forced review.

### Nash Equilibrium Analysis

In a game between the learner and the assessment system:

- **If the system allows immediate retakes with same questions:** The dominant strategy is "take, memorize wrong answers, retake." No genuine learning occurs. This is a bad equilibrium.
- **If the system allows immediate retakes with different questions:** The dominant strategy shifts toward "take, guess, retry" for risk-tolerant learners. Some learning occurs through exposure, but it is inefficient.
- **Under the MedEdPrep policy:** The dominant strategy is "prepare adequately OR, if failing, genuinely engage with content before retaking." The content gate converts the cost of failure from a trivial time cost (seconds to click "retake") into a meaningful time investment that happens to produce learning.

### Incentive Alignment

The key game-theoretic insight is that **the policy aligns the learner's self-interested incentive (minimize total time to credit) with the pedagogical goal (maximize learning)**. This is because:

1. The fastest path to credit is to pass on the first attempt (requires actual knowledge)
2. The second-fastest path is to fail, genuinely learn from the required content, and pass on the second attempt (also produces actual knowledge)
3. There is no shortcut path that avoids learning

This is a well-designed mechanism. In mechanism design theory, the gold standard is a system where truth-telling (genuine learning) is the dominant strategy. The MedEdPrep policy achieves this.

### What About Learners Who Already Know the Material?

A knowledgeable learner (high *p*) passes on the first attempt and is never subjected to the content gate. The policy imposes zero additional burden on competent practitioners. This is efficient --- the intervention is targeted only at those who demonstrate need.

### Verdict

The system's incentive structure makes genuine learning the dominant strategy for all learner types. There is no exploitable shortcut. The content gate is the mechanism that closes the game-theoretic loophole present in most CE retake policies. **Strongly supported by incentive analysis.**

## 5. Data Analysis: Item Exposure Probability

### The Question

With a 10,600+ item bank and random selection of 30 questions per attempt, what is the probability that a retaking learner sees the same item again?

### Mathematical Analysis

**Setup:**
- Total item bank: *N* = 10,600 items
- Items per assessment: *n* = 30
- Question: For a single item seen on attempt 1, what is *P*(seeing that same item on attempt 2)?

**Single-Item Overlap Probability:**

The probability that any specific item from attempt 1 appears on attempt 2, assuming simple random sampling without replacement within each attempt:

$$P(\text{specific item reappears}) = \frac{n}{N} = \frac{30}{10{,}600} = 0.00283$$

That is a **0.283% chance** for any single item.

**Expected Number of Overlapping Items:**

For 30 items seen on attempt 1, the expected number that reappear on attempt 2:

$$E[\text{overlap}] = n \times \frac{n}{N} = 30 \times \frac{30}{10{,}600} = \frac{900}{10{,}600} \approx 0.085$$

Expected overlap: **0.085 items** --- less than one-tenth of one question.

**Probability of Zero Overlap:**

Using the hypergeometric distribution, the probability of seeing *zero* repeated items:

$$P(\text{zero overlap}) = \frac{\binom{30}{0}\binom{10{,}570}{30}}{\binom{10{,}600}{30}}$$

This can be approximated (given the extreme ratio of *n* to *N*) as:

$$P(\text{zero overlap}) \approx \left(1 - \frac{30}{10{,}600}\right)^{30} \approx (0.99717)^{30} \approx 0.9183$$

**There is a 91.8% probability that a retake contains zero repeated items.**

**Probability of Seeing 1 or More Repeated Items:**

$$P(\text{at least 1 overlap}) \approx 1 - 0.9183 = 0.0817 \approx 8.2\%$$

**Probability of Seeing 2 or More Repeated Items:**

Using the Poisson approximation with lambda = 0.085:

$$P(k \geq 2) \approx 1 - e^{-0.085}(1 + 0.085) \approx 1 - 0.9184 \times 1.085 \approx 1 - 0.9965 \approx 0.35\%$$

### Summary Table

| Scenario | Probability |
|---|---|
| Zero items repeated on retake | 91.8% |
| Exactly 1 item repeated | ~7.8% |
| 2 or more items repeated | ~0.35% |
| 3 or more items repeated | ~0.01% |
| Expected number of repeats | 0.085 items |

### Practical Implications

- A learner who fails and retakes will, in over 9 out of 10 attempts, see an **entirely different set of questions**
- Even in the unlikely event of a single repeated item, that one item out of 30 provides negligible advantage (3.3% of the assessment)
- The probability of seeing enough repeated items to meaningfully affect the score is vanishingly small
- **Item memorization is not a viable strategy** under this bank size and selection method

### Comparison to Competitors

Most EMS CE providers use 10-15 questions drawn from a pool of 10-20 items (sometimes the same 10 questions every time). In that scenario:

$$P(\text{exact same test}) = 1.0 \text{ (100%)}$$

The contrast is not incremental --- it is categorical. MedEdPrep's item bank is approximately **700x larger** than a typical competitor's per-assessment item pool.

### Note on Topic-Level Stratification

If items are stratified by topic/learning objective (as they should be for content validity), the per-stratum overlap probability increases slightly because sampling occurs within topic pools rather than the full bank. However, even with 70 topics and ~150 items per topic, the per-stratum calculation yields:

$$E[\text{overlap per stratum}] = \frac{(30/70)^2}{150/70} \approx \frac{0.184}{2.14} \approx 0.086$$

The effect of stratification is negligible at this bank size.

### Verdict

The 10,600-item bank makes item memorization statistically futile. A retaking learner has a 91.8% chance of seeing zero repeated items. Even in topic-stratified sampling, overlap is negligible. **The item bank provides overwhelming exposure control.** This is not a marginal advantage --- it is a structural one that no competitor can replicate without years of item development.

## 6. Fairness Considerations

### The Equity Question

Is it equitable that some learners must complete additional educational content (because they scored below 70%) while others pass on the first attempt and do not?

### Analysis Through Multiple Fairness Frameworks

**Formal Equality (treat everyone the same):**
Under strict formal equality, the policy is "unequal" --- different learners face different requirements. However, formal equality is the weakest fairness standard and is rejected by virtually all educational equity frameworks because treating unequal situations equally perpetuates inequality.

**Substantive Equality (equal outcomes through differentiated support):**
The policy is strongly equitable under substantive equality. Learners who demonstrate knowledge gaps receive *additional support* (educational content) to help them reach competency. This is equivalent to the accommodation principle in education: providing more resources to those who need them.

**Rawlsian Fairness (greatest benefit to the least advantaged):**
Under Rawls' difference principle, inequalities are justified only if they benefit the least-advantaged group. The additional content requirement benefits the failing learner by:
1. Providing targeted education in their specific gap areas
2. Increasing their probability of passing on the next attempt
3. Ensuring their eventual CE credit represents genuine learning

The least-advantaged learner (the one who fails) receives *more* education, not less. This is Rawlsian-fair.

**Procedural Fairness (fair process):**
- All learners face the same assessment
- The 70% threshold is applied uniformly
- Question selection is randomized (no learner gets systematically harder items)
- The content gate applies equally to all who score below the threshold
- No demographic, institutional, or financial factors influence who is gated

The process is procedurally fair.

### The Real Inequity: Systems Without Content Gates

The deeper fairness problem exists in systems *without* remediation gates. When any learner can pass through unlimited immediate retakes on the same questions, two inequities emerge:

1. **Learners who know the material subsidize those who don't.** Both receive the same credential, but only one actually earned it through knowledge. This devalues the credential for the competent practitioner.
2. **Struggling learners are denied the help they need.** A system that lets you fail and immediately retry without support is not "fair" to the failing learner --- it is indifferent to them. It offers neither diagnosis nor remedy.

### Accommodation Considerations

The policy should be evaluated for potential disparate impact on:
- **Non-native English speakers:** May fail due to language rather than knowledge. Mitigation: item review for linguistic accessibility, possible accommodations.
- **Test-anxious learners:** May underperform on first attempt. Mitigation: the unlimited retakes provision addresses this directly --- test anxiety diminishes with familiarity.
- **Time-constrained practitioners:** The content requirement adds time. Mitigation: content is targeted and relatively brief (not a full course); the alternative would be repeated failed attempts that consume equal or more total time.

None of these considerations argue against the policy. They argue for thoughtful implementation *of* the policy.

### Verdict

The policy is equitable under substantive equality, Rawlsian fairness, and procedural fairness frameworks. The content gate benefits the learners it is imposed upon. The real inequity would be allowing failing learners to brute-force their way to a credential without learning. **Strongly supported on fairness grounds.**

## 7. Competency-Based Education (CBE) Comparison

### CBE Principles

Competency-based education, as defined by the Competency-Based Education Network (C-BEN) and implemented in institutions like Western Governors University (WGU), Southern New Hampshire University (SNHU), and increasingly in health professions education, rests on several principles:

1. **Learners advance upon demonstrated mastery**, not seat time
2. **Competencies are explicitly defined** and measurable
3. **Assessment is meaningful** and aligned to competencies
4. **Learners receive differentiated support** based on their needs
5. **Learning happens at variable pace** (time is the variable, competency is the constant)

### Alignment Analysis

| CBE Principle | MedEdPrep Policy | Traditional CE |
|---|---|---|
| Advance on mastery | Pass at 70% with validated assessment | Watch video, pass trivial quiz |
| Explicit competencies | CAPCE learning objectives per activity | Often vaguely defined |
| Meaningful assessment | 30 randomized items from calibrated bank | 10 static items, same every time |
| Differentiated support | **Content gate for those who fail** | Same (nothing) for everyone |
| Variable pace | Pass fast if competent; slower path if gaps exist | Fixed seat time for all |

The MedEdPrep policy is a **near-complete implementation of CBE** for continuing education --- a context where CBE is rarely attempted.

### The "Time as Variable" Insight

In traditional CE, time is the constant (everyone completes the same 1-hour course) and competency is the variable (some learn, some don't). The MedEdPrep model inverts this:

- **Competent practitioners:** Take assessment, pass, earn credit. Time investment: ~20 minutes.
- **Practitioners with gaps:** Take assessment, fail, complete content, retake, pass. Time investment: ~60-90 minutes.

Competency is the constant (everyone must demonstrate 70% mastery on a valid assessment). Time varies based on need. This is the definition of CBE.

### Comparison to Health Professions CBE Initiatives

The Association of American Medical Colleges (AAMC) and the Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education (ACGME) have been moving toward CBE in medical education through "Entrustable Professional Activities" (EPAs) and "Milestones." The American Association of Colleges of Pharmacy (AACP) has implemented CBE in pharmacy education. In all cases, the core mechanic is:

1. Assess competency
2. Identify gaps
3. Provide targeted remediation for gaps
4. Reassess

This is exactly the MedEdPrep retake loop.

### Verdict

The retake policy is a textbook CBE implementation. It is more aligned with CBE principles than virtually any other CE product in the EMS market, which remains mired in seat-time models. **Strongly supported by CBE frameworks.**

## 8. Cost-Benefit Analysis for the Provider

### Benefits of Unlimited Retakes with Content Gate

**Revenue and Engagement:**
- Each retake attempt = additional platform engagement (more time on platform)
- Content completion requirement = additional content consumption (which can be tracked for analytics, reported to employers/agencies, and monetized in B2B contexts)
- Unlimited retakes reduce refund requests and support tickets ("I failed and want my money back")
- Learners who fail and succeed on retry become **stronger brand advocates** than those who pass trivially on the first attempt (the effort justification effect from cognitive dissonance research)

**Data and Analytics:**
- Each attempt generates item response data that feeds IRT calibration
- Failure patterns identify weak topics across the learner population (valuable B2B intelligence)
- Multi-attempt data enables growth measurement (pre/post content consumption performance delta)
- The content gate creates a natural A/B experiment: performance before content vs. after content

**Competitive Differentiation:**
- No EMS CE competitor offers this model
- The policy is a tangible marketing differentiator ("We don't just test you --- we teach you")
- It demonstrates a genuine commitment to competency that employers and medical directors value

### Costs of Unlimited Retakes

**Server and Infrastructure:**
- Each retake consumes compute: item selection, response scoring, diagnostic generation
- Estimated marginal cost per attempt: negligible (sub-$0.01 for item delivery; potentially $0.01-0.05 if AI-generated diagnostics are used)
- At scale (10,000 learners, 1.3 average attempts each), annual additional compute cost: ~$50-200
- **This is a rounding error.** Server costs are not a meaningful concern.

**Item Exposure:**
- Each attempt exposes 30 items. With 10,600 items and ~1.3 average attempts per learner, a cohort of 10,000 learners would see approximately 390,000 item exposures total.
- Each item would be exposed ~37 times on average across the cohort --- well within acceptable exposure limits.
- At 100,000 learners (ambitious Year 2 target), each item sees ~370 exposures. Still manageable.
- The bank is large enough to support exposure control for years before item refresh is needed.

**Content Creation:**
- The educational content required for the remediation gate must exist. This is a real cost.
- However, this content serves dual purpose: it is both the remediation intervention AND available as enrichment for passing learners.
- In the assessment-only track, rationale review IS the content. No additional creation needed.
- In the content+assessment track, the content already exists as the primary educational material.

### Net Cost-Benefit

| Factor | Impact | Magnitude |
|---|---|---|
| Server cost of retakes | Negative | Negligible (~$200/yr at scale) |
| Item exposure risk | Negative | Negligible (bank is 350x larger than needed) |
| Content creation for gate | Negative | Zero marginal (content exists for other purposes) |
| Additional engagement data | Positive | High (feeds IRT, B2B analytics) |
| Reduced refund/support | Positive | Moderate |
| Brand differentiation | Positive | High |
| Learner advocacy effect | Positive | Moderate |

**The policy is net positive for the provider across every time horizon.** The costs are structurally negligible because the item bank already exists and compute is cheap. The benefits are substantial and compound over time as data accumulates.

### Verdict

Unlimited retakes with a content gate are commercially advantageous, not merely pedagogically justified. The cost objections do not survive quantification. **Strongly supported on cost-benefit grounds.**

| Provider Cost-Benefit | Strong | Net positive; costs are negligible, benefits compound |

### What Could Weaken This Policy?

For intellectual honesty, the conditions under which this policy would become problematic:

1. **Tiny item bank:** If the bank were 50-100 items instead of 10,600, item overlap would undermine the "different questions" element. This is not the case.
2. **Poor content quality:** If the required educational content is low-quality filler, the gate becomes punitive without being instructive. Mitigation: the content is rationale-based and substantive.
3. **Excessively long content requirement:** If completion takes 4+ hours, the gate becomes a barrier rather than an intervention. Mitigation: targeted content of 30-60 minutes is proportionate.
4. **No diagnostic feedback:** If learners are told only "you failed" without knowing which areas to focus on, the content gate is less targeted. Mitigation: the diagnostic report addresses this directly.

None of these risk conditions apply to MedEdPrep's implementation.

### Final Judgment

**This independent analysis fully supports the retake policy.** The combination of unlimited retakes + randomized questions + content gate after failure is not merely defensible --- it is the optimal design for a CE assessment system. It is supported by six decades of learning science (Bloom 1968, Zimmerman 2000, Bjork & Bjork 2011), aligns with current credentialing best practices, passes game-theoretic analysis, is statistically robust against item exposure, satisfies multiple fairness frameworks, exemplifies competency-based education, and is commercially sustainable.

The single strongest element is the forced-remediation content gate. This is the mechanism that converts a compliance exercise into a learning system. Without it, the policy would be good. With it, the policy is exemplary.
