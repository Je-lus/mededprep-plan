## Executive Summary

A 70% passing score is the appropriate threshold for MedEdPrep's CAPCE-accredited continuing education assessments. This conclusion is supported by five converging lines of evidence:

1. **Industry consensus** — 70% is the de facto standard across virtually all CAPCE-accredited CE providers
2. **CAPCE compliance** — CAPCE requires summative assessment but does not mandate a specific passing percentage, giving accredited organizations discretion; 70% is the prevailing norm
3. **Psychometric validity** — 70% produces meaningful score variance that distinguishes competent from non-competent performance on well-constructed items
4. **Cross-profession alignment** — Pharmacy (ACPE), nursing (ANCC), and medicine (ACCME) continuing education programs converge on 70-80% passing thresholds
5. **Compatibility with unlimited retakes** — 70% with item bank rotation preserves assessment integrity in ways that 100%-with-unlimited-retakes cannot

This document presents the evidence for each of these conclusions.

## 1. CAPCE Standards and Requirements

### What CAPCE Requires

The Commission on Accreditation for Pre-Hospital Continuing Education (CAPCE) is the recognized accrediting body for EMS continuing education in the United States. CAPCE accreditation standards require:

- **Summative assessment** for all CE activities that award credit — learners must demonstrate knowledge acquisition
- **Assessment items must map to stated learning objectives** — every test question must connect to a defined objective
- **Item banking is permitted** for adaptive assessments, provided all items map to the same learning objectives and difficulty is comparable across item sets
- **Assessment-only CE is allowed** — no video or lecture content is required; the assessment itself (with rationales) constitutes the educational activity, using CAPCE's character count method (~7,500 characters = 0.5 CEH)

### What CAPCE Does Not Mandate

CAPCE does **not** prescribe a specific minimum passing percentage in its accreditation standards. The passing threshold is left to the discretion of the accredited organization, which is responsible for establishing and justifying its own quality standards as part of the organizational accreditation process.

This is a deliberate design choice by CAPCE. As an accrediting body, CAPCE sets process standards (objectives must exist, assessment must occur, items must align to objectives) rather than prescribing specific psychometric parameters. This parallels how regional academic accreditors operate — they require institutions to have assessment processes without dictating specific grade thresholds.

### Why 70% Is the Universal Default

Despite CAPCE not mandating a specific percentage, 70% has emerged as the overwhelming industry consensus through a combination of:

- **Historical convention** — 70% has been the traditional passing threshold in American education since the mid-20th century, rooted in the A-F grading scale where 70% marks the boundary between "passing" (C-) and "failing" (D+)
- **Cross-profession alignment** — healthcare CE accreditors across disciplines cluster around 70-80%, creating a professional norm
- **Regulatory expectation** — state EMS offices and NREMT accept 70% as a reasonable standard; deviating significantly in either direction would invite scrutiny
- **Practical equilibrium** — 70% produces pass rates in the 75-90% range on well-constructed assessments, which is the appropriate range for CE (high enough to validate that learning occurred, low enough to be meaningful)

### Pattern: Accreditors Set Process Standards, Not Score Thresholds

A striking pattern emerges across all major healthcare CE accrediting bodies: **none of them mandate a specific passing percentage**. Every accreditor requires that summative assessment occur and that it be linked to learning objectives, but all leave the specific threshold to the accredited provider.

This is not an oversight. It reflects a shared understanding that:

1. The appropriate passing score depends on item difficulty, item quality, and the stakes of the assessment
2. Accredited organizations are responsible for their own quality standards
3. A mandated percentage without corresponding item quality standards would be meaningless — a 70% threshold on trivially easy questions provides less assurance than a 60% threshold on appropriately challenging items

Major CME providers (e.g., UpToDate, Medscape Education, Pri-Med) use 70%.

## 4. Psychometric Rationale for 70%

### Cut-Score Theory in Continuing Education

In formal psychometric practice, passing scores (cut scores) are established through structured standard-setting procedures such as the Angoff method, the Bookmark method, or the Ebel method. These methods convene subject matter expert panels to establish the minimum level of knowledge a "borderline competent" practitioner should demonstrate.

For **high-stakes certification exams** (e.g., NREMT, NCLEX, board exams), standard-setting studies are essential and legally defensible. The resulting cut scores vary by exam and are often expressed as scaled scores rather than raw percentages.

For **continuing education assessments**, the stakes are different. CE assessments serve an educational verification function — they confirm that the learner engaged with and retained the material. They are not making licensure decisions. In this context, a formal standard-setting study for every CE activity would be disproportionate to the purpose of the assessment.

Instead, CE providers rely on the 70% convention, which functions as a **reasonable proxy** for the borderline competent practitioner threshold. This is defensible because:

1. **CE items are purpose-built** — they assess specific learning objectives from the CE activity, not broad clinical competence
2. **Item difficulty is calibrated to the content** — well-constructed CE items should be answerable by someone who engaged with the material
3. **70% is the equilibrium point** — it is high enough to distinguish engaged learners from random guessing (25% on 4-option multiple choice) while being achievable for learners who made a genuine effort

### Why Not 80%?

An 80% threshold would:

- **Increase false negatives** — competent practitioners who made 1-2 careless errors on a 10-question test would fail (missing 3 of 10 = 70%, passing; but failing at 80%)
- **Penalize shorter assessments disproportionately** — on a 10-question test, the difference between 70% and 80% is a single question; on a 20-question test, it is two questions
- **Exceed cross-profession norms** — most CE providers use 70%; setting a higher bar without justification creates unnecessary friction
- **Interact poorly with item difficulty** — if items are appropriately challenging (average difficulty p = 0.65-0.75), an 80% threshold would produce unacceptably high failure rates

An 80% threshold is appropriate when: (a) the assessment is longer (25+ items), (b) items are intentionally easier (average p > 0.80), or (c) the stakes justify higher stringency (e.g., AHA certification courses). None of these conditions apply to standard CE post-tests.

### Why Not 60%?

A 60% threshold would:

- **Approach chance level** — on 4-option multiple choice, random guessing yields 25%; 60% is only 35 percentage points above chance, providing weak evidence of learning
- **Fall below professional norms** — no CAPCE provider uses 60%; adopting it would invite questions about rigor
- **Undermine MedEdPrep's competency positioning** — a platform positioning itself as "continued competency validation" cannot credibly use a lower standard than every competitor

### Score Variance and Measurement Precision

A critical psychometric consideration is that the passing threshold must produce meaningful **score variance** in the population of test-takers. If virtually everyone passes (threshold too low) or virtually everyone fails (threshold too high), the assessment provides no useful information about individual differences in competency.

At 70% on well-constructed items (average difficulty p = 0.65-0.75):

- Expected pass rate: **75-90%** — appropriate for CE (most practitioners should pass)
- Expected score distribution: meaningful spread from ~50% to ~100%
- Discriminating power: the assessment can distinguish between practitioners who deeply know the material (~90-100%), those who have adequate knowledge (~70-85%), and those who need further study (<70%)

This score variance is what enables MedEdPrep's diagnostic reporting to be meaningful. Without variance, there is nothing to report.

## 5. Why 100% With Unlimited Retakes Is Psychometrically Meaningless

### The Mastery vs. Memorization Problem

Some CE platforms use a model where learners must answer 100% of questions correctly, but can retake the assessment unlimited times with the same questions. This creates a fundamental validity problem.

**What 100%/unlimited-retakes actually measures:**

- **First attempt:** Genuine knowledge assessment
- **Second attempt:** Mix of knowledge + memory of previous questions
- **Third+ attempts:** Almost entirely recognition memory — the learner remembers which answers were marked wrong and selects different options

By the third or fourth attempt, the learner is not demonstrating clinical knowledge. They are demonstrating that they can remember which of four options was marked correct on a previous attempt. This is **recognition memory**, the lowest form of cognitive processing — far below the comprehension, application, and analysis that CE is supposed to assess.

### The Mathematical Inevitability

Consider a 10-question, 4-option multiple choice test at 100% passing:

- **Attempt 1:** Learner gets 7/10 correct (70%). Fails.
- **Attempt 2:** Learner remembers 3 wrong answers, guesses correctly on 2. Gets 9/10 (90%). Fails.
- **Attempt 3:** Learner remembers the 1 remaining wrong answer. Gets 10/10 (100%). Passes.

The learner achieved "100%" while potentially understanding only 70% of the material. The additional 30% was acquired through trial-and-error memorization, not learning.

**With the same questions on each attempt, 100% passing is mathematically guaranteed** for any learner willing to retry. The only variable is how many attempts it takes. The threshold provides zero additional information beyond the first attempt.

### What the Testing Effect Literature Actually Says

Proponents of 100%/unlimited-retakes often cite the "testing effect" (retrieval practice enhances long-term retention). This literature is robust — repeated testing does improve retention. However, the testing effect research assumes:

1. **Novel retrieval attempts** — the learner is reconstructing the answer from memory, not recognizing a previously seen correct answer
2. **Spaced practice** — retrieval attempts are separated by time intervals
3. **Feedback that promotes understanding** — not just "correct/incorrect" signals

When a learner retakes the same 10 questions immediately after seeing the correct answers, conditions (1) and (3) are violated. The learner is not retrieving — they are recognizing. And they are not building understanding — they are matching patterns.

### MedEdPrep's Superior Alternative: 70% + Item Bank Rotation

MedEdPrep's approach resolves this problem entirely:

- **70% passing threshold** — a meaningful, first-attempt measure of competency
- **Unlimited retakes with different questions** — from a 10,600+ item bank mapped to the same learning objectives
- **Forced rationale reading on wrong answers** — immediate corrective feedback at the moment of error
- **Follow-up verification question** — a similar (not identical) question to confirm learning occurred
- **Fail <70% triggers remediation** — must complete Pathway 2 content resources before retaking

This model preserves the benefits of the testing effect (retrieval practice, spaced repetition, corrective feedback) while eliminating the memorization loophole. Each retake is a **genuine assessment** because the learner encounters novel items targeting the same objectives.

### Comparison Table

| Feature | 100% Same Questions | 70% + Item Rotation |
|---------|:------------------:|:-------------------:|
| Measures clinical knowledge | First attempt only | Every attempt |
| Vulnerable to memorization | Yes (guaranteed pass by ~3rd attempt) | No (new items each time) |

## 6. Item Bank Difficulty Implications

### If the Passing Score Is 70%, Items Need Appropriate Difficulty

The passing threshold and item difficulty are interdependent. A 70% passing score is only meaningful if the items are neither trivially easy nor impossibly hard.

### Target Difficulty Distribution

For a CE assessment with a 70% passing threshold, the optimal item difficulty distribution is:

| Difficulty Band | p-value Range | Target % of Items | Purpose |
|----------------|:------------:|:-----------------:|---------|
| Easy | 0.80-0.95 | 15-20% | Confidence building, baseline knowledge confirmation |
| Medium-Easy | 0.65-0.79 | 25-30% | Core competency verification |
| Medium | 0.50-0.64 | 25-30% | Discrimination — separates strong from adequate |
| Medium-Hard | 0.35-0.49 | 15-20% | Identifies knowledge gaps, drives diagnostic reporting |
| Hard | 0.15-0.34 | 5-10% | Challenges advanced practitioners, anchors the high end |

This distribution produces:

- **Expected mean score:** ~65-72% — centering the distribution near the passing threshold, which maximizes measurement precision at the decision point
- **Expected pass rate:** ~75-85% — appropriate for CE
- **Meaningful score variance:** Standard deviation of ~12-18%, enabling diagnostic differentiation

### MedEdPrep's Current Item Bank Status

From the 2026-04-06 question bank analysis (10,629 items, 1,550,942 responses):

- **Adaptive pool:** Best difficulty distribution — 16.4% hard/very-hard items, good spread across bands
- **Standard pool:** Moderate skew toward easy — 65% of items have p > 0.60
- **Unit exam pool:** Significant easy skew — 55% of items have p > 0.80, only 13% in the diagnostically rich 0.41-0.60 band

**For CE assessment-only activities (Pathway 1)**, items should be drawn from the adaptive pool or from curated subsets of the standard pool that match the target difficulty distribution. The unit exam pool's easy skew would produce inflated scores that undermine the 70% threshold's discriminating power.

### Item Difficulty and the 70% Threshold Interaction

| Scenario | Mean Item Difficulty | Expected Pass Rate | Diagnostic Value |
|----------|:-------------------:|:-----------------:|:----------------:|
| Items too easy (p = 0.85 avg) | High | ~95%+ | Minimal — everyone passes easily |
| Items appropriately calibrated (p = 0.65 avg) | Target | ~78-85% | High — meaningful score spread |
| Items too hard (p = 0.45 avg) | Low | ~30-40% | Low — excessive failure discourages learners |

The 70% threshold only works as intended when items are calibrated to produce mean scores in the 65-75% range. MedEdPrep's IRT calibration data (1.55M responses) provides the empirical foundation to achieve this calibration — a significant advantage over competitors who set items without psychometric data.

### Ongoing Calibration Requirement

Item difficulty is not static. As items are reused across learner populations, difficulty statistics should be:

1. **Monitored continuously** — flag items that drift outside target bands (e.g., items that were p = 0.65 at calibration but drop to p = 0.40 in CE populations)
2. **Recalibrated at thresholds** — recalculate difficulty parameters after every 500 CE-specific responses (per the IRT recalibration trigger defined in the CE strategy)
3. **Retired or revised when needed** — items with discrimination indices below 0.15 provide no useful measurement information regardless of difficulty

## 7. Interaction With Unlimited Retakes and Item Rotation

### MedEdPrep's Retake Model

MedEdPrep allows unlimited retakes across all three CE pathways, with the following critical design features:

1. **Different questions on each attempt** — drawn from the 10,600+ item bank, mapped to the same learning objectives
2. **Comparable difficulty across attempts** — IRT calibration ensures that each form is psychometrically equivalent
3. **Forced remediation on failure** — scoring <70% on a Pathway 1 assessment requires completing Pathway 2 content before retaking
4. **Forced rationale reading** — wrong answers trigger immediate display of the full rationale, which must be read before continuing
5. **Follow-up verification questions** — after an incorrect answer, a similar (not identical) question tests whether the learner absorbed the rationale

### Why This Preserves Assessment Integrity

**Item rotation eliminates the memorization path.** A learner who fails at 62% cannot simply memorize the 4 questions they missed and retake. They will encounter 4 different questions on the same objectives — questions they cannot predict or memorize in advance.

**Forced remediation ensures the retake is a genuine reassessment.** By requiring Pathway 2 completion (video or reading content) before a retake, MedEdPrep ensures that the learner has engaged with educational material between attempts. This transforms the retake from a trial-and-error loop into a genuine learn-then-reassess cycle.

**The item bank is large enough to sustain rotation.** With 10,600+ items across the full bank, even topics with hundreds of mapped items can support 5-10+ unique assessment forms per topic. This means a learner could fail and retake multiple times without ever seeing the same question — an impossibility for providers with small, static question sets.

### Retake Score Data Is Diagnostically Valuable

Unlike 100%/same-questions models where retake data is meaningless (scores converge to 100%), MedEdPrep's retake data is genuinely informative:

- **Persistent low scores across retakes** (e.g., 55%, 60%, 58%) on the same objectives — despite content remediation — signal a genuine knowledge gap, not a memorization failure
- **Score improvement after remediation** (e.g., 55% → 78%) validates that the content pathway was effective
- **Per-objective retake patterns** reveal which specific objectives are most challenging for each learner, enabling targeted diagnostic reports

This data feeds directly into MedEdPrep's four-standard diagnostic reporting (NES, NSOP, NMG, NCCP), which is the platform's core differentiator. None of this is possible with 100%/same-questions models because all learners converge to the same score (100%), erasing the variance that makes diagnostics meaningful.

A 70% passing score on CAPCE-accredited activities is fully accepted for NREMT recertification.

State EMS offices accept CAPCE-accredited CE for state recertification. No state imposes a different passing score requirement on CAPCE-accredited activities. Georgia (MedEdPrep's initial market) accepts CAPCE CE without modification.

B2B agency customers (fire departments, ambulance services, hospital-based EMS) expect CE that meets CAPCE standards. A 70% passing score is the expected norm — it is what these agencies see from every other CE provider. Setting a significantly different threshold would create confusion and friction in the B2B sales process.

NREMT has been signaling a move toward competency-based recertification models. MedEdPrep's 70% threshold with diagnostic reporting positions the platform to support this transition:

- 70% establishes a minimum competency floor
- Diagnostic reports identify specific competency gaps
- Per-objective scoring maps to NCCP domains and NES standards
- Retake data tracks competency development over time

If NREMT eventually requires evidence of competency (not just CE hours), MedEdPrep's data model — built on meaningful assessment scores, not inflated 100% pass rates — will be the only platform capable of providing it.

## 9. Recommendation

### Confirmed: 70% Is the Appropriate Passing Score

Based on the evidence presented in this document, **70% is confirmed as the passing threshold for all MedEdPrep CE assessments** across all three pathways:

- **Pathway 1 (Adaptive Competency Exam):** 70% on 15-45 questions (0.5 or 1.0 CEH)
- **Pathway 2 (CE Content + Post-Test):** 70% on 15-question post-test
- **Pathway 3 (Combined):** 70% on each pathway's assessment independently

### Implementation Parameters

| Parameter | Value | Rationale |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| **Passing threshold** | 70% | Industry standard, cross-profession consensus |
| **Retake policy** | Unlimited | Educational purpose; new items each attempt |
| **Retake prerequisite (Pathway 1 fail)** | Must complete Pathway 2 | Forced remediation before reassessment |
| **Item difficulty target** | p = 0.60-0.75 mean | Centers score distribution near cut point |
| **Minimum assessment length** | 15 questions (0.5 CEH) | Sufficient reliability for CE purposes |
| **Maximum assessment length** | 45 questions (1.0 CEH) | Comprehensive domain coverage |
| **Wrong-answer intervention** | Forced rationale + verification question | Immediate corrective feedback (formative, not scored) |

### What This Means for Item Development

- Items must be written to achieve mean difficulty in the p = 0.60-0.75 range
- Items should be pilot-tested with initial cohorts and difficulty-adjusted before going into full rotation
- IRT parameters from the existing 1.55M-response dataset provide a calibration baseline
- The first 500 CE-specific responses per topic should trigger IRT recalibration to establish CE-population norms (CE learners may differ from initial-education learners in the existing database)

### Competitive Advantage

By combining 70% with item bank rotation, forced remediation, and diagnostic reporting, MedEdPrep achieves something no competitor in the EMS CE market currently offers: **a CE assessment system that is simultaneously learner-friendly (unlimited retakes, immediate feedback) and psychometrically rigorous (meaningful scores, genuine measurement, diagnostic value)**. The 70% threshold is not a compromise — it is the keystone that makes the entire measurement architecture work.

## Appendix A: Standard-Setting Methods (Reference)

For future consideration — if MedEdPrep pursues formal standard-setting for specific high-stakes CE assessments (e.g., agency competency validation programs), the following methods are available:

| Method | Description | Appropriate When |
|--------|-------------|------------------|
| **Modified Angoff** | SME panel estimates probability that a borderline-competent practitioner would answer each item correctly; cut score = mean of estimates | Established item bank with known parameters |
| **Bookmark** | SME panel reviews items ordered by difficulty; places a "bookmark" at the point where a borderline practitioner would begin to struggle | IRT-calibrated items available |
| **Ebel** | SME panel categorizes items by difficulty and relevance; cut score derived from category weights | When items vary in relevance to practice |
| **Contrasting Groups** | Empirical method — compare score distributions of known-competent vs. known-not-competent groups | When criterion groups can be identified |

These methods are not necessary for standard CE post-tests (where 70% is sufficient by convention) but become important if MedEdPrep expands into agency competency validation or certificate programs where the stakes and legal defensibility requirements are higher.
