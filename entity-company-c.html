🎯 The Certification ThesisOwn the credential, not just the prep ▾
The Strategic Position
Company A (MedEdPrep) prepares candidates to pass someone else’s exam. Company C issues the credential itself — a structurally separate Georgia LLC with its own brand, independent governance board, and a question database that never touches the education side. NCCA explicitly permits for-profit certification bodies; the constraint is not corporate form, it is the firewall between education and certification. That firewall is exactly why A and C must be separate entities.
Why a Separate Entity
  • NCCA & ISO/IEC 17024 require structural separation between certification and any affiliated education/training entity
  • Prevents the “diploma mill” conflict — the body that teaches cannot be the body that judges
  • Independent oversight board with a public member — a 501(c)(3)-style governance requirement
  • Separate brand needed for NCCA credibility and market positioning distinct from MedEdPrep
The Differentiators
  • Bifactor dual-validation — pass/fail + diagnostic from the same items
  • Unified Paramedic Practitioner capstone — nobody offers it; IBSC does narrow specialties only
  • Recert-by-exam calibration — CE volume that pretests items at industrial scale
  • Psychometric rigor built on infrastructure NREMT removed in its 2025 CAT redesign
⚠ Future State — Not Yet Formed
Company C does not exist yet. It is a Year 2–4 build. The sequence is deliberate: stand up a defensible program with full psychometric rigor → launch and collect operational data → then apply for NCCA accreditation. You cannot accredit a proposal — NCCA only accredits programs that are already issuing certifications with at least one administration cycle of statistical data.

Where the Paramedic Practitioner Fits
The advanced-practice space already exists — and that is validating, not threatening. IBSC (formerly BCCTPC) certifies narrow specialties: FP-C (flight), CCP-C (critical care), CP-C (community), WP-C (wilderness), TP-C (tactical). GEMR offers a broad 51-function APP credential but has limited US state recognition. Kentucky (202 KAR 7:410) is the first state to codify “Advanced Practice Paramedic” in regulation, using IBSC specialty certs as the pathway. The gap: no one offers a unified, NCCA-accredited, US-focused capstone that sits above the IBSC specialties. The PP credential complements them — require an IBSC specialty as a prerequisite, then layer advanced practice on top. Note: I-CAPP, the professional body for advanced practice paramedics, is based in Suwanee, Georgia — the same state, a potential ally rather than a competitor.
Correction on the Record
IBSC is NCCA-accredited (since 2018) — its CP-C, CCP-C, FP-C, and TP-C exams all carry accreditation. The competitive differentiator for Company C is therefore not accreditation status. It is (1) a unified advanced-practice credential that no one offers, and (2) diagnostic feedback that IBSC does not provide.
🧬 Bifactor Dual-ValidationOne item set → pass/fail + diagnostic ▾
The Core Innovation
A traditional certification exam produces a single number: pass or fail. A bifactor model fits a general competence factor (the dimension the pass/fail cut score is built on) plus orthogonal specific factors (airway, cardiology, trauma, medical, operations) from the same response data. The candidate gets a defensible go/no-go decision; the candidate, employer, and education partner get a diagnostic competency profile — for free, from items they already answered. This is the psychometric architecture that NREMT removed when it moved to its 2025 CAT format.
The Pass/Fail Decision
Built on the general factor with a defensible cut score set via a documented method — Modified Angoff or Bookmark — tied to minimally competent practice from the Job Task Analysis. This is what survives a state board’s prescriptive audit (e.g., Florida Rule 64B-1.011: scores must be “statistically reliable,” passing score set by “psychometrically sound methods,” reliability target Cronbach’s α ≥ 0.80).
The Diagnostic Profile
The specific factors give per-domain sub-scores: strong on cardiology, weak on operations. This feeds the remediation loop — Company C reports the diagnostic, Company A (education) sells the targeted prep, and Company E (data) studies the competency gap at population level. The same exam that gates the candidate also maps exactly what to study next.
Why It Matters for the Portfolio
Diagnostic feedback is something IBSC, GEMR, and the NREMT do not provide. It converts a one-time pass/fail event into an ongoing relationship — and it is the connective tissue between the three operating companies (C judges, A teaches, E measures) without ever sharing individual-level data across the certification/education firewall.
🔒 Separate & Secured Question DatabaseItem-bank independence is structural, not cosmetic ▾
⚠ The Firewall Is the Product
Company C runs a separate question database — independent from MedEdPrep’s 10,083+ education items. This is not a preference; it is the NCCA/ISO 17024 separation requirement made physical. Certification items must never appear in education products, or the credential is compromised. An NCCA reviewer will scrutinize the founder’s personal access precisely because the founder owns both entities.
Gold-Standard Technical Separation
  • Separate AWS account for Company C infrastructure — separate VPC, no peering to Company A’s network
  • Separate CI/CD pipeline and separate KMS/HSM key management for the certification database
  • Encryption at rest (AES-256) and in transit (TLS 1.2+); encrypted backups stored separately from Company A’s
  • SOC 2 Type II audit of Company C infrastructure; penetration testing of the separation boundary
  • Automated alerts if any cross-account access is attempted

Proving It at Audit
The clean approach is not to pretend the founder cannot see the items — it is to prove items are developed, reviewed, and approved through an independent process, governed by Company C’s board, with architecture diagrams, access logs, signed NDAs, annual conflict-of-interest declarations, and an incident-response plan on file.
🏆 The NCCA Accreditation PathFor-profit allowed · launch first, accredit second ▾
What NCCA Is — and Why For-Profit Is Fine
The National Commission for Certifying Agencies (NCCA), the accreditation arm of the Institute for Credentialing Excellence (ICE), is the domestic US standard that validates a certification program’s psychometric rigor, governance, and public-protection mission — the credential state licensing boards look for. NCCA accredits the program, not the organization, and does not require nonprofit status. The gating constraints are the 21 standards: independent governance with a public member, a JTA-based blueprint, psychometrically sound standard setting, audited financials, and a firewall between certification and education.
1
Build — Year 1
Job Task Analysis, exam blueprint, item development, governance setup. The JTA defines “minimally competent” practice and weights the content domains.
2
Launch & Collect — Year 2
Pilot testing, standard setting (Angoff/Bookmark), first operational exam, and at least one administration cycle of statistical data. The EMT credential is the first launch target.
3
Apply & Accredit — Year 3
Submit through the ICE portal (windows open three times/year). Review takes 4–5 months; on approval the program enters a 5-year renewal cycle. Then approach state boards with the accredited credential.

⚡ “Recert by Exam” — the Item-Calibration EngineCE revenue that pretests items at scale ▾
A Dual-Purpose Product
CAPCE-accredited continuing-education exams can be two things at once: (1) a legitimate, revenue-generating CE offering EMS clinicians use toward NREMT recertification, and (2) a high-volume pretesting pipeline that calibrates items destined for Company C’s certification exams. The NREMT requires 16–60 CE hours per 2-year cycle across 620,000+ nationally certified clinicians — an enormous, recurring, self-funding source of item-response data.
Step 1
Author CE module
60-question CAPCE F3 (distributive) module with topic intro + detailed rationales after each item.
Step 2
Clinicians take it
Thousands of already-certified practitioners answer for CE credit toward their NREMT cycle.
Step 3
Calibrate items
Response data yields IRT difficulty/discrimination parameters at industrial volume.
Step 4
Promote to cert bank
Calibrated, vetted items graduate into Company C’s operational certification exams.

Why It Works Operationally
CAPCE’s F3 distributive format is explicitly built for online, exam-based activities. Since October 2024 a direct CAPCE–NREMT integration auto-imports approved course records into the clinician’s NREMT account with automatic NCCP-domain assignment — a real UX edge. The 2022 distributive-education waiver (now permanent) removed the cap on online CE.
The Structural Complexity
Three open questions: (1) can Company C hold both NCCA certification accreditation and CAPCE CE-provider accreditation without violating either firewall; (2) are IRT estimates from a CE population (already-certified) linkable to certification candidates (new students); (3) will NCCA accept items pretested outside the certification exam. Feasible, but the linking design must be deliberate. Target organizational CAPCE accreditation (approve your own activities), not course-by-course. Contact: Jay Scott, CAPCE Executive Director.

⚠ The A↔C Data Firewall
Company A licenses student-performance data to Company E; Company C licenses certification data to E at aggregate level only. Critically, E may not share individual-level data between A and C. This data firewall is what enforces the NCCA/ISO 17024 separation between education and certification — the same separation that requires A and C to be distinct legal entities. The structure is not bureaucratic overhead; it is the precondition for the credential to be accreditable.
