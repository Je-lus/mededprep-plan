# Regulatory & CPD Landscape (UK / Ireland)

**Date:** 2026-06-19
**Classification:** Internal expansion scouting

**Bottom line:** The regulatory barrier to *selling content* is actually **lower** than the US — the UK requires no pre-accredited content and mandates no fixed CE hours. But our US accreditation moat (**CAPCE/NREMT**) is **worthless** in Europe, and content must be **clinically re-authored** against local guidelines (UK JRCALC, Ireland PHECC CPGs), not merely translated.

---

## 1. United Kingdom — HCPC

- **Regulator:** Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC). Registration is mandatory to practice as a paramedic (~30,000+ registrants).
- **CPD rules:** HCPC does **not** mandate a fixed number of CE hours and does **not** require content to be pre-accredited. It requires CPD that is **varied, relevant, and documented with reflection**, audited on a sample basis at renewal.
- **Strategic implication:** There is **no accreditation gatekeeper** to clear before selling. Quality content a paramedic can log "counts." Far lower barrier to entry than the US CAPCE regime — we compete on **content quality + platform**, not on an accreditation stamp.
- **Clinical standard:** UK practice runs on **JRCALC** guidelines. Content must align to JRCALC, not US NHTSA/NREMT.

## 2. Ireland — PHECC

- **Regulator:** Pre-Hospital Emergency Care Council (PHECC) — statutory body; sets standards, examines at 6 practitioner levels (EMT / Paramedic / Advanced Paramedic), controls registration, publishes Clinical Practice Guidelines (CPGs).
- **CPD rules:** Formal **Continuing Professional Competence (CPC)** framework links ongoing competence to registration — more structured than UK. Annual registration fee is nominal (~€10).
- **Clinical standard:** Ireland runs on **PHECC CPGs** — distinct from both UK and US.

## 3. Why the US moat doesn't transfer

- **CAPCE / NREMT are US constructs.** They have no recognition or standing with HCPC or PHECC. The accreditation pipeline we're building in the US confers **zero advantage** in Europe.
- Recognition runs the other way too — US bodies only accept US-sourced education. International credentials don't auto-recognize across the Atlantic in either direction.

## 4. Localization is clinical, not linguistic

Language is English throughout this cluster, so **translation is not the cost**. The real work is **clinical re-authoring**:

- Different guidelines (JRCALC vs PHECC vs US NHTSA).
- Different drug names and formularies (e.g., paracetamol vs acetaminophen) and different drug availability.
- Different scope of practice per practitioner level.
- Different terminology, protocols, and assessment conventions.

US content lifted directly will be **clinically wrong** and a UK/Irish paramedic will spot it immediately. This is why the entry strategy (see `04-entry-strategy-and-fit.md`) is platform-first with content re-authored against local guidelines.
