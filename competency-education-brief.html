Hours Prove Attendance. Not Competence.

The Opportunity

EMS continuing education is still measured by seat time

Every two years, EMS providers renew by logging hours, not by proving they can still perform. Research across healthcare professions shows seat time does not predict competence.

MedEdPrep builds adaptive competency assessments for EMS. A provider takes an assessment that adapts in real time to what they know. The result is a measured competency profile, not a certificate of attendance.

The Kansas Board of EMS has opened a formal review of continuing education through Board Task 2024AS0001. Kansas also has a regulation on the books allowing renewal through a cognitive assessment paired with medical director validation, instead of logging CE hours.

How It Works

Two pathways, both built on what Kansas already accepts

This does not require new legislation. The immediate path is continuing education credit. The larger path is board approval for competency-based renewal.

Available Today
CAPCE-accredited adaptive assessment
Kansas automatically accepts CAPCE-accredited programs for CE credit. CAPCE already has a designation for adaptive learning courses through F4 accreditation, and Virginia classifies adaptive learning as classroom instruction for CE credit. A CAPCE-accredited adaptive competency assessment can count toward CE hours in Kansas and every other state that accepts CAPCE.
With Proof of Concept
Board-approved cognitive assessment for recertification
Kansas regulation allows providers to renew by passing a board-approved cognitive assessment combined with medical director validation of psychomotor skills, instead of completing CE hours. This renewal pathway is separate from the initial certification exam regulation.

The medical director validation solves the hardest infrastructure problem.

No one needs to stand up skills testing stations.

The provider demonstrates cognitive competency through an adaptive assessment.

Their medical director attests to clinical skills.

Both pathways work because of what Kansas already has in place, and both solve problems Kansas cannot currently address.

Recommended Continuing Education Focus
Longitudinal Decay

Prior evidence fades over time. A Strong classification from six months ago starts the next session with partial credit. After eighteen months, the prior has decayed enough that a fresh assessment is warranted. This follows established spaced repetition research and mirrors the decay assumptions in ABIM's longitudinal model.

What this means operationally: decayed domains are prioritized in the next assessment. Recently changed protocols trigger immediate reassessment regardless of prior status. State directors can surface providers whose Strong classifications are older than 12 months for targeted re-evaluation before renewal.

The technology is the foundation. These are the moves that put it in front of Kansas.

Next Steps

The work breaks into three phases

Phase 1: Ready Now
  1. 1 Georgia integration outcomes
    The proof of concept is already collecting data. MedEdPrep is the required testing platform for Georgia's grant-funded EMS programs through the Georgia Trauma Commission. That outcome data is the evidence package for Kansas.
  2. 2 CAPCE F4 accreditation requirements
    The F4 adaptive learning designation makes this product automatically accepted in Kansas and every state that accepts CAPCE. Only one provider currently holds it.
  3. 3 Ohio competency recertification toolkit
    Ohio is the only state that has operationalized competency-based recertification. Their Option 4 pathway allows medical directors to verify competency across six domains with zero CE hours required. The EMFTS Board approved the toolkit in October 2025. Understanding what they built informs the assessment design. Contact: Joseph A. Kirk, Ohio Division of EMS, jakirk@dps.ohio.gov, 614-466-5605.
Phase 2: With Product Design
  1. 4 Competency assessment domain structure
    The assessment needs a domain hierarchy covering full scope of practice for each EMS level, going deeper than NREMT content domains. Kansas EMTs have expanded scope, including supraglottic airways, CPAP, capnometry, and 12-lead EKG acquisition, that a Kansas-specific assessment should cover.
  2. 5 Kansas-specific question generation
    The existing 10,600-item bank already has domain and sub-topic tagging in place. New questions for Kansas expanded scope can be generated from the existing competency model, covering areas like supraglottic airways and 12-lead EKG acquisition that go beyond standard NREMT content.
Phase 3: With Proof of Concept
  • Kansas Rethinking CE engagement
    Board Task 2024AS0001 is an open, active review with public input invited. It was introduced in December 2024, assigned to the EETC committee, and remains on the active committee assignments. This is the venue for presenting a competency-based approach backed by Georgia data and national precedent. Public input: joseph.house@ks.gov, reference item 2024AS0001.
  • NASEMSO annual meeting alignment
    A Kansas proof of concept positions the model for national visibility where state EMS directors evaluate education innovations.
The regulation exists. The national precedent exists. The data exists. What remains is execution.
