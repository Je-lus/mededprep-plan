Why It All Sits Here
MedEdPrep LLC has been operating in Georgia for years. There is no IP licensing structure, no intercompany agreements, and no entity proliferation — development, IP, and operations are intentionally commingled because the business has not yet reached the revenue scale that justifies the administrative cost of separation. Spinning out entities prematurely adds registered-agent fees, foreign-qualification filings, and a separate compliance calendar for a solo operator, with no offsetting benefit. Build the revenue first; carve out the entities as each one earns its keep.

◆ State-Mandated Testing Tool
MedEdPrep is already a Georgia Trauma Commission (GTC) vendor for testing. Grant-funded EMS programs in Georgia are mandated to use MedEdPrep for testing. This is more than a revenue line — it is credibility evidence for every other grant application ("state-mandated testing tool"), and it establishes a documented fair-market price for the future 501(c)(3)'s arm's-length software purchases.
Why Company A Stays in Georgia
  • Already operating as MedEdPrep LLC — no reason to redomicile
  • GTC vendor relationship is valuable and state-specific
  • Customers (Georgia schools, EMS programs) are in-state
  • EOPA approved through OEMS + GaDOE CTAE — Georgia pathways
  • Redomiciling would force vendor re-registration and customer confusion
Georgia LLC Economics
  • Annual registration: $50/year
  • State income tax: 5.49% flat (2026) on GA-apportioned income
  • No franchise tax
  • Pass-through taxation; LLC liability protection
  • S-Corp election (Form 2553) available later if net income exceeds ~$80K–$100K

⚠ The Vault's Domicile Doesn't Change Company A's
When The Vault forms in Wyoming and licenses IP back to Company A, Georgia's intangible-expense addback statute (O.C.G.A. §48-7-28.3) disallows the royalty deduction on Company A's Georgia return — so there is no Georgia state-tax benefit from the IP licensing structure while operations stay in-state. The Vault is for asset protection and clean IP architecture, not Georgia tax arbitrage. Company A's correct answer either way: stay a Georgia LLC.

How to Read This Tree
Everything below The Vault is a future operating company. Today there is exactly one box that exists: Company A. It currently holds the IP that will eventually sit in The Vault, runs the products that will eventually become Companies B, C, and the CE line, and generates the data that justifies Company E. Each spin-out happens only when its own revenue and risk profile justifies the formation and compliance cost.
Phase 1 — Foundation Now
Company A keeps running in Georgia, unchanged. The Vault forms in Wyoming and IP transfers in via Section 351; the 501(c)(3) forms in Georgia. Companies B, C, D, and E are deliberately NOT formed yet — avoid entity proliferation before revenue justifies it.
  • Keep Company A as-is
  • Form The Vault (WY)
  • Form 501(c)(3) (GA)
  • Execute IP license → Company A
Phase 2 — Entity Separation As revenue scales
Carve out the operating companies as each product line matures. Company B when CoAssist runs as a standalone product, Company C when certification development begins (independent board immediately), Company E when data aggregation becomes a distinct operation.
  • Form Company B (GA)
  • Form Company C (GA, separate brand)
  • Form Company E (GA)
Phase 3 — Certification Launch ~2.5–3 yr process
Company C pursues NCCA accreditation. The firewall NCCA Standard 3 requires — staff separation, SME separation, independent governance, data separation — is satisfied by a separate Georgia LLC with proper governance. Different-state formation is not required.

  • Bifactor dual-validation certs

Establish the Wyoming DAPT trust above The Vault, implement full transfer-pricing documentation, evaluate S-Corp elections for entities above ~$100K net income, and form Company D (hospice software) when that product begins.
