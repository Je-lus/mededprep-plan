The Structural Reality

The 501(c)(3) is a standalone nonprofit corporation incorporated in Georgia under O.C.G.A. Title 14, Chapter 3. It cannot be owned. It has no parent entity. It has no subsidiary relationship to The Vault, MedEdPrep LLC, or any other entity in the structure.

IRS 4-Part Independence Test

A director is independent only if: (1) not compensated as officer or employee, (2) did not receive >$10K as contractor, (3) no reportable Schedule L transactions with the nonprofit, (4) no reportable transactions with related organizations. All 4 independent directors pass all 4 tests.

🎯 Mission & Programs3 program areas · Dual mission ▾
“The corporation is organized exclusively for charitable and educational purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, including but not limited to: advancing the quality, accessibility, and equity of emergency medical services education and training; promoting public health and safety through workforce development in out-of-hospital care; supporting end-of-life and hospice care education and access; and providing educational tools and resources to underserved communities and educational institutions.”
Program 1: EMS Education Access
  • Provide free or subsidized access to validated educational tools, test prep, and state-approved exams for high school EMS programs, underserved communities, and students facing financial barriers.
  • How: Foundation receives grant funding → purchases MedEdPrep access at FMV → provides free to qualifying programs and students.
  • Baseline: MedEdPrep currently serves HS programs with free test prep and $30/student EOPA exams. Foundation formalizes and scales this.
  • Measurable outcomes: HS programs served, students with free access, pass rates on EOPA/state exams, geographic reach.
Program 2: Out-of-Hospital Workforce Development
  • Advance EMS education quality through research, standards development, CE access, and professional development for EMS educators and practitioners.
  • How: Fund scholarships, support research (powered by anonymized aggregate data from Company E), develop open educational resources, host/sponsor CE events.
  • This is the GEMSA-model program — positions the nonprofit as advancing EMS education broadly.
  • Measurable outcomes: Scholarships awarded, research publications, CE events hosted, educator training programs supported.
Program 3: End-of-Life Care Education & Access
  • Improve hospice and palliative care quality through technology access, workforce training, and community education.
  • How: As Company D (Hospice) matures, foundation purchases hospice tech at FMV; provides hospice education to underserved communities; supports training for hospice workers and caregivers.
  • Ensures nonprofit serves beyond EMS/MedEdPrep interests. Broadens mission and opens grant doors in aging/palliative space.
  • Measurable outcomes: Communities served, hospice workers trained, technology access provided.
Vision Statement

Propel out-of-hospital education, clinical capabilities, and end-of-life care forward through equitable technology access, workforce development, and public safety advancement.

Public Charity Status Matters
The 501(c)(3) transaction model depends on PUBLIC CHARITY status. If the nonprofit fails the one-third public support test, it becomes a private foundation — and Section 4941 self-dealing rules prohibit ALL transactions with MedEdPrep regardless of FMV. Keep founder/MedEdPrep contributions minimal while grant revenue ramps.
