## Bottom line up front
The belief "**no one** in EMS test-prep is doing real psychometrics" is **not strictly true** — but the honest version of the story is *more useful and still strongly in MedEdPrep's favor.*

- **One competitor group genuinely does it:** **Ascend Learning / Jones & Bartlett** has a real corporate psychometrics department and even published an EMS predictive-validity study. JBL also owns **FISDAP**, historically the most serious EMS-assessment shop.
- **But nobody does what MedEdPrep is planning:** an **independent, EMS-first** company with an **in-house psychometrician** building **IRT-calibrated, in-product adaptive testing on uniquely rich behavioral data** (mandatory continuous confidence, school/instructor hierarchy, distractor traces). That specific combination is open white space.

**The defensible claim is not "we're the only ones who do psychometrics." It's: "we're the only independent EMS-first platform doing in-house, in-product psychometrics on data this rich — versus competitors who do none, lean on classical methods + marketing, or treat EMS as one side-domain of a giant publisher's centralized team."** That claim survives the evidence.

## The three findings that actually matter

**1. The serious EMS psychometric capability has consolidated into one publisher.**
JBL/Ascend owns **both** Navigate (its own EMS test prep, with the published validity study) **and** FISDAP (the historically data-driven EMS shop). The independent EMS-first psychometric capability that FISDAP once represented effectively got absorbed — and FISDAP's strongest era (Bowen as Director of Testing) **ended in 2019** when he left for Meazure Learning. So among *independent* EMS-first vendors operating today, in-house psychometrics is essentially **absent**.

**2. The big player does it generically, not EMS-natively.**
Ascend's psychometrics is a **centralized corporate function** serving nursing, allied health, fitness, and EMS. There is **no EMS-dedicated psychometrician**, no named author on the EMS study, and the published method is a **basic logistic-regression validity study** — not in-product IRT-calibrated adaptive testing. Their scholarship skews nursing/healthcare.

**3. Everyone else is doing classical methods or none.**
Platinum publicly publishes **CTT** (not IRT, despite the marketing line). Pocket Prep and Limmer show **no measurement-science function at all** — Limmer's credibility is ex-regulator *item construction*, which is real but categorically different from quantitative psychometrics.

## Where the genuine white space is
No competitor publicly combines all of these — and MedEdPrep's plan hits every one:
- **In-house + in-product** (vs. Ascend's centralized corporate team, or others' none).
- **EMS-first/dedicated** (vs. EMS-as-side-domain).
- **IRT-calibrated adaptive** (vs. CTT or lay "adaptive").
- **On rich behavioral data competitors almost certainly lack:** mandatory **continuous per-item confidence** (1.6M responses), the **school→branch→instructor hierarchy**, full **distractor traces**, the metacognition signal.

That last point is the real moat: even the sophisticated player is doing *score → pass-probability*. MedEdPrep can do *calibrated-item + confidence + program-context → adaptive measurement*, which is a different league of signal.

## The honest competitive risk (name it, don't hide it)
**Ascend/JBL is the one competitor who *could* turn toward EMS-native psychometrics if they chose to** — they have the department, the data pipeline, and now FISDAP's heritage. The moat is therefore **"they're not currently focused on EMS-native, in-product work, and they don't have data this rich"** — *not* "they can't." That's a focus-and-data advantage, which is real but not permanent. Implication: the value is in **moving now and publishing first**, so MedEdPrep owns the "evidence-based EMS prep" position before anyone with more resources decides to contest it.
