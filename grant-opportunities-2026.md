## 1. Federal SBIR/STTR Programs

The SBIR/STTR programs were reauthorized on March 17, 2026 through September 30, 2031. All SBIR programs are available to for-profit small businesses (<500 employees) and do NOT require 501(c)(3) status.

**Prerequisite for all federal SBIR applications:** SAM.gov registration (COMPLETE), SBIR.gov company registration (COMPLETE), agency-specific portal registration (varies).

**How it works:** NSF uses a two-step process. Step 1 is a ~1,500-word Project Pitch across four prompts (Technology Innovation, Technical Objectives/Challenges, Market Opportunity, Company/Team). NSF reviews the pitch within 3--6 weeks and either invites a full proposal or declines. Step 2 is the full proposal, submitted through Research.gov within one year of the invitation.

**Relevant topics:** "Learning and Cognitive Technologies" (LCT), "Artificial Intelligence" (AI), "Human-Centered Computing" (HC). NSF does NOT issue topic-specific solicitations — the applicant defines the innovation.

**MedEdPrep alignment (STRONG):**
- The IRT-based adaptive assessment engine with 1.55M student responses represents genuine technical innovation, not "straightforward engineering" (NSF's key criterion)
- Bifactor psychometric modeling and CAT engine architecture are novel approaches in EMS education
- Metacognition research with UIUC provides academic validation
- Pulse gamification engine introduces novel game-based learning mechanics

**Key criterion:** Technical Risk. NSF explicitly does NOT want to fund product development or "straightforward engineering." The pitch must frame the work as high-risk, high-reward R&D. Frame the innovation as "novel IRT-based predictive model for professional competency assessment" rather than "test prep platform."

**Application requirements:**
- Research.gov account (COMPLETE — password pending)
- PI must commit 51%+ time to the project during the award period (or equivalent)
- 2/3 of Phase I work must be performed by the small business

**Action items:**
1. Complete Research.gov password setup (2-minute task — retry off-peak hours)

## 2. Workforce Development

### 2.1 DOL Workforce Innovation Grants

The Department of Labor periodically issues competitive grants under various workforce development authorities. Relevant programs:

**H-1B Skills Training Grants:** DOL uses H-1B visa fee revenue to fund workforce training grants. These fund partnerships between employers, educational institutions, and workforce boards. Healthcare/allied health is a priority sector. Awards range from $500K to $5M over 4 years. MedEdPrep's fit: technology vendor to grant recipient consortia, not direct applicant (for-profit can be a vendor/subcontractor, not lead applicant on most DOL grants).

**YouthBuild / Job Corps:** Federal programs that fund vocational training for underserved populations. EMS is an eligible career pathway. The nonprofit entity (once formed) could partner with existing YouthBuild grantees to provide EMS test prep.

**Apprenticeship grants:** DOL funds registered apprenticeship expansion. EMS agencies that operate registered apprenticeship programs can use these funds for training technology. Vendor/subcontractor opportunity.

**MedEdPrep positioning:** For most DOL grants, a for-profit company serves as a vendor or subcontractor to the grant-receiving entity (typically a workforce board, community college, or nonprofit). The strategy is to position MedEdPrep tools as line items in other organizations' DOL grant applications.

### 2.2 WIOA Funding (Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act)

WIOA is the primary federal workforce development law. It funds state and local workforce development boards, which in turn fund training for job seekers and incumbent workers.

**Three pathways for MedEdPrep:**

**Individual Training Accounts (ITAs):** Once MedEdPrep is on the Georgia Eligible Training Provider List (ETPL), WIOA-funded students can use ITAs (up to $5,000+ per student) to pay for MedEdPrep courses. This is a revenue stream, not a grant.

**Incumbent Worker Training:** Local workforce boards fund employer-side training. EMS agencies can use IWT funds to purchase MedEdPrep CE and test prep for their staff. Marketing opportunity.

**Youth Workforce Programs:** WIOA Title I Youth Programs fund training for 16--24 year olds. EMS certification is an eligible pathway. Partner with workforce boards to include MedEdPrep in youth EMT training programs.

**20 Local Workforce Development Areas (LWDAs) in Georgia.** Primary contacts: WorkSource Atlanta, WorkSource Coastal, WorkSource North Georgia.

### 4.2 Other HRSA Programs (Progression Pathway)

HRSA programs build on each other. The typical progression:

**Year 1: RHNDP — $100K planning grant**
Build the network, conduct needs assessment, develop strategic plan.

**Years 2--5: Rural Health Care Services Outreach — $250K--$300K/year for 4 years**
Implement the plan. Requires consortium of 3+ healthcare providers, 66% in rural areas. Total: up to $1.2M over 4 years. Probability: Medium-High after successful RHNDP.

**Years 3+: Rural Health Network Development (RHND) — $300K/year for 4 years**
Scale the network. More competitive. Total: up to $1.2M over 4 years. Probability: Medium after established track record.
