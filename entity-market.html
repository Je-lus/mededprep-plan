Market Landscape

EMS Education & Certification

The EMS assessment market is dominated by three incumbents — Platinum / EMSTesting, Ascend Learning’s JB Learning + Fisdap, and Pearson’s Brady / MyLab — plus a fragmented field of 40+ continuing-education providers. None ships true IRT-based adaptive testing, none offers free standalone accreditation compliance, and none pairs a B2C channel with institutional reach. MedEdPrep’s moat is the combination: psychometric rigor on 10,600+ items, CoAssist as a free compliance loss-leader, and a unified platform spanning test prep, clinicals, engagement, and CE.

📊 Market OverviewHow the EMS education market is structured ▾
The Shape of the Market
EMS education breaks into three layers: textbooks (Brady/Pearson and JB Learning split ~85–90% of adoptions), institutional assessment + clinical tracking (Platinum, Fisdap, and the publisher MyLab/Navigate companions), and continuing education (40+ providers selling CE hours). MedEdPrep is the only entrant building across all three layers on a single psychometric data spine, and the only one selling to both institutions (B2B) and individual students (B2C).

Two Publishers Own the Textbook Layer
Brady (Pearson) and Jones & Bartlett (Ascend / KKR) split the EMS textbook market roughly 50/50, together controlling an estimated 85–90% of adoptions — Brady historically stronger in EMT, J&B in paramedic. Each bundles a digital companion (MyLab Brady, Navigate) that pulls programs into a publisher-locked workflow. The textbook decision drives everything downstream.
Assessment Is Where MedEdPrep Competes
Platinum / EMSTesting is the strongest independent assessment platform; Fisdap dominates clinical tracking and readiness exams; publisher companions handle chapter quizzes. All run on Classical Test Theory. None ships item-level IRT calibration, real adaptive delivery, or multi-dimensional diagnostic reporting — the gap MedEdPrep is built around.

🔍 Competitor Deep DivesPlatinum · JB / Fisdap · Brady / Pearson ▾
🏛 Platinum Educational Group / EMSTesting
Independent · Jenison, MI · founded 2002 · ~25 employees
 Most Displaceable

The strongest independent EMS assessment company — not owned by a publisher or PE firm. Three products: EMSTesting (exam bank), Platinum Planner (clinical scheduling), and AccredAssist (CoAEMSP compliance). Estimated ~$4–8M revenue, with valuation analyses landing in the $20M–$40M range on EMS-ed-tech multiples. Occupies exactly the instructor-facing exam-bank position MedEdPrep targets — making it the competitor most likely to be displaced. Pearson resells EMSTesting through its channel, so a future Pearson acquisition is a credible watch item.

▲ Strengths
  • Deep instructor muscle memory — directors who’ve used it 10+ years can build an exam in 10 minutes
  • Purpose-built for EMS; large pre-validated bank trusted by educators
  • Three-product ecosystem creates switching cost
  • Free-for-schools Planner model removes the budget-approval barrier
  • 24 years of conference relationships and distribution
▼ Weaknesses
  • “CAT” is CTT — P-values, KR-20, point-biserial; no published IRT, no theta estimation
  • Institutional-only; students can’t review what they got wrong
  • No B2C channel, no classroom engagement layer
  • AccredAssist ($475) requires a Platinum subscription — vs. free, standalone CoAssist
  • CAPCE for workshops only (Nov 2025) — no online CE delivery
📚 JB Learning / Public Safety Group / Fisdap
Ascend Learning (KKR-backed, $2B+ revenue) · Navigate + Fisdap + Recert
 High Presence / Low Response

The largest entity in EMS education by footprint — textbooks (AAOS “Orange Book,” Nancy Caroline), Navigate courseware, Fisdap testing + clinical tracking, and Recert.com CE. A cradle-to-career lock-in no one else can replicate. But the products were acquired separately and remain architecturally disconnected: Navigate and Fisdap have separate logins, separate data models, and no automated data flow. That integration gap is the structural weakness MedEdPrep’s unified platform exploits.

▲ Strengths
  • Textbook→CE ecosystem lock-in — the only end-to-end pipeline in the market
  • ~50–60% of paramedic programs use a JBL primary text
  • Fisdap predictive-validity research (r ~ 0.3–0.5) is a strong sales asset
  • Mature clinical tracking; Ascend / KKR financial resources
  • NAEMT partnership (Recert) lends CE credibility
▼ Weaknesses
  • Navigate ≠ Fisdap — two products, two logins, no integration
  • Classical Test Theory only — documented, not speculation
  • Navigate is publisher-locked to JBL textbooks
  • Consistently negative student sentiment on Fisdap UX
  • PE operating model optimizes margin, not innovation — slow cadence
📘 Brady / Pearson / MyLab Brady
Pearson plc (~$4.7B revenue) · Brady is a publishing imprint, not a separate entity
 Biggest Structural Risk

The biggest structural risk — Pearson’s scale, textbook install base (~45–55% of programs), and theoretical ability to build adaptive testing into MyLab. In practice, EMS is <0.5% of Pearson revenue (~$20–50M), so EMS-specific innovation velocity is near zero. Brady’s brand power lives in its authors (Limmer, Bledsoe, Mistovich), not the corporate structure. MyLab Brady is a textbook companion, not a standalone assessment platform — every Brady program still needs a separate tool for comprehensive, entrance, and exit exams. The danger is measured in years, not quarters.

▲ Strengths
  • Textbook distribution lock-in — every adoption is a built-in MyLab channel
  • Generational brand recognition; author authority
  • Enterprise LMS integration (Canvas, Blackboard, D2L)
  • Inclusive Access / First-Day removes the “will students buy?” problem
  • Pearson VUE psychometric expertise exists (in a separate division)
▼ Weaknesses
  • “Adaptive” is branching logic — pretest/posttest, no IRT, no CAT
  • No standalone assessment — can’t do comprehensive/entrance/exit exams
  • No diagnostic reporting beyond % correct per chapter
  • Publisher-locked to Brady; no B2C path; no CE play
  • Innovation velocity near zero — EMS too small to prioritize
⚠ The Consolidation Watch Item
The single most dangerous scenario is Pearson acquiring Platinum outright (not just the existing distribution deal) and folding EMSTesting into MyLab Brady — textbook + assessment in one platform. Probability is low (~5–8% over two years) and integration would take 12–24 months even if it happened. Trigger signals to monitor: a Pearson–Platinum acquisition, Platinum hiring psychometric talent and publishing IRT documentation, Platinum launching a student-facing product, or AccredAssist going free and standalone.
★ MedEdPrep Competitive PositioningThe moat and the differentiators ▾
The Core Positioning
MedEdPrep does not win on any single feature — it wins on the combination no incumbent can assemble: psychometric rigor (IRT engine + bifactor model on a 1.55M-response dataset), a free standalone compliance loss-leader (CoAssist), dual B2B + B2C channels, and a unified platform spanning test prep, clinicals, engagement, and CE. The strategy is complement, don’t compete: run alongside the incumbents, then expand scope through demonstrated superiority — never force a binary switch.
Data Moat
10,600+ items, 1.55M calibrated responses. Enables IRT parameter estimation and diagnostic precision competitors cannot replicate without years of equivalent response data. Writing questions is easy; calibrating them through live student data takes years.
Dual Channel
B2B + B2C. Sells to institutions and to individual students. Every incumbent is institutional-only. Students buy MedEdPrep even at programs running Platinum or Fisdap — then advocate to instructors.
Free Compliance Wedge
CoAssist is free, standalone, vendor-agnostic. AccredAssist needs a Platinum subscription; Fisdap reports need Fisdap. Programs adopt CoAssist without switching testing — then discover MedEdPrep’s assessment from inside the ecosystem.
Engagement Layer
Pulse fills the gap between exam days — live games, leaderboards, XP, streaks, study tracking. Incumbents are invisible between testing events; Pulse is present in every class and study session, generating habit and data.
Psychometric Depth
Multi-dimensional diagnostics by domain, cognitive level, confidence, timing, and stamina — plus 1M+ metacognition-tagged responses. The closest competitor (EMSTesting’s MTO drill-down) is textbook-structured, not psychometrically derived.
Price & Independence
$26/student flat, no school fee, publisher-agnostic. 3–5x cheaper than a typical Platinum program ($2,475–4,125/yr) and works with any textbook — unlike Navigate (JBL-only) and MyLab (Brady-only). Hold price in Year 1; chase market share.
The Displacement Strategy
Phase 1 — Wedge (now–Q3 2026): CoAssist (free) and B2C student adoption get MedEdPrep inside programs without forcing a vendor switch. Phase 2 — Parallel Pilot (Q3–Q4 2026): “Run us alongside Platinum / Fisdap for one cohort” — low risk, the diagnostic gap becomes visible. Phase 3 — Conversion (Q1 2027+): programs that piloted see the data-quality difference and consolidate. Target SEO: “Fisdap alternative.” Move fast — the window is real but finite.

Why the Vacuum Exists — and Why It’s Defensible
CAPCE has confirmed assessment-only CE is allowed (0.25 CEH minimum, auto-reporting), and NREMT permanently removed online CE caps in 2023. The structural conditions all favor adaptive CE. No competitor offers it because they lack the psychometric infrastructure — the same data spine (Companies A and C) that powers MedEdPrep’s diagnostic loop. MedEdPrep’s CE play sells CE hours that also generate diagnostic data, closing the gap-identification-to-remediation loop no hours-only provider can match.
🔁 Market Context: The NREMT Exam RedesignThe largest exam restructuring in NREMT history ▾
⚠ Fully Live April 7, 2025
The National Registry replaced its legacy content areas (Airway / Trauma / Cardiology / Medical / OB-Pediatrics) with a new four-domain framework across all levels simultaneously, expanded Technology-Enhanced Items (TEIs) to every level, and — effective July 1, 2024 — permanently discontinued the ALS psychomotor exam, making cognitive testing the only gate for AEMT and Paramedic candidates.

What It Means for MedEdPrep
  • Content mapping: all 10,600+ items remapped to the four-domain structure; legacy tags retained for history
  • TEI validation: Pulse’s 10 question types (drag-drop, ordering, multi-select, classification) directly match the NREMT’s TEI expansion
  • Diagnostics realigned: reports now speak the new domain language programs and accreditors evaluate against
  • Cognitive-only gate elevates diagnostic feedback from valuable to singular — it’s now the primary readiness tool
The Competitive Window It Opens
Neither Platinum / EMSTesting, Fisdap, nor MyLab Brady has published documentation confirming alignment with the new four-domain structure. Fisdap’s unit exams were historically built on the legacy domains; whether they’ve been remapped is unconfirmed. Competitors still shipping only multiple-choice are now visibly misaligned with the actual exam format. This creates a window where MedEdPrep can be the first platform demonstrably aligned to the redesigned exam — in both content taxonomy and item types.
