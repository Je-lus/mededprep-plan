# Georgia State-Level Funding Sources

*Research date: 2026-04-06*

---

## 1. Georgia Trauma Commission (GTC) — Highest Priority

### Overview
GTC operates under DPH and oversees trauma system funding, including EMS education and workforce development. Funded primarily through motor vehicle surcharges (super speeder fines, tag fees).

### EMS Education Funding
- GTC historically contracted with GEMSA for statewide EMS education coordination
- **GEMSA did not get the contract renewed** (confirmed by founder)
- GTC has indicated it is **not particularly interested in funding the nonprofit specifically**

### Existing MedEdPrep/GTC Relationship
**MedEdPrep is already a GTC vendor for testing.** Grant-funded EMS programs in Georgia are mandated to use MedEdPrep for testing. This is significant:
- Provides strong credibility evidence for grant applications to other funders ("state-mandated testing tool")
- Establishes a documented market price (FMV) for the nonprofit's software purchases
- Must be disclosed on Form 1023 and managed through COI policy
- The nonprofit's value-add is providing access to programs that are NOT grant-funded (underfunded HS programs, rural programs, students facing financial barriers)

### GTC as a Grant Source
**Not a realistic near-term opportunity for the 501(c)(3).** GTC is not interested in this specific project. However, the existing vendor relationship and mandated usage remains a powerful piece of evidence for all other grant applications.

### How to Engage
- **GTC Board Meetings:** Public, typically quarterly. Agendas/minutes at gtc.georgia.gov
- **EMS Subcommittee:** Makes recommendations on education spending before full Commission votes
- **Procurement:** Follows state DOAS guidelines. Larger contracts go through competitive RFP. Smaller initiatives may be sole-sourced.
- **RFP Timing:** State fiscal year (July 1 - June 30). FY2027 budget planning begins late fall 2026.

### Immediate Action Items
1. Pull last 6 months of GTC meeting minutes — look for GEMSA contract status, education line items, new RFPs
2. Request FY2026 and FY2027 GTC budget (public document — email GTC office or file Open Records Request under Georgia Open Records Act)
3. Attend/watch next GTC meeting to understand current priorities
4. Identify GTC Executive Director, EMS Subcommittee Chair, fiscal officer
5. Determine whether education contract is currently vacant, rebid, or restructured

## 2. Georgia Office of EMS and Trauma (OEMS) — Under DPH

### Overview
Handles EMS licensure, education program approval, and exam standards. Primarily a regulatory body, not a major grant-maker.

### Relevance
- Maintains list of state-approved EMS education programs
- Approves End of Pathway Assessment (EOPA) exams used by CTAE high school programs
- Influences how federal pass-through money (HRSA rural EMS grants) gets distributed

### State-Approved Exam Connection
- MedEdPrep's $30/student EOPA is directly relevant here
- Approval runs through both OEMS (clinical/content) and Georgia DOE CTAE (pathway compliance)
- The nonprofit could serve as a distribution channel providing free exam access for Title I schools and underserved programs — funded by grants rather than per-student fees

### Action Items
1. Confirm current state-approved EOPA exam list for EMS pathway
2. Determine whether OEMS has any grant programs or cooperative agreements
3. Build relationship with OEMS Education Coordinator — they validate grant applications claiming to improve EMS education access

**Contacts:**
- DPH OEMS Director — find at dph.georgia.gov/EMS
- State EMS Medical Director
- EMS Education Coordinator within OEMS

## 3. Georgia Department of Education — CTAE

### Overview
**Highest-probability near-term funding pathway** for the nonprofit, through Perkins V.

See [perkins-v-funding-pathway.md](../03-market-landscape/perkins-v-funding-pathway.md) for complete Perkins V analysis.

### Key Facts
- Georgia receives ~$51.2M/year in federal Perkins V funds
- After state retention, ~$43.5M flows to local districts
- Health Science cluster: 61,420 HS students, 922 teachers statewide
- Estimated 60-100 high schools with EMR/EMT pathways across 40-60 districts

### How the Nonprofit Benefits
Districts can use Perkins funds to purchase CTE curriculum, software, test prep, and instructional tools. The nonprofit positions itself (and MedEdPrep) as a Perkins-eligible vendor.

### Contacts

- General: CTAEprograms@gadoe.org
- Georgia CTAE Foundation (potential ally or co-applicant)

### Adjacent Opportunity
**Georgia Foundation for Public Education (GFPE) — Workforce for Georgia Grants**
- Spring 2026 applications due April 14, 2026
- Contact: Jaclyn Colona, jaclyn.colona@doe.k12.ga.us, (404) 445-9828
- Eligible: District-level staff at LEAs with CTAE programs
- Not direct nonprofit funding, but a district could use these to purchase MedEdPrep tools

## 4. Other Georgia State Sources

### Georgia State Office of Rural Health (SORH)
- Within DPH, focuses on healthcare access in rural Georgia
- Administers federal pass-through grants (HRSA SORH grants, ~$150K-$200K/year state-level)
- Rural EMS workforce is a priority topic
- Connected to FLEX (Medicare Rural Hospital Flexibility Program) and SHIP (State Health Improvement Plan)
- **Action:** Contact SORH and explore partnership for HRSA consortium applications

### Georgia Board of Health Care Workforce
- Healthcare workforce data and loan repayment/scholarship programs
- Primarily physicians, nurses, dentists — EMS not core focus
- Publishes Georgia healthcare workforce needs assessment — useful data for grant applications
- Workforce pipeline arguments could resonate

### Georgia Center for Nonprofits
- Capacity-building organization, not a funder
- Training for new nonprofits
- Network connecting nonprofits with funders
- Georgia Gives Day campaigns
- Board matching program (useful for finding independent directors)
- **Action:** Join for network access and new-nonprofit training

### Georgia Department of Community Affairs (DCA)
- Various community development grants
- OneGeorgia Authority grants: $50K-$250K for rural community economic development
- EMS workforce development in rural areas could qualify

### Georgia Emergency Management and Homeland Security Agency (GEMA/HS)
- Administers some federal homeland security grants with EMS components
- State Homeland Security Grant Program (SHSGP) sometimes includes EMS training funds

## Realistic Assessment

### Highest Priority (6-12 months)
1. **GTC education contract** — if open, this is the biggest single opportunity
2. **CTAE/Perkins V vendor pathway** — highest probability, already aligned with existing product
3. **OEMS relationship building** — validates grant applications, potential partnership

### Medium Priority (12-24 months)
4. **State Office of Rural Health** — consortium partner for HRSA applications
5. **GTC budget influence** — attend meetings, build relationships for FY2028 priorities
6. **GEMA/HS** — if EMS training funds are available through SHSGP

### Key Risks
- **New nonprofit credibility gap:** State agencies prefer established organizations. MedEdPrep's existing track record helps.
- **Procurement timelines:** State RFPs move slowly. Even with an opening today, 12-18 months before funds flow.
- **Political relationships:** Georgia's EMS community is small and relationship-driven. GEMSA, Georgia Association of EMS Instructors, and regional EMS councils have established GTC/OEMS relationships.

### Recommended First Moves
1. Attend next GTC public meeting (check gtc.georgia.gov)
2. File Open Records Requests for GTC budget and GEMSA contract
3. Contact CTAE Health Science Program Specialist
4. Join Georgia Center for Nonprofits
5. Map every high school with an EMS pathway, every CTAE director, every regional EMS council
