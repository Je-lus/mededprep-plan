# 501(c)(3) Formation Design — Georgia

*Working document — 2026-04-06*

## Overview

This document lays out the formation design for the nonprofit entity: mission, programs, governance, filings, firewall design, and the relationship to the for-profit entities. The nonprofit will be incorporated as a Georgia nonprofit corporation under O.C.G.A. Title 14, Chapter 3, and will apply for federal 501(c)(3) status via IRS Form 1023.

---

## Mission

### Vision
Propel out-of-hospital education, clinical capabilities, and end-of-life care forward through equitable technology access, workforce development, and public safety advancement.

### Draft Mission Statement (for Articles of Incorporation)
*This language must satisfy the IRS organizational test under IRC 501(c)(3). It must limit purposes to charitable, educational, or scientific, and permanently dedicate assets to exempt purposes.*

> The corporation is organized exclusively for charitable and educational purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, including but not limited to: advancing the quality, accessibility, and equity of emergency medical services education and training; promoting public health and safety through workforce development in out-of-hospital care; supporting end-of-life and hospice care education and access; and providing educational tools and resources to underserved communities and educational institutions.

### Draft Dissolution Clause (required for Articles of Incorporation)
> Upon the dissolution of the corporation, assets shall be distributed for one or more exempt purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, or to the federal government, or to a state or local government, for a public purpose. No part of the net earnings of the corporation shall inure to the benefit of, or be distributable to, its members, trustees, officers, or other private persons.

## Program Areas

The nonprofit needs multiple program areas to demonstrate genuine public benefit and avoid the appearance of existing solely to benefit MedEdPrep. Three initial programs:

### Program 1: EMS Education Access
**Description:** Provide free or subsidized access to validated educational tools, test preparation resources, and state-approved examinations for high school EMS programs, underserved communities, and students who face financial barriers to EMS education.

**How it works:**
- The foundation receives grant funding (Perkins V CTE, Georgia Trauma Commission, private foundations)
- The foundation purchases software access from MedEdPrep Education (Company A) at fair market value
- The foundation provides that access at no cost to qualifying high school programs and students

**Existing baseline:** MedEdPrep currently offers free test prep to high school programs and a $30/student state-approved EOPA exam. The foundation formalizes and scales this.

**Measurable outcomes:**
- Number of high school programs served
- Number of students with free access
- Pass rates on EOPA and state certification exams
- Geographic reach (urban vs rural, underserved areas)

### Program 2: Out-of-Hospital Workforce Development
**Description:** Advance the quality of EMS education and training through research, standards development, continuing education access, and professional development programs for EMS educators and practitioners.

**How it works:**
- Fund scholarships for EMS educator training
- Support research into EMS education effectiveness (powered by anonymized, aggregate data from Company E)
- Develop and publish open educational resources and best practices
- Host or sponsor continuing education events (similar to GEMSA's role with GTC)

**This is the GEMSA-model program.** It positions the nonprofit as the entity that advances EMS education broadly, not just through MedEdPrep's products.

**Measurable outcomes:**
- Scholarships awarded
- Research publications or white papers produced
- CE events hosted or sponsored
- Educator training programs supported

### Program 3: End-of-Life Care Education and Access
**Description:** Improve the quality of hospice and palliative care through technology access, workforce training, and community education about end-of-life care options.

**How it works:**
- As Company D (Hospice) matures, the foundation purchases hospice education/technology at FMV
- Provide hospice care education resources to underserved communities
- Support training for hospice workers and caregivers

**This program ensures the nonprofit serves more than just EMS/MedEdPrep interests.** It broadens the mission and opens grant doors in the aging/palliative care space.

**Measurable outcomes:**
- Communities served
- Hospice workers trained
- Technology access provided to hospice organizations

## Board of Directors

### Georgia Legal Requirements
Under O.C.G.A. § 14-3-801, a Georgia nonprofit corporation must have a board of directors. Georgia law does not prescribe a minimum number of directors (can be as few as 1), but IRS expectations and best practices require more.

### IRS and Best Practice Requirements

| Requirement | Source | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum 3 directors | IRS practical expectation | Fewer than 3 raises governance red flags on Form 1023 |
| Majority independent | IRS Form 990 definition | At least 51% must meet the 4-part independence test |
| 2/3 independent | Best practice (Independent Sector) | Stronger governance signal; recommended for dual-entity structures |
| No more than 49% related by blood/marriage | California law / IRS preference | Prevents familial control |
| Public member | NCCA requirement (for Company C later) | Not required for the nonprofit itself, but good practice |

### IRS 4-Part Independence Test (Form 990)
A director is independent ONLY if ALL of the following are true for the entire tax year:
1. Not compensated as an officer or employee of the nonprofit or any related organization
2. Did not receive >$10,000 as an independent contractor from the nonprofit or related orgs
3. Neither they nor family were involved in a reportable transaction (Schedule L) with the nonprofit
4. Neither they nor family were involved in a reportable transaction with a related organization

### Recommended Board Structure (5 Directors)

| Seat | Role | Independence | Expertise Needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Seat 1** | Founder representative | NOT independent (connected to MedEdPrep) | EMS education, technology, organizational vision |
| **Seat 2** | Independent director | Independent | Legal / nonprofit governance |
| **Seat 3** | Independent director | Independent | Finance / accounting / grant management |
| **Seat 4** | Independent director | Independent | EMS education / public safety / healthcare |
| **Seat 5** | Independent director | Independent | Community representative / hospice / end-of-life care |

**Ratio:** 4 independent out of 5 = 80% independent (exceeds the 2/3 best practice)

**The founder can serve on the board** but should NOT be board chair and should NOT have a majority. The founder's role is to provide vision and institutional knowledge while the independent directors provide governance oversight.

### Board Recruitment: Who to Look For

The 3 independent directors (plus a 4th recruited later) need to have **zero financial ties to MedEdPrep, LLC.** They cannot be employees, contractors who've been paid over $10K, or family members of the founder. Beyond independence, each seat should fill an expertise gap the nonprofit actually needs.

**Seat 2 — Legal or Nonprofit Governance**
Someone who understands nonprofit compliance, fiduciary duty, or has served on other nonprofit boards. This person keeps the governance process honest and can help navigate COI situations.

Where to look:
- Local attorneys (nonprofit law, corporate law, healthcare law) willing to do pro bono board service
- CPAs or financial professionals with nonprofit clients
- People currently serving on other nonprofit boards (Georgia Center for Nonprofits maintains a board matching program)
- Faculty at Georgia universities in public administration, nonprofit management, or law

**Seat 3 — Finance, Accounting, or Grant Management**
Someone who can read financial statements, oversee the audit process, and understand grant compliance. Essential for the audit committee (which should be 100% independent directors).

Where to look:
- CFOs, controllers, or accountants at non-competing organizations
- Grant administrators at universities or government agencies
- Retired finance professionals
- Community foundation staff or former staff

**Seat 4 — EMS Education or Public Safety**
Someone with credibility in the EMS world who is NOT a MedEdPrep customer, vendor, or partner. This person makes the nonprofit's mission credible to grantors and state agencies.

Where to look:
- EMS educators at community colleges or universities (not programs that use MedEdPrep)
- Retired fire chiefs, EMS directors, or state EMS office staff
- Public health faculty with EMS/emergency management focus
- Emergency physicians or medical directors not affiliated with MedEdPrep
- NAEMSE (National Association of EMS Educators) members in Georgia

**Seat 5 (recruit later) — Community or End-of-Life Care**
Someone outside the EMS/tech world entirely. Demonstrates to the IRS that the board isn't just industry insiders. Also covers the hospice/end-of-life program area.

Where to look:
- Hospice or palliative care administrators
- Community health organizers
- Local business leaders with no healthcare ties
- School district administrators (connects to the HS EMS program mission)
- Faith-based community leaders involved in end-of-life care ministries

### Disqualified from Independence: Who NOT to Recruit

The following people **cannot** serve as independent directors, no matter how qualified:

- Anyone currently employed by or receiving salary from MedEdPrep, LLC
- Anyone who has been paid more than $10,000 by MedEdPrep as a contractor in the past year
- Family members of the founder (spouse, siblings, parents, children)
- Anyone with a significant financial transaction with MedEdPrep (loans, equity, leases, major business deals)
- MedEdPrep customers or partners where the financial relationship is significant or ongoing (use judgment — a school that pays $30/student for EOPA is probably fine; a reseller earning commissions is not)

**People who are close but risky:**
- Former MedEdPrep employees (safe if the employment ended and there are no ongoing payments, but the IRS may ask questions — cleaner to avoid)
- People who might enter a financial relationship with MedEdPrep in the future (like the potential reseller discussed — better to place them on the advisory board)

### Starting with Fewer Than 5 Directors

You can incorporate with as few as 3 directors (you + 2 independent) and add more later. Georgia law allows it. However:

| Starting Board | Independence Ratio | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| 3 directors (1 founder + 2 independent) | 67% | Meets the 2/3 threshold exactly. Tight — no margin if one person's status changes. |
| 4 directors (1 founder + 3 independent) | 75% | Comfortable. This is the target for initial filing. |
| 5 directors (1 founder + 4 independent) | 80% | Ideal. Demonstrates strong governance from day one. |

**Recommendation:** File with 4 directors if you can find 3 qualified independent people. Recruit the 5th within the first year.

## Advisory Board (Non-Governing)

An advisory board lets you involve people whose expertise you value but who can't or shouldn't serve as voting directors — either because they have financial ties to MedEdPrep, or because they're more useful as advisors than as governance participants.

### How It Differs from the Board of Directors

| | Board of Directors | Advisory Board |
|---|---|---|
| **Legal authority** | Fiduciary duty, voting power, legal liability | None — purely advisory |
| **IRS reporting** | Disclosed on Form 990, independence tested | Not reported to IRS |
| **Independence test** | Required | Does not apply |
| **COI policy** | Bound by it, must sign annual disclosure | Not required (but good practice) |
| **Meetings** | Formal, minutes required, quorum rules | Informal, no legal requirements |
| **Compensation** | Usually unpaid or modest stipend | No restrictions |
| **Removal** | Governed by bylaws | At founder's/board's discretion |

### Advisory Board Structure

The advisory board has no legal authority. Advisors provide expertise, connections, and feedback. The board of directors makes all decisions. This distinction must be clear in all communications — advisors should never be referred to as "directors" or "board members" in any official capacity.

**Recommended advisory roles:**

| Role | Why This Person | Who to Consider |
|---|---|---|
| **Industry Advisor** | Market insight, product-market fit, partnership intros | MedEdPrep customers, partners, potential resellers |
| **Technology Advisor** | Platform architecture, scaling, security | Developers, CTOs, technical founders outside of MedEdPrep |
| **EMS Practice Advisor** | Clinical relevance, scope of practice, credentialing insight | Active paramedics, medical directors, flight medics |
| **Education Policy Advisor** | Curriculum standards, CTE pathways, state education contacts | State CTE coordinators, education department contacts |
| **Hospice/Palliative Care Advisor** | End-of-life program design, hospice industry connections | Hospice directors, palliative care physicians, social workers |

You don't need to fill every role. Start with the people you already have — your customer/potential reseller is a natural industry advisor.

### Setting Up the Advisory Board

Minimal formality required:
1. **Define it in the bylaws** — One paragraph stating the board of directors may appoint advisory members who serve at the pleasure of the board and have no voting authority or fiduciary duty.
2. **Send a simple invitation letter** — State the advisory role, that it's non-governing, and what you're asking for (quarterly calls, feedback on programs, introductions).
3. **No annual disclosure required** — But it's good practice to note advisory relationships in your records in case the IRS asks.

### The Key Rule

Advisors advise. The board decides. Never let an advisory opinion substitute for a formal board vote on anything involving money, MedEdPrep transactions, or program direction. Keep the lines clean.

## Governance Documents

### Required Before Filing
1. **Articles of Incorporation** — Filed with Georgia Secretary of State
2. **Bylaws** — Internal governance rules (not filed with the state but required for Form 1023)
3. **Conflict of Interest Policy** — Required for Form 1023, Part V
4. **Organizational meeting minutes** — Document the first board meeting

### Conflict of Interest Policy (Critical for Dual-Entity)
The COI policy is the primary defense against private benefit/inurement claims. It must:

- Require annual written disclosure of ALL financial interests that could present a conflict (especially any interest in MedEdPrep, Company A-E, or The Vault)
- Define "interested person" broadly (directors, officers, key employees, and their family members)
- Establish the "disclose and exclude" protocol: interested person presents the transaction, then leaves the room during deliberation and vote
- Require the disinterested board to determine whether a more advantageous arrangement is available from an unrelated party
- Document the process in board minutes (names present, data relied upon, vote outcome)
- Invoke the **rebuttable presumption of reasonableness** for ALL transactions with related for-profit entities (approved in advance, based on comparable data, documented concurrently)

### Rebuttable Presumption of Reasonableness — How It Works
For EVERY transaction between the 501(c)(3) and MedEdPrep (or any related entity):

1. Independent board members approve the transaction in advance
2. They rely on appropriate comparability data (market rates, competitor pricing, independent appraisals)
3. They document their deliberation and decision concurrently in the minutes
4. **Result:** The burden shifts from the nonprofit to the IRS to prove the transaction wasn't fair

This is your primary defense. Build it into every board meeting where a MedEdPrep transaction is on the agenda.

## Filing Sequence

### Step 1: Georgia Incorporation

| Action | Details | Cost | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choose a name | Must include "Corporation," "Incorporated," "Company," or abbreviation. Check availability with GA SOS. | Free | Same day |
| File Articles of Incorporation | Online through GA SOS Corporations Division. Include IRS-required purpose and dissolution clauses. | $100 (online) | 7 business days (expedite: $100 for 2 days, $250 same day) |
| Publish notice of incorporation | Publish in the legal organ (newspaper) of the county where the registered office is located. | ~$40 | Within 1 day of filing |
| Obtain EIN | Apply online at IRS.gov (Form SS-4) | Free | Immediate |
| File Initial Annual Registration | Due within 90 days of incorporation. Form CD 920. | Free | Within 90 days |
| Hold organizational meeting | Elect officers, adopt bylaws, adopt COI policy, document in minutes | N/A | Before Form 1023 |

### Step 2: Federal 501(c)(3) Application

| Decision | Form 1023 | Form 1023-EZ |
|---|---|---|
| Eligibility | Any organization | Gross receipts <$50K/year (past 3 + projected 3) AND assets <$250K |
| Filing fee | $600 | $275 |
| Processing time | 3-6 months (80% within 191 days) | ~22 days (80% within 22 days) |
| Complexity | Detailed: narrative descriptions, financial statements, governance disclosure | 3 pages, attestation-based |
| For this organization | **Recommended** | Eligible initially, but Form 1023 is safer |

**Recommendation: File Form 1023 (full form), not 1023-EZ.**

Even though the nonprofit will likely qualify for 1023-EZ initially (under $50K receipts, under $250K assets), the full Form 1023 is recommended because:
- The dual-entity structure with related for-profits will raise IRS questions — better to address them proactively
- Form 1023 allows you to fully describe the firewall, COI policy, and arm's length transaction framework
- 1023-EZ has a higher post-approval audit/revocation rate
- The narrative sections let you tell the story of why the nonprofit exists independently

### Step 3: Georgia State Tax Exemption
Georgia provides automatic state income tax exemption for organizations with a federal 501(c)(3) determination letter. No separate state application needed.

### Step 4: Charitable Solicitation Registration
If the nonprofit will solicit donations in Georgia:
- Register with GA Secretary of State using Form C-100
- Filing fee: $35
- Registration valid for 24 months
- Must be renewed and amended within 30 days of any changes

**Exemption note:** Educational institutions and organizations operated in connection with nonprofit educational institutions may be exempt from charitable solicitation registration under O.C.G.A. § 43-17-9. Worth evaluating whether this applies.

## The Firewall: Nonprofit ↔ For-Profit Separation

### What the IRS Will Scrutinize on Form 1023
The IRS will look at:
1. **Who controls the nonprofit?** Board composition, founder influence, overlap with for-profit management
2. **Who benefits from the nonprofit's activities?** If the primary beneficiary is MedEdPrep, that's private benefit
3. **Are transactions at arm's length?** Software purchases from MedEdPrep must be at FMV with independent board approval
4. **Does the nonprofit have independent programs?** Activities that don't involve MedEdPrep at all strengthen independence

### Firewall Checklist

| Requirement | Implementation |
|---|---|
| **Separate legal entity** | Georgia nonprofit corporation, separate EIN, separate articles |
| **Separate bank accounts** | Nonprofit funds never commingled with MedEdPrep accounts |
| **Separate accounting** | Independent bookkeeping, separate financial statements |
| **Independent board majority** | 75%+ independent directors who meet the 4-part test |
| **Conflict of interest policy** | Written, annual disclosure, disclose-and-exclude protocol |
| **Arm's length transactions** | All purchases from MedEdPrep at FMV, approved by independent board with comparability data |
| **Independent programs** | Program 2 (workforce development) and Program 3 (hospice) operate independently of MedEdPrep products |
| **Separate physical presence** | Ideally separate office/mailing address (even if minimal) |
| **No shared employees without allocation** | If any staff works for both, time must be tracked and costs allocated at FMV |

### Transactions That Will Need Board Approval
Every time the nonprofit transacts with MedEdPrep or any related entity:

1. **Software license purchases** — Nonprofit buys MedEdPrep Education access for HS programs at FMV
2. **Shared services** — If any office space, staff, or infrastructure is shared, costs allocated by documented methodology
3. **Data access** — If the nonprofit accesses Company E data for research, terms must be documented
4. **Hospice technology** — Future purchases from Company D at FMV

Each transaction: independent board approval → comparability data → documented in minutes → rebuttable presumption secured.

## Form 1023 Strategy — Key Sections to Get Right

### Part IV: Narrative Description of Activities
This is where you tell the story. The narrative must:
- Describe all three programs in detail with measurable outcomes
- Explain WHY the nonprofit exists independently of MedEdPrep
- Describe the firewall between the nonprofit and the for-profit entities
- Explain how FMV is determined for transactions with related parties
- Demonstrate that public benefit is primary, private benefit is incidental

### Part V: Compensation and Financial Arrangements
- Disclose the founder's role on the board and any compensation (recommend: no compensation for the founder as a board member)
- Disclose ALL relationships between board members and MedEdPrep / related entities
- Describe the COI policy and how it's enforced
- Describe the arm's length pricing methodology

### Part VIII: Revenue (projected)
For a new organization, you'll project 3 years of revenue/expenses:
- Year 1: Minimal — self-funded or small grants, establishing programs
- Year 2: First grant applications, beginning HS program access
- Year 3: Grant revenue growing, program delivery expanding

### Schedules
- **Schedule H** may apply if the organization operates through a related for-profit or has joint ventures
- Financial statements for each year of operation (or projections if <5 years old)
