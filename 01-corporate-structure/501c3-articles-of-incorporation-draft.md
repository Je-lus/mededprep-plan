## ARTICLES OF INCORPORATION OF [FOUNDATION NAME], INC.

The undersigned incorporator, acting pursuant to the Georgia Nonprofit Corporation Code, O.C.G.A. Title 14, Chapter 3, hereby adopts the following Articles of Incorporation for a Georgia nonprofit corporation:

---

### ARTICLE I — NAME

The name of the corporation shall be:

**[FOUNDATION NAME], Inc.**

*Note: The name must include "Corporation," "Incorporated," "Company," or an abbreviation thereof (e.g., "Corp.," "Inc.," "Co.") per O.C.G.A. Section 14-3-401. The name must be distinguishable from any name on file with the Georgia Secretary of State. Check availability at https://ecorp.sos.ga.gov/BusinessSearch before filing.*

---

### ARTICLE II — DURATION

The period of duration of the corporation shall be perpetual.

---

### ARTICLE III — PURPOSE

The corporation is organized exclusively for charitable and educational purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended (or the corresponding provision of any future United States internal revenue law), including but not limited to:

(a) Advancing the quality, accessibility, and equity of emergency medical services education and training;

(b) Promoting public health and safety through workforce development in out-of-hospital care;

(c) Supporting end-of-life and hospice care education and access;

(d) Providing educational tools and resources to underserved communities and educational institutions; and

(e) Engaging in any and all lawful activities incidental to the foregoing purposes.

Notwithstanding any other provision of these Articles, the corporation shall not carry on any activities not permitted to be carried on by a corporation exempt from federal income tax under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended (or the corresponding provision of any future United States internal revenue law).

No part of the net earnings of the corporation shall inure to the benefit of, or be distributable to, its members, trustees, officers, or other private persons, except that the corporation shall be authorized and empowered to pay reasonable compensation for services rendered and to make payments and distributions in furtherance of the purposes set forth herein.

No substantial part of the activities of the corporation shall be the carrying on of propaganda, or otherwise attempting, to influence legislation, and the corporation shall not participate in, or intervene in (including the publishing or distributing of statements) any political campaign on behalf of (or in opposition to) any candidate for public office.

---

### ARTICLE IV — MEMBERS

The corporation shall have no members within the meaning of the Georgia Nonprofit Corporation Code, O.C.G.A. Section 14-3-601. All rights, powers, and privileges that would otherwise vest in members shall vest in the Board of Directors.

---

### ARTICLE V — DISSOLUTION

Upon the dissolution of the corporation, assets shall be distributed for one or more exempt purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended (or the corresponding provision of any future United States internal revenue law), or to the federal government, or to a state or local government, for a public purpose. Any such assets not so disposed of shall be disposed of by a court of competent jurisdiction of the county in which the principal office of the corporation is then located, exclusively for such purposes or to such organization or organizations as said court shall determine which are organized and operated exclusively for such purposes.

---

### ARTICLE IX — INDEMNIFICATION

To the fullest extent permitted by the Georgia Nonprofit Corporation Code, as it exists on the date hereof or as it may hereafter be amended, the corporation shall indemnify any person who was or is a party to, or is threatened to be made a party to, any threatened, pending, or completed action, suit, or proceeding, whether civil, criminal, administrative, or investigative, by reason of the fact that such person is or was a director, officer, employee, or agent of the corporation, or is or was serving at the request of the corporation as a director, officer, employee, or agent of another corporation, partnership, joint venture, trust, or other enterprise.

The corporation may purchase and maintain insurance on behalf of any person who is or was a director, officer, employee, or agent of the corporation against any liability asserted against and incurred by such person in any such capacity, or arising out of such person's status as such, whether or not the corporation would have the power to indemnify such person against such liability under the provisions of the Georgia Nonprofit Corporation Code.

*Note: Indemnification may alternatively be addressed solely in the Bylaws. Including it in the Articles provides stronger protection because Articles take priority over Bylaws and cannot be unilaterally changed by the board without a formal amendment filing with the Secretary of State. For a startup nonprofit recruiting independent directors, the Articles-level indemnification signals commitment to protecting board members.*

---

### ARTICLE X — LIMITATION OF DIRECTOR LIABILITY

To the fullest extent permitted by the Georgia Nonprofit Corporation Code, O.C.G.A. Section 14-3-851, a director of the corporation shall not be personally liable to the corporation for monetary damages for breach of the duty of care or any other duty as a director, except for liability arising from:

(a) Any appropriation, in violation of the director's duties, of any business opportunity of the corporation;

(b) Acts or omissions which involve intentional misconduct or a knowing violation of law;

(c) The types of liability set forth in O.C.G.A. Section 14-3-862 (unlawful distributions); or

(d) Any transaction from which the director derived an improper personal benefit.

If the Georgia Nonprofit Corporation Code is hereafter amended to authorize corporate action further eliminating or limiting the personal liability of directors, the liability of a director of the corporation shall be eliminated or limited to the fullest extent permitted by the Georgia Nonprofit Corporation Code as so amended.

## FILING INSTRUCTIONS

### Step 1: File with the Georgia Secretary of State

**Method:** Online filing through the Georgia Corporations Division
**URL:** https://ecorp.sos.ga.gov/
**Filing Type:** Nonprofit Corporation — Articles of Incorporation
**Filing Fee:** $100 (online)

**Processing Times:**
| Service Level | Fee | Processing Time |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | $100 (included in filing fee) | 7 business days |
| Expedited | $100 additional | 2 business days |
| Same-Day | $250 additional | Same business day (must file by noon) |

**What to file:** Upload or enter the Articles of Incorporation as adopted above. Georgia's online system will prompt you for the required fields (name, registered agent, registered office, incorporator). The purpose and dissolution clauses should be entered in the "additional provisions" or purpose field.

### Step 2: Publish Notice of Incorporation

**Requirement:** O.C.G.A. Section 14-3-202.1 requires publication of a notice of incorporation in the newspaper that is the legal organ of the county where the registered office is located.

**Timing:** The notice must be published once a week for two consecutive weeks, beginning within one business day of the date of incorporation.

**Content of Notice:** The notice must include the name of the corporation, the date of incorporation, and the name of the registered agent and the address of the registered office.

**Cost:** Approximately $40 (varies by county and newspaper).

**How to find the legal organ:** Contact the county clerk's office or the Georgia Secretary of State's office. Each Georgia county has a designated legal organ newspaper. The legal organ for the county where your registered office is located is the only newspaper that will satisfy this requirement.

**Filing proof:** After publication, obtain an affidavit of publication from the newspaper. Retain this in your corporate records. It is not filed with the Secretary of State but may be requested.

### Step 3: Obtain EIN

**Method:** Apply online at https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online
**Cost:** Free
**Processing:** Immediate (EIN assigned at end of online session)
**When:** After the Articles are filed and the corporation exists as a legal entity.
**Note:** On the EIN application, select "Other Nonprofit/Tax-Exempt Organizations" as the entity type.

### Step 4: File Initial Annual Registration

**Form:** CD 920 (Annual Registration of Corporation)
**Due:** Within 90 days of incorporation
**Cost:** Free for the initial filing
**URL:** Filed through the Georgia Secretary of State's Corporations Division

### Step 5: Hold Organizational Meeting

**Before Form 1023 can be filed, the board must hold an organizational meeting to:**
- Elect officers (President, Secretary, Treasurer at minimum)
- Adopt the Bylaws
- Adopt the Conflict of Interest Policy
- Approve any initial programs or transactions
- Document everything in formal meeting minutes

## IRS FORM 1023 REVIEW — WHAT THE IRS LOOKS FOR IN THESE ARTICLES

When reviewing the Articles of Incorporation as part of a Form 1023 application, the IRS examines the following. Every item below is addressed in this draft.

### Organizational Test Requirements (must be in the Articles)

| Requirement | Where in This Draft | What the IRS Checks |
|---|---|---|
| **Exclusive limitation to 501(c)(3) purposes** | Article III, first paragraph | The word "exclusively" must appear. Purposes must be limited to those described in Section 501(c)(3). |
| **Private inurement prohibition** | Article III, third paragraph | The exact IRS-required language prohibiting net earnings from inuring to private persons. |
| **Lobbying restriction** | Article III, fourth paragraph | The exact IRS-required language limiting legislative activity. |
| **Political campaign prohibition** | Article III, fourth paragraph | The exact IRS-required language prohibiting political campaign intervention. |
| **501(c)(3) limitation clause** | Article III, second paragraph | A catch-all clause restricting ALL activities to those permitted under 501(c)(3). |
| **Dissolution clause** | Article V | Assets must be permanently dedicated to exempt purposes upon dissolution. Must reference Section 501(c)(3) or describe distribution to exempt organizations or government. |

### Governance Red Flags the IRS Will Examine

| Factor | What They Look For | This Draft's Position |
|---|---|---|
| **Board size** | Fewer than 3 directors raises concerns | 4 initial directors (Article VII) |
| **Independence** | Majority must be independent of the founder and related for-profits | 3 of 4 directors independent (75%) |
| **Members** | Membership corporations add governance complexity | No members (Article IV) |
| **Founder control** | Single individual should not control the board | Founder is 1 of 4 directors (25%) |

### Dual-Entity Scrutiny

Because this nonprofit has a related for-profit (MedEdPrep, LLC), the IRS will apply heightened scrutiny to:

1. **Purpose clause specificity** — The IRS will confirm the nonprofit has a genuine independent charitable purpose, not merely to benefit MedEdPrep. The program areas in Article III (EMS education access, workforce development, end-of-life care) demonstrate breadth beyond any single for-profit relationship.

2. **Dissolution clause** — Must permanently dedicate assets to exempt purposes. This prevents a founder from dissolving the nonprofit and redirecting assets to the for-profit. Article V satisfies this.

3. **Board composition** — The IRS will check whether the founder or related-party insiders control the board. The 4-director structure with 3 independent directors demonstrates adequate independence.

4. **Private benefit language** — The inurement prohibition and lobbying/political restrictions are boilerplate IRS language and must appear verbatim or substantially identical to the IRS model language. This draft uses the standard IRS language.

## OPTIONAL BUT RECOMMENDED PROVISIONS

### Included in This Draft

**Article IX — Indemnification**
Authorizes the corporation to indemnify directors, officers, employees, and agents to the fullest extent permitted by Georgia law. Also authorizes the purchase of D&O insurance. This provision is not required by the IRS or by Georgia law for incorporation, but it is strongly recommended for recruiting independent directors. Including it in the Articles (rather than only in the Bylaws) provides stronger protection because amending the Articles requires a state filing, preventing the board from quietly weakening indemnification protections.

**Article X — Limitation of Director Liability**
Limits directors' personal liability for monetary damages arising from breach of the duty of care, with exceptions for intentional misconduct, knowing violations of law, improper personal benefit, and unlawful distributions. Georgia law (O.C.G.A. Section 14-3-851) permits this limitation. Combined with the indemnification provision, this creates a strong protective framework for volunteer board members.

### Not Included — Consider for Bylaws

The following provisions are typically addressed in the Bylaws rather than the Articles. They are noted here for completeness:

- **Officer positions and duties** — Define in Bylaws. The Articles do not need to specify officers.
- **Committee structure** — Audit committee (100% independent), compensation committee, and executive committee should be defined in Bylaws.
- **Conflict of Interest Policy** — Adopted as a standalone policy at the organizational meeting, not in the Articles. Required for Form 1023, Part V.
- **Fiscal year** — Typically defined in Bylaws. Most nonprofits use a calendar year (January 1 - December 31) or a fiscal year aligned with their grant cycles.
- **Amendment procedures** — Georgia default rules under O.C.G.A. Section 14-3-1003 apply unless the Articles specify otherwise. The Bylaws should detail the procedure.
- **Advisory board authorization** — The Bylaws should include a provision authorizing the Board of Directors to appoint advisory members who serve at the pleasure of the board and have no voting authority or fiduciary duty.
- **Specific program descriptions** — The Articles state purposes broadly. Specific program details belong in the Form 1023 narrative and board resolutions, not the Articles (to avoid needing to amend the Articles every time programs evolve).
