# Conflict of Interest Policy — 501(c)(3) Nonprofit Corporation

*Working document — 2026-04-06*

This Conflict of Interest Policy satisfies the requirements of IRS Form 1023, Part V, and is designed specifically for a 501(c)(3) nonprofit that operates within a dual-entity structure alongside MedEdPrep, LLC and related for-profit entities.

---

## Article I: Purpose

The purpose of this Conflict of Interest Policy ("Policy") is to protect the Corporation's interest when it is contemplating entering into a transaction or arrangement that might benefit the private interest of a director, officer, or key employee of the Corporation, or might result in a possible excess benefit transaction as defined in Section 4958 of the Internal Revenue Code.

This Policy is intended to supplement, not replace, any applicable state and federal laws governing conflicts of interest applicable to Georgia nonprofit corporations.

This Policy is particularly important because the Corporation operates alongside MedEdPrep, LLC and other entities that share common ownership, control, or founding personnel with MedEdPrep (collectively, "Related Entities"). Transactions between the Corporation and Related Entities require heightened scrutiny to protect the Corporation's tax-exempt status and to ensure that no private inurement or impermissible private benefit occurs.

---

## Article II: Definitions

### Section 2.1 — Interested Person
An "Interested Person" is any director, officer, or key employee of the Corporation, or any member of such person's Family (as defined below), who has a Financial Interest (as defined below).

### Section 2.2 — Key Employee
A "Key Employee" is any person who is in a position to exercise substantial influence over the affairs of the Corporation, whether or not they hold a formal title. This includes, without limitation, the executive director (if any), the chief financial officer, program directors, and any other person so designated by the Board.

### Section 2.3 — Family Member
"Family" means a person's spouse, domestic partner, ancestors, siblings (whole or half-blood), children (natural or adopted), grandchildren, great-grandchildren, and the spouses or domestic partners of siblings, children, grandchildren, and great-grandchildren.

- This definition tracks IRC section 4958(f)(4) and the Form 990 definition of family member

### Section 2.4 — Financial Interest
A person has a "Financial Interest" if the person has, directly or indirectly, through business, investment, or family:

**(a) Compensation Arrangement.** An existing or proposed compensation arrangement (including salary, consulting fees, independent contractor payments, royalties, or fringe benefits) with the Corporation or with any entity or individual with which the Corporation has a transaction or arrangement.

**(b) Ownership or Investment Interest.** An ownership or investment interest in any entity with which the Corporation has a transaction or arrangement. This includes, without limitation, any ownership or investment interest in MedEdPrep, LLC or any Related Entity.

**(c) Potential Ownership or Investment Interest.** A potential ownership or investment interest in any entity with which the Corporation is negotiating a transaction or arrangement.

**(d) Transaction with the Corporation.** Any direct or indirect transaction with the Corporation, including but not limited to: sales, purchases, leases, loans, grants, shared services, data access agreements, or licensing arrangements.

A Financial Interest is not necessarily a conflict of interest. A person who has a Financial Interest may have a conflict of interest only if the Board or appropriate committee decides that a conflict of interest exists, as described in Article IV.

### Section 2.5 — Related Entity
A "Related Entity" is any entity that: (a) shares common ownership, control, or founding personnel with MedEdPrep, LLC; (b) is under the direct or indirect control of any person who is also a director, officer, or key employee of the Corporation; or (c) is identified by the Board from time to time as a Related Entity. As of the date of adoption, Related Entities include but are not limited to:

- MedEdPrep, LLC (the parent holding company)
- Company A — MedEdPrep Education (EMS education and testing platform)
- Company B — MedEdPrep Technology (SaaS/API services)
- Company C — Accrediting body (NCCA accreditation)
- Company D — Hospice entity
- Company E — Data analytics entity (The Vault)
- Any entity subsequently formed or acquired under MedEdPrep common control

The Board shall maintain a current list of Related Entities and update it annually or whenever a new entity is formed.

## Article III: Duty to Disclose

### Section 3.1 — Disclosure Obligation
Each Interested Person shall disclose to the Board (or to the relevant committee) all material facts concerning the Financial Interest, including but not limited to:

- The nature and extent of the Financial Interest
- The relationship to any Related Entity
- The dollar amount or estimated dollar amount involved
- Any comparable data or alternatives known to the Interested Person

The disclosure must be made before the Board or committee takes any action on the transaction or arrangement.

### Section 3.2 — Affirmative Duty
Each director, officer, and key employee has an affirmative and continuing duty to disclose any Financial Interest that arises during their service, even if the interest was not present at the time of the most recent annual disclosure statement.

### Section 3.3 — Annual Written Disclosure
Each director, officer, and key employee shall complete and sign the Annual Disclosure Statement (Appendix A) at the beginning of each fiscal year, and at any time during the year when a new Financial Interest arises. The Annual Disclosure Statement shall require the signer to:

1. Affirm receipt, reading, and understanding of this Policy
2. Agree to comply with this Policy
3. Disclose all Financial Interests, including any relationship to MedEdPrep, LLC or any Related Entity
4. Disclose all positions held in or compensation received from MedEdPrep, LLC or any Related Entity
5. Disclose all Family Members who have a Financial Interest as defined in this Policy

## Article IV: Procedures for Addressing Conflicts

### Section 4.1 — Determining Whether a Conflict Exists
After disclosure of the Financial Interest and all material facts, the Interested Person shall leave the Board or committee meeting while the remaining members discuss and determine whether a conflict of interest exists.

### Section 4.2 — Procedures When a Conflict Exists

**(a) Presentation by Interested Person.** The Interested Person may make a presentation at the Board or committee meeting to provide any information or answer any questions the Board deems relevant. The Interested Person shall answer all questions truthfully and completely.

**(b) Recusal.** After the presentation, the Interested Person shall leave the meeting room. The Interested Person shall not be present during the Board's or committee's deliberation or vote on the transaction or arrangement. The Interested Person shall not attempt to influence the deliberation or vote by any means.

**(c) Investigation of Alternatives.** The Board Chair (or committee chair) shall, if appropriate, appoint a disinterested person or committee to investigate alternatives to the proposed transaction or arrangement.

**(d) Determination.** After exercising due diligence, the Board or committee shall determine whether the Corporation can obtain, with reasonable efforts, a more advantageous transaction or arrangement from a person or entity that would not give rise to a conflict of interest.

**(e) Vote.** If a more advantageous transaction or arrangement is not reasonably possible under circumstances not producing a conflict of interest, the Board or committee shall determine by a majority vote of the disinterested directors whether the transaction or arrangement is in the Corporation's best interest, for its own benefit, and whether it is fair and reasonable. The Board or committee shall make its decision in conformity with the rebuttable presumption procedures described in Article V.

### Section 4.3 — Quorum for Conflicted Transactions
The presence of Interested Persons shall not count toward a quorum for purposes of voting on a conflicted transaction. A quorum of disinterested directors must be present for a valid vote on any transaction involving a conflict of interest.

## Article V: Rebuttable Presumption of Reasonableness

### Section 5.1 — Applicability
The rebuttable presumption of reasonableness protocol shall be invoked for ALL of the following:

1. Any compensation arrangement between the Corporation and any disqualified person (as defined in IRC section 4958(f)(1))
2. Any transaction between the Corporation and MedEdPrep, LLC or any Related Entity
3. Any transaction between the Corporation and any director, officer, key employee, or Family Member thereof
4. Any transaction between the Corporation and any entity in which a director, officer, key employee, or Family Member has a Financial Interest

### Section 5.2 — Three Requirements
To establish the rebuttable presumption of reasonableness under Treasury Regulation 53.4958-6, the Board must satisfy ALL three of the following requirements:

**(a) Advance Approval by Independent Board Members.** The transaction must be approved in advance by members of the Board (or an authorized committee thereof) who:
- Have no conflict of interest with respect to the transaction
- Are not parties to the transaction
- Are not Family Members of any party to the transaction
- Were not under the control of any party to the transaction

**(b) Reliance on Appropriate Comparability Data.** Before making its decision, the independent Board members must obtain and rely upon appropriate comparability data. Appropriate comparability data includes, but is not limited to:
- Compensation surveys compiled by independent firms for functionally comparable positions at similarly situated organizations (for compensation arrangements)
- Current market prices charged by unrelated parties for the same or substantially similar products or services (for purchases from MedEdPrep or Related Entities)
- Written offers from two or more unrelated vendors for substantially similar products or services
- Independent appraisals or valuations
- Published pricing or rate schedules from comparable organizations
- Any other data the Board determines to be relevant and reliable

**(c) Concurrent Documentation.** The Board must document its deliberation and decision concurrently — meaning at the time of the decision or within a reasonable time thereafter (generally within 60 days). The documentation must include:
- The terms of the transaction and the date approved
- The names of the Board members present during the discussion and those who voted
- The comparability data obtained and relied upon, and how the data was obtained
- Any actions taken with respect to consideration of the transaction by anyone who had a conflict of interest
- The specific basis for the Board's determination that the transaction is at fair market value and is in the Corporation's best interest
- A record of each director's vote (for, against, or abstain)

### Section 5.3 — Effect
When all three requirements are met, the burden of proof shifts from the Corporation to the IRS. The IRS must demonstrate by clear and convincing evidence that the transaction exceeded fair market value. Without the presumption, the Corporation bears the burden of proving the transaction was reasonable.

### Section 5.4 — Failure to Follow Protocol
If the rebuttable presumption protocol is not followed for a transaction that falls within Section 5.1, the Board Chair shall note the failure in the minutes and shall cause the transaction to be brought before the Board for ratification at the next regular meeting, following the full protocol. If ratification is not obtained, the Board shall take appropriate corrective action, which may include renegotiating or terminating the arrangement.

## Article VI: MedEdPrep-Specific Transaction Procedures

### Section 6.1 — Scope
This Article applies to every transaction between the Corporation and MedEdPrep, LLC or any Related Entity, regardless of dollar amount. This includes, without limitation:

- **Software license purchases** — purchasing MedEdPrep Education access (test prep, EOPA exams, or other products) for distribution to beneficiary populations
- **Shared services** — any arrangement for shared office space, administrative staff, technology infrastructure, or other services
- **Data access agreements** — any arrangement for the Corporation to access, use, or benefit from data held by any Related Entity (including Company E / The Vault)
- **Hospice technology purchases** — any future purchase of hospice education or technology products from Company D
- **Consulting or professional services** — any engagement of MedEdPrep personnel or Related Entity personnel to provide services to the Corporation
- **In-kind contributions** — any donation of software, services, equipment, or other assets from MedEdPrep or any Related Entity to the Corporation

### Section 6.2 — Required Process for Each Transaction
For every transaction described in Section 6.1, the Board shall:

1. **Receive written disclosure** from every Interested Person (including the founder, if applicable) identifying the nature and extent of their interest in the transaction
2. **Obtain comparability data** demonstrating fair market value, including at least one of: (a) written bids or quotes from unrelated vendors for substantially similar products or services; (b) published pricing or rate schedules; (c) an independent appraisal; or (d) other data the Board deems reliable
3. **Exclude all Interested Persons** from the deliberation and vote
4. **Vote to approve or reject** the transaction by majority vote of disinterested directors, based on a determination that: (a) the price is at or below fair market value; (b) no more advantageous arrangement is reasonably available from an unrelated party; and (c) the transaction serves the Corporation's exempt purposes
5. **Document the decision concurrently** in the Board minutes, including all elements required by Section 5.2(c)

### Section 6.3 — Annual Review of Ongoing Arrangements
Any ongoing or recurring transaction with MedEdPrep or a Related Entity (such as an annual software license or a shared services agreement) shall be reviewed and re-approved by the Board at least annually, following the full procedures described in this Article. The Board shall obtain updated comparability data for each annual review.

### Section 6.4 — Written Agreements
All transactions with MedEdPrep or Related Entities shall be memorialized in written agreements specifying: the parties, the services or products to be provided, the price and payment terms, the term and termination provisions, and the basis for the fair market value determination. These agreements shall be maintained in the Corporation's permanent records.

## Article VII: Violations of the Policy

### Section 7.1 — Investigation
If the Board has reasonable cause to believe that a director, officer, or key employee has failed to disclose an actual or possible conflict of interest, it shall inform the person and afford the person an opportunity to explain the alleged failure to disclose.

### Section 7.2 — Corrective Action
If, after hearing the person's response and conducting any further investigation it deems warranted, the Board determines that the person has in fact failed to disclose an actual or possible conflict of interest, the Board shall take appropriate disciplinary and corrective action. Such action may include, without limitation:

- A formal reprimand
- Removal from any committee assignment
- Removal from the Board (in accordance with the Bylaws)
- Termination of employment (for key employees)
- Reporting to the IRS or other governmental authority, if the violation constitutes an excess benefit transaction
- Seeking restitution or rescission of the transaction

### Section 7.3 — Good Faith
A director, officer, or key employee who makes a good faith disclosure of a potential conflict, and who follows the procedures set forth in this Policy, shall not be subject to disciplinary action solely because the conflict existed.

---

## Article VIII: Annual Statements

### Section 8.1 — Requirement
Each director, officer, and key employee shall annually sign the Annual Disclosure Statement attached as Appendix A. The completed statements shall be provided to the Secretary (or the officer designated by the Board to oversee compliance) no later than January 31 of each year, or within 30 days of assuming a position with the Corporation, whichever is later.

### Section 8.2 — Review
The Board (or a designated committee) shall review all Annual Disclosure Statements and identify any actual or potential conflicts. The review and its findings shall be documented in the Board minutes.

### Section 8.3 — Retention
All Annual Disclosure Statements shall be maintained in the Corporation's permanent records for at least seven (7) years.

---

## Article IX: Periodic Reviews

### Section 9.1 — Annual Compliance Review
To ensure that the Corporation operates in a manner consistent with its exempt purpose and does not engage in activities that could jeopardize its tax-exempt status, the Board shall conduct or cause to be conducted periodic reviews. The periodic reviews shall, at a minimum, include the following:

1. Whether compensation arrangements with all directors, officers, and key employees are reasonable and are the result of arm's length negotiations
2. Whether all transactions with MedEdPrep, LLC or any Related Entity were conducted in accordance with this Policy and the rebuttable presumption protocol
3. Whether all partnerships, joint ventures, or other arrangements with any entity conform to the Corporation's exempt purposes and do not result in impermissible private benefit or inurement
4. Whether the list of Related Entities is current and complete
5. Whether the Corporation's programs serve primarily public rather than private interests

### Section 9.2 — Use of Outside Experts
When conducting periodic reviews, the Corporation may, but need not, use outside advisors. If outside experts are used, their use shall not relieve the Board of its responsibility for ensuring that periodic reviews are conducted.
