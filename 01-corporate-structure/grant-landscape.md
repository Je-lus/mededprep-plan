# Grant Landscape for the 501(c)(3)

*Research date: 2026-04-06*

## Overview

The 501(c)(3)'s ability to fund its programs — free EMS education tools for high school programs, hospice/end-of-life care initiatives — depends on accessing grant funding. This document maps the federal, state, and private funding landscape.

The grant landscape for EMS education is narrower than general healthcare workforce development. There is no single large federal program dedicated to EMS education access. Funding comes from multiple smaller streams that must be pursued in parallel.

## Federal Grant Programs

### HRSA — Bureau of Health Workforce
HRSA awarded over $11.1 billion in grants in FY 2025 across health centers, workforce training, HIV/AIDS, maternal/child health, and rural health.

**EMS-relevant programs:**

| Program | Focus | EMS Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **Emergency Medical Services for Children (EMSC)** | Only federal grant program specifically focused on children in prehospital/hospital emergency systems | Direct EMS relevance but narrow (pediatric focused). Could fund pediatric-specific education content. |
| **Telehealth Network Grant Program (TNGP)** | Rural tele-emergency: tele-stroke, tele-behavioral health, tele-EMS | Could fund technology-based EMS education/training in rural areas |
| **Rural Health Network Development Planning Program (RHNDP)** | One-year planning grants for integrated rural healthcare networks | Could fund planning for rural EMS education delivery |
| **Rural Health Network Development Program (RHND)** | Supports established rural healthcare networks | Requires established network; longer-term opportunity |
| **Rural Health Care Coordination Program (RHCC)** | Care coordination, workforce development in rural areas | Workforce development angle could include EMS training |

**Application info:** [HRSA Apply for a Health Workforce Grant](https://bhw.hrsa.gov/funding/apply-health-workforce-grant)

### Other Federal Sources

- **FEMA — Assistance to Firefighters Grant (AFG):** Historically funds EMS training equipment and programs for fire-based EMS
- **DOL — Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act (WIOA):** Can fund healthcare workforce training through state workforce boards
- **DOE — Career and Technical Education (Perkins V):** Funds CTE programs including EMS pathways in high schools — directly relevant to the HS EMS education mission
- **SAMHSA:** Behavioral health integration into EMS — could fund mental health first aid / crisis intervention training content

### Perkins V (Carl D. Perkins Career and Technical Education Act) — High Relevance

Perkins V is the primary federal funding mechanism for Career and Technical Education in high schools and community colleges. EMS is a recognized CTE pathway. States receive Perkins funding and distribute it to local education agencies.

**Why this matters for the 501(c)(3):** If the nonprofit's mission includes providing free education tools to high school EMS programs, Perkins V funding is the most direct federal pathway. The nonprofit could:
- Partner with state CTE offices to provide supplemental materials
- Apply through local education agencies as a provider of CTE-aligned content
- Support states in meeting Perkins V quality requirements for EMS CTE pathways

**Research needed:** State-by-state Perkins V allocation for EMS CTE pathways, especially Georgia.

## State-Level Funding

### Georgia Trauma Commission (GTC)
The GTC funds EMS education through contracts — GEMSA held a contract for education delivery that ran through approximately November 2025. GTC EMS Committee meeting minutes from August 2025 discussed the contract transition.

**Relevance:** If GEMSA's education contract has ended or is transitioning, there may be an opportunity for the 501(c)(3) to position itself as a provider of EMS education services to the GTC. This requires understanding:
- What services GEMSA provided under the contract
- Whether the GTC is seeking new education partners
- What the RFP or procurement process looks like

**GTC also funds:** AVLS (Automated Vehicle Location System) support, trauma courses, instructor preparedness programs. The 501(c)(3) could pursue multiple GTC-funded program areas.

Source: [GTC EMS Stakeholders FY 2026 Proposed Budget](https://trauma.georgia.gov/document/document/5fy-2026-ems-proposed-pdf/download)

### Other State Sources
- State EMS offices often have education grant programs
- State workforce development boards (WIOA funded)
- State CTE offices (Perkins V funded)
- State rural health offices

## Private Foundation Funding

### Healthcare Education & Workforce
- **Robert Wood Johnson Foundation:** Health workforce development, community health
- **The Hearst Foundations:** Education and health, including healthcare training
- **The Leona M. and Harry B. Helmsley Charitable Trust:** Rural healthcare, telehealth — has funded EMS-related projects

### Hospice / End-of-Life Care
- **The John A. Hartford Foundation:** Aging, palliative care, geriatric training
- **The Fan Fox & Leslie R. Samuels Foundation:** Health and social services including end-of-life
- **The Arthur Vining Davis Foundations:** Hospice and palliative care education
- **Coalition to Transform Advanced Care (C-TAC):** Policy and education around serious illness

### EMS-Specific
- **National EMS Memorial Foundation:** Small grants, specific to EMS
- **Firehouse Subs Public Safety Foundation:** Equipment and training grants for first responders
- **State Farm Good Neighbor grants:** Community safety including EMS

**Research needed:** Systematic search of Foundation Directory Online or Candid for grants matching "EMS education" + "healthcare workforce" + "hospice" + "end-of-life care" keywords.

## Grant Strategy for the 501(c)(3)

### Phase 1: Establish Credibility (Year 1)
- File for 501(c)(3) status
- Begin programs with self-funding or small grants
- Build relationships with state CTE offices and GTC
- Document impact metrics from existing HS EMS program access

### Phase 2: Federal Applications (Years 2-3)
- Apply for Perkins V support through state CTE partnerships
- Pursue HRSA EMSC or TNGP grants
- Build track record with smaller private foundation grants

### Phase 3: Scale (Years 3+)
- Leverage track record for larger federal grants
- Pursue multi-year foundation partnerships
- Explore corporate sponsorships from EMS equipment/device companies

### Key Insight
The 501(c)(3)'s strongest early-stage grant argument is the **high school EMS program access** mission. MedEdPrep already serves high school programs and can document existing impact. The nonprofit just needs to formalize this as a charitable program and apply for funding that supports CTE pathways in health sciences.
