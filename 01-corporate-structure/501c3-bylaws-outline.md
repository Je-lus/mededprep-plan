# Bylaws Outline — Georgia 501(c)(3) Nonprofit Corporation

*Working document — 2026-04-06*

This is a complete bylaws outline for the MedEdPrep ecosystem 501(c)(3). Each article includes the specific Georgia statutory references (O.C.G.A. Title 14, Chapter 3) and IRS requirements that drive the provision. This outline is structured for conversion into final bylaws by legal counsel or by the founder directly.

---

## Article I: Name, Principal Office, and Registered Agent

### Section 1.1 — Name
The name of the corporation shall be [_______________], Inc. (the "Corporation").

- The name must include "Corporation," "Incorporated," "Company," or an abbreviation thereof (O.C.G.A. section 14-3-401(a))
- The name must be distinguishable from any name on file with the Georgia Secretary of State (O.C.G.A. section 14-3-401(b))
- Verify availability at the GA SOS Corporations Division before filing

### Section 1.2 — Principal Office
The principal office of the Corporation shall be located at [address], County of [___], State of Georgia. The Board of Directors may change the principal office by resolution.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-501 — nonprofit corporation may have its principal office inside or outside Georgia
- For firewall purposes, the nonprofit should maintain a separate mailing address from MedEdPrep, LLC, even if minimal (P.O. Box or registered agent address)

### Section 1.3 — Registered Agent and Registered Office
The Corporation shall maintain a registered agent and registered office in Georgia as required by law.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-501 — must continuously maintain a registered office and registered agent in Georgia
- The registered agent must be a Georgia resident individual or a corporation authorized to do business in Georgia
- The registered office address must be a street address (not a P.O. Box)
- If the registered agent or office changes, file a notice with the GA Secretary of State (O.C.G.A. section 14-3-502)

## Article II: Purpose

### Section 2.1 — Exempt Purpose
The Corporation is organized exclusively for charitable and educational purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, including but not limited to: advancing the quality, accessibility, and equity of emergency medical services education and training; promoting public health and safety through workforce development in out-of-hospital care; supporting end-of-life and hospice care education and access; and providing educational tools and resources to underserved communities and educational institutions.

- This language mirrors the Articles of Incorporation (required for IRS organizational test under IRC section 501(c)(3))
- Treasury Regulation 1.501(c)(3)-1(b)(1)(i) — articles must limit purposes to one or more exempt purposes
- The bylaws purpose clause should be consistent with but may be broader than the articles; however, for dual-entity structures, keeping them identical avoids IRS questions

### Section 2.2 — Limitation on Activities
No part of the net earnings of the Corporation shall inure to the benefit of, or be distributable to, its members, directors, officers, or other private persons, except that the Corporation shall be authorized and empowered to pay reasonable compensation for services rendered and to make payments and distributions in furtherance of its exempt purposes.

- IRC section 501(c)(3) — absolute prohibition on private inurement
- IRC section 4958 — intermediate sanctions for excess benefit transactions apply to insiders

### Section 2.3 — Political Activity Restriction
No substantial part of the activities of the Corporation shall consist of carrying on propaganda or otherwise attempting to influence legislation. The Corporation shall not participate in or intervene in any political campaign on behalf of or in opposition to any candidate for public office.

- IRC section 501(c)(3) — prohibition on political campaign activity; limitation on lobbying
- IRC section 501(h) — optional election for expenditure test (if lobbying becomes relevant)

## Article III: Board of Directors

### Section 3.1 — General Powers
The business and affairs of the Corporation shall be managed by or under the direction of the Board of Directors, which may exercise all corporate powers except those reserved to the members (if any) by the Articles of Incorporation or these Bylaws.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-801 — all corporate powers exercised by or under authority of the board
- Georgia law does not require a nonprofit to have members; this Corporation operates without members, making the board self-perpetuating

### Section 3.2 — Number of Directors
The Board shall consist of no fewer than three (3) and no more than nine (9) directors. The initial Board shall consist of [4-5] directors. The exact number within this range shall be fixed by resolution of the Board.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-803 — bylaws must specify the number of directors or the method for determining the number
- Georgia law permits as few as one director, but IRS practical expectation is a minimum of three
- The formation design recommends starting with 4 directors (1 founder + 3 independent) and adding a 5th within the first year
- Upper limit of 9 provides room for growth without requiring a bylaws amendment

### Section 3.3 — Independence Requirement
At all times, at least two-thirds (2/3) of the Board shall consist of directors who are independent. An independent director is one who, during the tax year, meets all four criteria of the IRS Form 990 independence test:

1. Was not compensated as an officer or employee of the Corporation or any related organization
2. Did not receive total compensation exceeding $10,000 as an independent contractor from the Corporation or related organizations
3. Neither the director nor any family member was involved in a transaction with the Corporation reportable on Form 990, Schedule L
4. Neither the director nor any family member was involved in a transaction with a related organization reportable on that entity's Schedule L

For purposes of this section, "related organization" includes MedEdPrep, LLC and all entities under common control or ownership with MedEdPrep.

- IRS Form 990, Part VI — definition of independent voting member
- Independent Sector best practice — 2/3 independence threshold
- This section is not required by Georgia statute but is critical for IRS scrutiny of the dual-entity relationship

### Section 3.4 — Qualifications
Directors need not be residents of Georgia. No person who is an employee of, or who receives compensation exceeding $10,000 annually from, MedEdPrep, LLC or any entity under common control with MedEdPrep shall be eligible for election as an independent director. Family members (spouse, ancestors, siblings, children, and their spouses) of any person who would be disqualified under this section are likewise ineligible for independent director status.

### Section 3.5 — Election and Terms of Office
Directors shall be elected by the Board of Directors at the annual meeting. Each director shall serve a term of three (3) years. Terms shall be staggered into three classes so that approximately one-third of the Board stands for election each year. A director may serve a maximum of three (3) consecutive terms (nine years total). After a one-year absence, a former director may be re-elected.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-805 — terms and staggered terms permitted
- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-806 — staggered terms for directors
- Term limits are not required by Georgia law but are IRS and Independent Sector best practice
- Staggering prevents wholesale board turnover and preserves institutional knowledge

### Section 3.6 — Vacancies
A vacancy on the Board, including a vacancy created by an increase in the number of directors, may be filled by the affirmative vote of a majority of the remaining directors, even if fewer than a quorum. A director elected to fill a vacancy shall serve for the unexpired term of the predecessor.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-810 — board may fill vacancies unless articles provide otherwise

### Section 3.7 — Removal of Directors
A director may be removed with or without cause by a two-thirds (2/3) vote of the remaining directors at a meeting called for that purpose, provided that written notice of the proposed removal is given to all directors at least ten (10) days before the meeting.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-808 — directors of a corporation without members may be removed by the directors
- Requiring 2/3 vote (rather than simple majority) provides governance stability

### Section 3.8 — Resignation
A director may resign at any time by delivering written notice to the Board Chair or Secretary. The resignation is effective upon delivery unless the notice specifies a later effective date.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-807

### Section 3.9 — Regular Meetings
The Board shall hold a minimum of four (4) regular meetings per year, at least quarterly. The annual meeting of the Board shall be held in the first quarter of each fiscal year for the purpose of electing directors and officers, reviewing the annual budget, and conducting other business. Notice of regular meetings is not required if the time and place are fixed by the Board.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-820 — regular meetings may be held without notice if time and place are fixed
- IRS expects evidence of active governance; quarterly meetings is the minimum defensible frequency for an organization with related-party transactions

### Section 3.10 — Special Meetings
Special meetings may be called by the Board Chair, the President, or any two (2) directors. Written notice of special meetings shall be given to each director at least five (5) days before the meeting and shall state the date, time, place, and purpose of the meeting.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-822 — special meetings; notice requirements

### Section 3.11 — Place and Manner of Meetings
Meetings may be held at any place designated by the Board or in the notice of meeting. Any director may participate in a meeting by telephone or video conference, provided all participants can hear each other simultaneously. Participation by such means constitutes presence in person.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-820(b) — permits meetings by any means of communication where all can hear simultaneously

### Section 3.12 — Quorum
A majority of the directors then in office shall constitute a quorum for the transaction of business. If a quorum is present when a vote is taken, the affirmative vote of a majority of directors present shall be the act of the Board, unless a greater vote is required by these Bylaws or by law.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-824 — quorum is a majority unless otherwise specified in articles or bylaws
- For related-party transactions, Article V (Conflict of Interest) imposes additional requirements on who may vote

### Section 3.13 — Voting
Each director shall have one vote. Proxy voting is not permitted. A director who is present at a meeting and abstains from voting is considered present for quorum purposes.

- Prohibiting proxy voting is standard nonprofit practice and ensures directors exercise personal judgment
- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-824

### Section 3.14 — Action Without Meeting
Any action required or permitted to be taken at a Board meeting may be taken without a meeting if every director signs a written consent describing the action taken. The consent shall have the same effect as a unanimous vote and shall be filed with the minutes.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-821 — unanimous written consent in lieu of meeting
- Note: Georgia requires unanimity for action without meeting; this cannot be reduced to a lesser threshold
- For related-party transactions, formal meetings with minutes are strongly preferred over written consent to document the deliberation process

### Section 3.15 — Committees

#### (a) Executive Committee
The Board may designate an Executive Committee of not fewer than two (2) directors. The Executive Committee may exercise the authority of the Board between regular meetings, except that the Executive Committee may not: (i) authorize distributions; (ii) approve or recommend dissolution; (iii) amend the Articles of Incorporation or Bylaws; (iv) fill Board vacancies; (v) approve related-party transactions; or (vi) approve compensation of officers.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-825 — board may create committees with delegated authority, subject to limitations
- Excluding related-party transactions from Executive Committee authority ensures the full Board reviews all MedEdPrep dealings

#### (b) Audit Committee
The Board shall establish an Audit Committee composed entirely of independent directors (as defined in Section 3.3). The Audit Committee shall: (i) recommend the selection of an independent auditor; (ii) review the annual financial statements and audit report; (iii) review internal controls and financial policies; (iv) report findings to the full Board.

- Independent Sector best practice — audit committee should be 100% independent
- Dual-entity framework (from companion research) — 100% independent audit committee recommended

#### (c) Program Committee
The Board may establish a Program Committee to oversee the Corporation's program areas. The Program Committee shall review program effectiveness, evaluate measurable outcomes, and recommend program changes to the full Board.

#### (d) Compensation Committee
The Board may establish a Compensation Committee composed entirely of independent directors. The Compensation Committee shall review and recommend all compensation arrangements for officers and key employees, using appropriate comparability data, to establish the rebuttable presumption of reasonableness under Treasury Regulation 53.4958-6.

- Treasury Regulation 53.4958-6 — rebuttable presumption requires approval by independent body with comparability data
- Dual-entity framework — compensation committee should be 100% independent

#### (e) General Committee Provisions
Each committee shall have two (2) or more directors. The Board shall appoint committee members and may designate a committee chair. Committees shall keep regular minutes and report to the Board. Committee members serve at the pleasure of the Board.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-825

### Section 3.16 — Compensation of Directors
Directors shall not receive compensation for their service as directors. Directors may be reimbursed for reasonable expenses incurred in the performance of their duties, upon approval by the Board.

- IRS Form 1023, Part V — compensation of directors must be disclosed
- For the dual-entity structure, unpaid directors eliminate one vector for private inurement claims
- Reasonable expense reimbursement is permitted and expected

## Article IV: Officers

### Section 4.1 — Officers Designated
The officers of the Corporation shall be a President, a Secretary, and a Treasurer. The Board may also appoint a Vice President and such other officers as it deems necessary. Any two offices may be held by the same person, except that the President may not also serve as Secretary.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-840 — corporation shall have the officers described in its bylaws
- Georgia law does not prescribe specific officer titles; these are set by the bylaws
- Separating President and Secretary ensures independent attestation of board minutes and corporate records

### Section 4.2 — Election and Term of Office
Officers shall be elected by the Board at the annual meeting. Each officer shall hold office until the next annual meeting and until a successor is elected and qualified, or until earlier resignation or removal.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-840

### Section 4.3 — Removal
Any officer may be removed by the Board at any time with or without cause. Removal does not affect any contractual rights of the removed officer.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-843

### Section 4.4 — President
The President shall be the principal executive officer of the Corporation and shall, subject to the control of the Board: (a) supervise and control the affairs and programs of the Corporation; (b) preside at all meetings of the Board unless a separate Board Chair is designated; (c) sign, together with the Secretary, all instruments requiring execution on behalf of the Corporation; (d) perform all duties incident to the office and such other duties as assigned by the Board.

- The President should NOT be the founder if the founder serves on the Board. The formation design recommends the founder NOT serve as Board Chair; the same principle applies to the President role. An independent officer heading the organization strengthens the governance narrative for Form 1023.

### Section 4.5 — Secretary
The Secretary shall: (a) keep or cause to be kept the minutes of all Board meetings and committee meetings; (b) ensure that all required notices are given; (c) maintain the corporate records and the seal (if any); (d) maintain a register of the names and addresses of all directors and officers; (e) authenticate records of the Corporation when required; (f) perform all duties incident to the office and such other duties as assigned by the Board.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-840
- Accurate minutes are critical for the rebuttable presumption of reasonableness — the Secretary's role in documenting deliberations on related-party transactions is a compliance function

### Section 4.6 — Treasurer
The Treasurer shall: (a) have charge and custody of all funds and securities of the Corporation; (b) receive and give receipts for monies due and payable to the Corporation; (c) deposit all funds in the name of the Corporation in banks or depositories selected by the Board; (d) keep accurate books and records of all receipts and disbursements; (e) present financial reports to the Board at each regular meeting; (f) prepare or oversee preparation of all required financial filings (Form 990, state filings); (g) perform all duties incident to the office and such other duties as assigned by the Board.

- The Treasurer role is essential for maintaining the financial firewall — nonprofit funds must never be commingled with MedEdPrep accounts
- The Treasurer should ideally be an independent director with financial expertise (Seat 3 from the formation design)

### Section 4.7 — Compensation of Officers
Officers may receive reasonable compensation for services rendered, as determined by the Board (or the Compensation Committee, if established) using appropriate comparability data in accordance with the rebuttable presumption procedures set forth in Article V.

- IRC section 4958 — excess benefit transaction rules apply to officer compensation
- Treasury Regulation 53.4958-6 — rebuttable presumption protocol
- For a startup nonprofit, the formation design recommends no compensation initially; this section preserves the option for future use

## Article V: Conflict of Interest

### Section 5.1 — Purpose
The purpose of the Conflict of Interest Policy is to protect the Corporation's tax-exempt status by ensuring that all transactions involving potential conflicts are conducted at arm's length and in the Corporation's best interest. This is especially critical given the Corporation's relationship to MedEdPrep, LLC and related entities.

- IRS Form 1023, Part V, Line 5a — requires a conflict of interest policy
- The full Conflict of Interest Policy is maintained as a separate document adopted by the Board (see 501c3-coi-policy.md). This Article incorporates its key provisions into the Bylaws.

### Section 5.2 — Interested Persons
An "interested person" is any director, officer, or key employee of the Corporation, or any family member of such person, who has a direct or indirect financial interest in a transaction or arrangement under consideration by the Corporation. This includes, without limitation, any ownership interest, investment, compensation arrangement, or potential compensation arrangement with MedEdPrep, LLC or any entity under common control or ownership with MedEdPrep.

### Section 5.3 — Duty to Disclose
Each interested person shall disclose to the Board all material facts relating to the financial interest, including the nature and extent of the interest and any relevant relationship with MedEdPrep or related entities, before the Board acts on any transaction or arrangement that may involve a conflict.

### Section 5.4 — Procedures for Addressing Conflicts
When a potential conflict is identified:

1. The interested person may make a presentation to the Board regarding the transaction
2. The interested person shall leave the meeting room during the Board's deliberation and vote on the transaction
3. The disinterested members of the Board shall determine whether a more advantageous transaction or arrangement is available from an unrelated party
4. If no more advantageous arrangement is reasonably available, the disinterested Board members shall determine by majority vote whether the transaction is in the Corporation's best interest and is fair and reasonable
5. The Board shall document in the minutes: the names of all persons present, the comparability data relied upon, the deliberation, the vote (including the names of those voting and how each voted), and any recusals

### Section 5.5 — Rebuttable Presumption of Reasonableness
For ALL transactions between the Corporation and MedEdPrep, LLC (or any entity under common control or ownership with MedEdPrep, or any disqualified person as defined in IRC section 4958), the Board shall invoke the rebuttable presumption of reasonableness by ensuring:

1. The transaction is approved in advance by members of the Board who have no conflict of interest with respect to the transaction
2. The Board obtains and relies upon appropriate comparability data (market rates, competitor pricing, independent appraisals, or similar evidence of fair market value)
3. The Board documents its deliberation and decision concurrently in the minutes, including the comparability data reviewed and the basis for the Board's determination

When these three conditions are met, the burden of proof shifts from the Corporation to the IRS to demonstrate that the transaction was not at fair market value.

- Treasury Regulation 53.4958-6 — the three requirements for the rebuttable presumption
- This is the Corporation's primary defense mechanism for related-party transactions

### Section 5.6 — Annual Disclosure
Each director, officer, and key employee shall annually sign a written statement that: (a) affirms they have received a copy of the Conflict of Interest Policy; (b) affirms they have read and understand the Policy; (c) agrees to comply with the Policy; and (d) discloses all financial interests that could give rise to a conflict, including any interest in MedEdPrep, LLC or related entities.

### Section 5.7 — Violations
If the Board has reasonable cause to believe a person has failed to disclose an actual or possible conflict of interest, it shall inform the person of the basis for such belief and afford an opportunity to explain. If the Board determines that a failure to disclose has occurred, it shall take appropriate corrective action, which may include removal from the Board or termination of employment.

### Section 5.8 — Records
The minutes of all Board and committee meetings shall contain: (a) the names of persons who disclosed or were found to have a financial interest; (b) the nature of the financial interest; (c) any action taken to determine whether a conflict existed; (d) the Board's decision as to whether a conflict of interest in fact existed; (e) the names of persons present for the discussion and vote; (f) the content of the discussion; (g) the vote, including a record of each director's vote; and (h) the comparability data relied upon.

## Article VI: Advisory Board

### Section 6.1 — Establishment
The Board of Directors may appoint an Advisory Board consisting of individuals whose expertise, connections, or perspective would benefit the Corporation's mission. The Advisory Board is purely advisory and has no governing authority.

### Section 6.2 — Role and Limitations
Advisory Board members shall have no fiduciary duty to the Corporation, no voting authority, and no power to bind the Corporation. Advisory Board members shall not be referred to as "directors" or "board members" in any official capacity. The Advisory Board advises; the Board of Directors decides.

### Section 6.3 — Appointment and Removal
Advisory Board members shall be appointed and may be removed at any time at the discretion of the Board of Directors. There is no fixed number of Advisory Board members, and no term limits apply.

### Section 6.4 — Meetings
The Advisory Board may meet at the call of the Board Chair or President. Advisory Board meetings are informal and are not subject to quorum requirements, notice requirements, or minute-keeping obligations of the Board of Directors. However, the Corporation should maintain records of Advisory Board meetings as a matter of good practice.

### Section 6.5 — Compensation
Advisory Board members may receive reimbursement for reasonable expenses. Any other compensation arrangement must be approved by the Board of Directors in accordance with the Conflict of Interest Policy.

### Section 6.6 — Conflicts
Although Advisory Board members are not bound by the Conflict of Interest Policy, they are encouraged to disclose financial interests relevant to any matter on which they provide advice. The Board of Directors should note in its own minutes when advice from an Advisory Board member may be influenced by a financial interest in MedEdPrep or a related entity.

## Article VII: Financial Matters

### Section 7.1 — Fiscal Year
The fiscal year of the Corporation shall end on December 31 of each year.

- The fiscal year must be stated in the Articles or Bylaws and reported on Form 1023
- Calendar year is simplest for a new organization and aligns with most grant reporting cycles

### Section 7.2 — Books and Records
The Corporation shall maintain correct and complete books and records of account, minutes of all Board and committee proceedings, and a record of all directors and officers with their addresses. Any director may inspect all books and records for any proper purpose at any reasonable time.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-1602 — right of directors to inspect records
- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-1601 — records the corporation must keep

### Section 7.3 — Financial Statements and Audit
The Corporation shall prepare annual financial statements in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP). When the Corporation's annual gross receipts exceed $500,000, the Board shall cause an independent audit to be conducted by a certified public accountant. Below that threshold, the Board may authorize a review or compilation engagement. The Audit Committee shall oversee the audit process.

- IRS Form 990, Part XII — requires description of financial statement preparation and audit
- OMB Uniform Guidance (2 CFR 200.501) — if federal grants exceed $750,000, a single audit is required
- Starting the audit practice early (even if not yet required) builds credibility with grantors

### Section 7.4 — Separate Accounts
The Corporation shall maintain its own bank accounts, separate from those of MedEdPrep, LLC or any other entity. Corporate funds shall not be commingled with the funds of any other person or entity.

- This is a firewall requirement from the formation design, not a statutory requirement, but essential for IRS compliance

### Section 7.5 — Check Signing Authority
Disbursements exceeding [___] dollars ($[___]) shall require the signatures of two authorized persons. The Board shall designate by resolution which officers and other persons are authorized to sign checks, drafts, and other orders for payment of money on behalf of the Corporation.

- Dual-signature requirements for larger amounts are a standard internal control
- Recommended threshold: $2,500 or $5,000 for dual signature, to be set by Board resolution

### Section 7.6 — Contracts and Instruments
The Board may authorize any officer or agent to enter into contracts or execute instruments on behalf of the Corporation. Such authority may be general or limited to specific instances.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-302 — general powers of nonprofit corporations

### Section 7.7 — Investment Policy
The Corporation may invest its funds in accordance with an investment policy adopted by the Board. All investments shall comply with the Georgia Uniform Prudent Management of Institutional Funds Act (O.C.G.A. sections 44-15-1 through 44-15-9, UPMIFA) and shall prioritize preservation of capital, liquidity sufficient to meet operational needs, and compliance with any donor restrictions.

- O.C.G.A. sections 44-15-1 through 44-15-9 — Georgia UPMIFA
- For a startup nonprofit, a formal investment policy is not urgent but should be adopted when reserves exceed $50,000

### Section 7.8 — Charitable Contributions
All gifts, bequests, and devises to the Corporation shall be accepted or rejected by the Board or its designee. The Corporation shall not accept any gift that would create a conflict of interest or would be inconsistent with its exempt purpose.

### Section 7.9 — Loans to Directors and Officers Prohibited
The Corporation shall not make any loan to a director or officer. Any director who votes for or assents to the making of such a loan shall be jointly and severally liable for the amount of the loan until it is repaid.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-832 — prohibits loans to directors unless approved by members (this Corporation has no members, so loans are effectively prohibited)

## Article VIII: Indemnification

### Section 8.1 — Indemnification of Directors and Officers
The Corporation shall indemnify any person who was or is a party, or is threatened to be made a party, to any threatened, pending, or completed action, suit, or proceeding (whether civil, criminal, administrative, or investigative) by reason of the fact that such person is or was a director or officer of the Corporation, against expenses (including attorneys' fees), judgments, fines, and amounts paid in settlement actually and reasonably incurred in connection with such action, to the fullest extent permitted by the Georgia Nonprofit Corporation Code.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-851 — mandatory indemnification when the director is wholly successful
- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-852 — permissive indemnification (requires good faith and reasonable belief that conduct was in the corporation's best interest)
- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-856 — indemnification provisions in articles or bylaws
- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-857 — corporation may purchase D&O insurance

### Section 8.2 — Advance of Expenses
Expenses incurred in defending an action may be paid by the Corporation in advance of the final disposition of such action, upon receipt of an undertaking by or on behalf of the director or officer to repay such amount if it is ultimately determined that the person is not entitled to indemnification.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-853

### Section 8.3 — Insurance
The Corporation may purchase and maintain directors and officers (D&O) liability insurance on behalf of any person who is or was a director or officer, against any liability asserted against and incurred by such person in any such capacity, whether or not the Corporation would have the power to indemnify such person against such liability.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-857
- D&O insurance is strongly recommended, especially given the related-party transaction environment

### Section 8.4 — Non-Exclusivity
The indemnification provided by this Article shall not be exclusive of any other rights to which those indemnified may be entitled under any agreement, vote of the Board, or otherwise.

## Article IX: Amendment of Bylaws

### Section 9.1 — Authority to Amend
These Bylaws may be amended or repealed, and new bylaws adopted, by the affirmative vote of two-thirds (2/3) of the directors then in office at any regular or special meeting of the Board, provided that:

1. Written notice of the proposed amendment is given to all directors at least ten (10) days before the meeting
2. The notice states the text or substance of the proposed amendment
3. No amendment shall be made that would cause the Corporation to cease to qualify as a tax-exempt organization under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-1020 — board may amend bylaws unless articles reserve that power to members
- Requiring 2/3 vote (rather than simple majority) provides stability and prevents hasty changes
- The restriction in item 3 protects tax-exempt status from inadvertent bylaw changes

### Section 9.2 — Record of Amendments
All amendments to these Bylaws shall be recorded and maintained with the corporate records, including the date of adoption and the vote.

## Article X: Dissolution

### Section 10.1 — Procedure
The Corporation may be dissolved by the affirmative vote of two-thirds (2/3) of the directors then in office, subject to compliance with O.C.G.A. sections 14-3-1401 through 14-3-1409.

- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-1402 — dissolution by board of directors (for corporations without members)
- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-1405 — articles of dissolution to be filed with GA SOS
- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-1406 — known claims against dissolved corporation
- O.C.G.A. section 14-3-1407 — unknown claims

### Section 10.2 — Distribution of Assets
Upon the dissolution of the Corporation, assets shall be distributed for one or more exempt purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, or to the federal government, or to a state or local government, for a public purpose. No part of the net earnings of the Corporation shall inure to the benefit of, or be distributable to, its members, trustees, officers, or other private persons.

- This language must exactly match the dissolution clause in the Articles of Incorporation
- IRC section 501(c)(3) — assets must be permanently dedicated to exempt purposes
- Treasury Regulation 1.501(c)(3)-1(b)(4) — the dedication of assets requirement
- This clause is reviewed by the IRS on Form 1023 and must be present in both the Articles and the Bylaws

### Section 10.3 — Notification
Upon dissolution, the Corporation shall notify the Georgia Secretary of State, the Internal Revenue Service, the Georgia Department of Revenue, and any other government agency as required by law.

## Article XI: Miscellaneous

### Section 11.1 — Nondiscrimination
The Corporation shall not discriminate on the basis of race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age, disability, or any other protected characteristic in its programs, activities, or governance.

### Section 11.2 — Parliamentary Authority
In matters not covered by these Bylaws or by law, the proceedings of the Board shall be governed by the most recent edition of Robert's Rules of Order, Newly Revised.

### Section 11.3 — Severability
If any provision of these Bylaws is held to be invalid or unenforceable, the remaining provisions shall continue in full force and effect.

### Section 11.4 — Adoption
These Bylaws were adopted by the Board of Directors at the organizational meeting held on [DATE].
