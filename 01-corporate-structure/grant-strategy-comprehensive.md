## The Public Charity Problem This Strategy Must Solve

The 501(c)(3)'s entire transaction model — purchasing MedEdPrep software at FMV and providing free access to beneficiary populations — depends on public charity classification. If the nonprofit fails the one-third public support test, it becomes a private foundation, and Section 4941 self-dealing rules prohibit ALL transactions with MedEdPrep regardless of FMV.

**What counts toward public support:** Government grants, foundation grants, broad-based public donations (individual donations count but are limited to 2% of total support per donor).

**What does NOT count:** Contributions from the founder, MedEdPrep, or any entity the founder controls. MedEdPrep donating 10% of proceeds to the nonprofit actively hurts the ratio.

**The math:** If the nonprofit receives $50K total in its first reporting period, at least ~$17K must come from qualifying public sources (grants, diverse donors) to meet the one-third test. The IRS evaluates this over a rolling 5-year period with some flexibility for new organizations, but the trajectory matters.

**This strategy is designed to ensure grant revenue materializes fast enough to maintain public charity status.**

## Critical First Step: Federal Registration

Before applying for ANY federal grant, the nonprofit must complete:

1. **EIN** — Apply at IRS.gov (immediate, done during incorporation)
2. **SAM.gov registration** — Required for ALL federal funding. Takes 4-8 weeks. **Start this the day you get the EIN.**
3. **Grants.gov account** — Can register 1 day after SAM submission. Takes 7-10 business days.
4. **HRSA Electronic Handbooks (EHB)** — Required for HRSA-specific grants.

**Set Grants.gov keyword alerts for:** "rural health network," "EMS," "telehealth network," "health workforce," "emergency medical services," "career technical education"

## Funding Sources by Timeline

### IMMEDIATE (Months 1-6 after incorporation)

#### 1. Firehouse Subs Public Safety Foundation
- **Amount:** $15,000-$25,000
- **Probability:** High
- **What to request:** Training manikins, AED trainers, curriculum materials for high school EMS programs
- **Application:** Digital, grants.firehousesubs.com. Max 600 apps/quarter, fills fast.
- **Quarterly windows (10 AM ET):** Q4 2026: July 9, 2026
- **Must be within 60 miles of a Firehouse Subs** (not an issue in Georgia)
- **3-year reapplication wait** after approval
- **Action:** Apply in the first quarter after incorporation

#### 2. AHA Heart Club Partnerships
- **Amount:** In-kind CPR training kits + $500/school
- **Probability:** High
- **What:** Help high schools establish Heart Clubs as pipeline into EMS programs. AHA provides free CPR in Schools Kits (manikins, AED simulators, materials).
- **Action:** Contact AHA Georgia regional office immediately

#### 3. Hero Fund America
- **Amount:** Up to $1,500
- **Probability:** High
- **Application:** June 2026 (annual First Responders Grant Round)
- **Value:** Small dollars but builds grant track record

#### 4. Georgia's Own Foundation
- **Amount:** Under $5,000 (small grants)
- **Probability:** Moderate
- **Application:** Spring 2026 cycle
- **Action:** Apply as soon as 501(c)(3) status is pending

#### 5. Laerdal Medical — Corporate Partnership
- **Amount:** In-kind (training equipment donations/discounts)
- **Probability:** High for product partnerships
- **Why:** Laerdal manufactures the exact training tools the nonprofit needs. Strong mission alignment ("helping save lives"). Most natural corporate partner.
- **Action:** Outreach to Laerdal corporate social responsibility team. Propose co-branded initiative for high school EMS programs.

#### 6. ZOLL Medical — Equipment Partnership
- **Amount:** In-kind (AED trainers, equipment)
- **Probability:** Moderate
- **Why:** ZOLL has a model of partnering with nonprofits on AED distribution (e.g., RCX Sports Foundation). Propose similar model for high school EMS programs.

### SHORT-TERM (Months 6-12)

#### 7. HRSA Rural Health Network Development Planning Program (RHNDP)
- **Amount:** $100,000 (one year)
- **Probability:** High — this program is literally designed for new organizations building networks
- **What:** Plan a network connecting the nonprofit with rural Georgia EMS agencies, high schools, community health centers
- **Eligibility:** Public or private nonprofit. Must form network of 3+ entities with at least one in HRSA-designated rural area. New organizations welcome.
- **Next cycle:** Expected late 2026/early 2027 (HRSA-26-XXX). Most recent deadline was February 2025.
- **Action:** Begin building consortium partnerships NOW (rural EMS agencies, high schools, community health centers). Have letters of commitment ready when the NOFO drops.

#### ~~8. Georgia Trauma Commission (GTC)~~ — NOT A GRANT TARGET
- GEMSA lost the education contract (confirmed). GTC is not interested in funding the nonprofit.
- **However:** MedEdPrep is already a GTC vendor — grant-funded EMS programs in Georgia are mandated to use MedEdPrep for testing. This is a powerful credibility point for all other grant applications, not a direct funding source.
- The GTC vendor relationship also supports the FMV argument when the nonprofit purchases MedEdPrep access — there is a documented market price.

#### 9. Kaiser Permanente Georgia
- **Amount:** $5,000-$25,000
- **Probability:** Moderate-High
- **Why:** Kaiser's Community Health Careers initiative specifically targets exposing diverse/disadvantaged students to healthcare professions — direct alignment with high school EMS education mission.
- **Action:** Start with sponsorship request for a specific program. Build relationship before formal grant application.

#### 10. Community Foundation for Greater Atlanta
- **Amount:** $5,000-$50,000
- **Probability:** Moderate (if Atlanta-area based)
- **Application:** Annual cycle — 2026 window closed February 27. Watch for 2027 window (~January).
- **$1.4B in assets** — one of the top 20 community foundations nationally.

#### 11. CTAE / Perkins V — District Vendor Pathway
- **Amount:** $60K-$600K/year statewide (vendor revenue to MedEdPrep, not grants to the nonprofit)
- **Probability:** High — this is a sales/partnership play, not a grant application
- **How it works:** School districts use Perkins V funds to purchase MedEdPrep tools. The nonprofit facilitates access for underserved programs.
- **Critical timing:** Districts make spending decisions March-April for upcoming fiscal year. CLNA update cycle (every 2 years) is the key window.
- **Action items:**
  1. Request list of districts with EMR/EMT pathways from GaDOE (CTAEprograms@gadoe.org)
  2. Build "Perkins-eligible" positioning sheet mapping products to Section 135 allowable uses
  3. Create one-pager for CTAE coordinators showing how to use Perkins funds for MedEdPrep
  4. Target 5-10 pilot districts
- **Note:** This is MedEdPrep vendor revenue, not nonprofit grant revenue. It doesn't directly help the public support test, but it demonstrates the ecosystem the nonprofit operates in.

### MEDIUM-TERM (Year 2, with documented outcomes)

#### 12. HRSA Rural Health Care Services Outreach Program
- **Amount:** $250,000-$300,000/year for 4 years
- **Probability:** Medium-High with consortium partners
- **Requires:** Consortium of 3+ healthcare providers, 66% in HRSA-designated rural areas. Must consult Georgia State Office of Rural Health.
- **Best after:** RHNDP planning year provides track record
- **Next cycle:** Expected late 2026/early 2027

#### 13. HRSA Rural Health Network Development Program (RHND)
- **Amount:** $300,000/year for 4 years
- **Probability:** Medium — better with RHNDP track record
- **Not posted annually** — last cycle was 2023

#### 14. Georgia Health Foundation
- **Amount:** $10,000-$30,000
- **Probability:** Moderate-High with Year 1 outcomes
- **Critical:** Does NOT fund startups. Explicitly requires proven track record. Target for Year 2.
- **Deadline:** June 1 annually

#### 15. RWJF — Exploring Equitable Futures
- **Amount:** $50,000+
- **Probability:** Moderate — explicitly seeks unconventional ideas
- **Annual cycle:** Closes ~October 15, reopens ~February 1
- **Frame as:** Innovative, tech-enabled model for closing health equity gaps in EMS workforce pipeline

#### 16. WIOA — Local Workforce Board Contracts
- **Amount:** $50,000-$500,000
- **Probability:** Moderate — relationship-driven
- **How:** Partner with Georgia local workforce development boards. Healthcare is a state priority sector.
- **Path:** Get on the Eligible Training Provider List (ETPL) through TCSG, or contract directly with a local board
- **Requires:** Full training programs (not just test prep) leading to recognized credentials

#### 17. FEMA AFG — As Vendor to Fire/EMS Departments
- **Amount:** Indirect — fire departments include MedEdPrep tools in their AFG training grant budgets
- **Probability:** Moderate
- **Action:** Help Georgia fire departments write AFG applications that include the nonprofit's training products as a line item

## Strategic Framing for All Grant Applications

Across all funders, the strongest language positions the nonprofit as:

**"Healthcare workforce pipeline + health equity"** — not just "EMS training"

Key phrases that resonate with funders:
- "Removing financial barriers to healthcare career entry"
- "Diversifying the EMS workforce pipeline"
- "Rural healthcare workforce shortage"
- "Health equity in emergency care"
- "Career and Technical Education pathways to healthcare"
- "Technology-enabled education access"

The EMS education program should lead all Year 1-2 grant seeking. The hospice/end-of-life care program area has fewer accessible funders early on — the major palliative care foundations are either invitation-only, geographically restricted, or fund through intermediaries. Build the hospice program on the EMS program's credibility, and pursue hospice-specific funding once the organization has 2+ years of track record.
