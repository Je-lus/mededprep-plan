⚠ Critical Finding: Georgia O.C.G.A. § 48-7-28.3 — The Addback Statute
Georgia requires operating companies to add back royalty payments made to related entities (defined as 50%+ common ownership) on their Georgia income tax returns. Wyoming has no state income tax, so the “taxed elsewhere” exception does NOT apply. The Vault structure produces zero Georgia state tax benefit for the operating companies. This is by design — Georgia enacted this statute to prevent the “Delaware IP holding company” strategy used in the early 2000s.
What The Vault Still Provides
  • Asset protection: IP is legally separated from operational liability. OpCo litigation cannot reach The Vault’s IP assets.
  • Centralized IP management: Clean licensing architecture for multiple operating companies and future franchisees.
  • Transactional agility: Clean IP portfolio simplifies due diligence for fundraising, M&A, or third-party licensing.
  • Federal tax structure: Intercompany royalties still affect federal taxable income allocation between entities.
  • Future flexibility: Royalty deductions become valuable when OpCos expand to states without addback statutes.

◆ Transfer PricingSection 482 arm’s length — phased royalty ramp ▾
Rate Correction
Some documents reference a flat 8–15% rate. The correct approach is a three-phase ramp. Charging full royalties when an OpCo has minimal revenue creates persistent losses — a red flag the IRS explicitly identifies as triggering audit scrutiny. The Commensurate with Income (CWI) standard under Treas. Reg. 1.482-4(f)(2) contemplates that royalty rates should reflect actual economic results.
Three-Phase Royalty Ramp
Phase Revenue Threshold Rate Rationale
Phase 1 — Launch Pre-revenue or <$50K/yr 0% or cost-recovery Independent licensee would negotiate a royalty holiday. Persistent losses trigger IRS scrutiny. Document as “launch period” clause with objective revenue threshold trigger.
Phase 2 — Growth $50K–$500K annual revenue 5–8% net revenue OpCo retains margin to reinvest. IP unproven in specific market. OpCo bears disproportionate launch risk. CWI contemplates upward adjustment as income materializes.
Phase 3 — Mature $500K+ annual revenue 10–15% net revenue Falls within interquartile range for SaaS/software licensing (SEC EDGAR benchmarks). IP has demonstrated income-generating capacity. Entity-specific rates apply.

Primary TP Method: CPM + CUT Corroboration
Comparable Profits Method (CPM) is the primary method. Comparable Uncontrolled Transaction (CUT) serves as a corroborating check. CUT requires finding highly comparable third-party educational question-bank license agreements (scarce in EDGAR). CPM benchmarks the OpCo’s operating margin against independent SaaS distributors; residual profit flows to The Vault as implied royalty. Using CPM proactively puts the analysis on the same footing the IRS would use in an audit, reducing the attack surface.
“Lite” TP Study Timeline
Formal benchmark study (RoyaltyStat / EDGAR database access) is warranted at $500K revenue. At current scale, a contemporaneous “lite” TP memo — documenting economic rationale, phase-in logic, and method selection — is sufficient for penalty protection under Treas. Reg. § 1.6662-6. Annual review is required: the CWI standard requires royalties to be commensurate with actual income. If Company A unexpectedly generates $2M in Year 1, the Phase 1 rate must be replaced by Phase 3 rates.

Founder owns The Vault directly (once formed). No trust layer yet. Revocable trust available now for probate avoidance ($1,000–$3,000 legal cost) — adds estate planning value without changing tax treatment. Revocable trust provides zero additional creditor protection: trust property remains reachable by the settlor’s creditors.
Revocable Living Trust $100K–$250K Revenue
Place The Vault into a revocable living trust. Provides probate avoidance and continuity of control at incapacity/death. Founder remains trustee during life; names successor trustee. Tax treatment unchanged — grantor trust, founder reports all income. One-time cost: ~$1,000–$3,000 legal drafting.
Wyoming DAPT (Irrevocable) $1M–$2M Entity Value
Convert to irrevocable Wyoming Domestic Asset Protection Trust. Self-settled DAPTs allowed by Wyoming statute. 2-year statute of limitations for post-transfer creditors (shortest alongside Nevada). No state income tax on trust income. Pairs naturally with Wyoming-domiciled Vault. One-time planning cost: $7,500–$25,000. Annual compliance: $1,000–$6,000 (Form 1041, fiduciary accounting).
⚠ DAPT Limitations
  • Must be funded well before any creditor claims exist — fraudulent transfer risk is real
  • Federal bankruptcy 10-year lookback for self-settled trusts (actual intent to hinder/defraud standard)
  • Wyoming: 2-year SOL for post-transfer creditors; longer for pre-existing claims under fraudulent transfer law
  • Cannot retain full managerial control as trustee; need independent or co-trustee per Wyoming statute
  • Rev. Rul. 2023-2: generally no §1014 basis step-up at death if trust assets are not included in gross estate

◆ The IP TransferSection 351 tax-free capitalization ▾

The transfer of IP from Company A (MedEdPrep LLC) into The Vault (Wyoming LLC) must be structured as a Section 351 exchange to avoid triggering immediate tax liability on built-in gain. Internally developed software now carries a capitalized basis under Section 174 (mandatory 5-year amortization for domestic R&D post-TCJA 2017) — this basis absorbs assumed liabilities and reduces Section 357(c) exposure.

Four Section 351 Requirements
1
All Substantial Rights
Must transfer ALL rights, title, and interest — not a partial license. IRS Revenue Ruling 69-156: transfer must be equivalent to a sale or exchange to qualify as “property” under Section 351. Retaining any right to license IP to third parties may cause the transfer to be treated as a taxable license at ordinary income rates.
2
80% Control — Immediately After
Founder must own ≥80% of total voting power AND ≥80% of all other share classes immediately after transfer (IRC §368(c)). Step-transaction doctrine: if a binding commitment to sell equity to third parties pre-existed, IRS collapses the steps. Do NOT execute Section 351 transfer within a pending equity financing round.
3
No Boot
Transfer must be solely in exchange for LLC membership interests. Any cash, promissory notes, or non-equity consideration = “boot” = gain recognition up to the value of boot received. Avoid side agreements, contingent payments, or assumed liabilities that exceed aggregate property basis.
4
Section 357(c) — Liabilities vs. Basis
If liabilities assumed by The Vault exceed the aggregate adjusted tax basis of contributed property, the excess is recognized as immediate taxable gain. Section 174 capitalization (post-TCJA 2017) creates a tangible basis in software development costs — amortized over 5 years. This basis absorbs assumed liabilities and reduces Section 357(c) exposure substantially.

◆ Required Intercompany AgreementsSection 482 compliance — must be executed before royalties flow ▾
⚠ Without Executed ICAs, the IRS Sets the Terms
Without formal, executed Intercompany Agreements (ICAs), the IRS has authority to impute contractual terms based on its own interpretation of economic substance. Failure to maintain adequate contemporaneous documentation exposes the enterprise to 20% and 40% penalties under IRC §6662(e) for substantial transfer pricing misstatements.

Principal Operating Agreements
1 Master IP License Agreement (The Vault → each OpCo)
One per operating company — not a single omnibus agreement. The IRS requires each transaction to be independently analyzable. Key provisions:
  • Licensed IP defined with specificity (software modules, question banks, algorithms, trademarks, trade secrets)
  • Net Revenue defined precisely: gross minus returns, refunds, taxes, and CC processing fees
  • Exclusive license within field of use (Company A: educational testing; Company C: professional certification)
  • Derivative works automatically assigned to The Vault; OpCo receives perpetual license-back
  • Phase-in royalty schedule with revenue thresholds as objective triggering events (not dates)
  • Quarterly payments, due within 30 days of quarter-end; late payment at prime+2%
  • Annual audit rights (once/year, 30 days notice; underpayment >5% → OpCo bears audit costs)
  • 5-year initial term, rolling 1-year renewals; 12-month notice for termination for convenience
  • The Vault indemnifies OpCo against third-party IP infringement claims; OpCo indemnifies The Vault against operational claims
2 Shared Services Agreement
Required if The Vault or any entity provides IT infrastructure, admin, accounting, or HR services to others. Key provisions: service catalog with scope, direct allocation preferred (usage metrics for IT; headcount for HR; revenue for G&A). IRS Services Cost Method (Rev. Proc. 2007-13) permits 0% markup for qualifying low-value-adding services; 5–7% for non-qualifying routine services. Maintain contemporaneous time records and cost allocation worksheets.
3 Data Sharing Agreements (A→E, C→E)
Company A licenses student performance data to Company E — not a transfer of ownership; A retains raw records. Company C licenses certification data to Company E at aggregate level only. Critical: E may NOT share individual-level data between A and C. This data firewall enforces the NCCA/ISO 17024 separation requirement between education and certification. FERPA compliance clauses required for student records. E may not share individual-level data with any entity.
4 501(c)(3) Purchase Agreements (Foundation → A, Foundation → D)
Foundation purchases software/services at fair market value, funded by grants. Not an in-kind donation. Arm’s length, auditable, traceable. Required for Form 990 related-party transaction disclosure. Rebuttable presumption protocol applies: independent board approval + comparable market data. Arm’s length pricing prevents private inurement concerns under IRC §4958.
