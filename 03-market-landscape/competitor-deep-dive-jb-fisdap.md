# Competitor Deep Dive: Jones & Bartlett Learning / Public Safety Group / Fisdap

**Date:** 2026-05-21
**Classification:** Internal competitive intelligence
**Analyst:** MedEdPrep strategy team

## Executive Summary

Jones & Bartlett Learning (JBL), operating its EMS division under the **Public Safety Group (PSG)** brand, is the single largest entity in EMS education by footprint. Through a combination of organic textbook dominance and strategic acquisitions, PSG now controls three critical layers of the paramedic education pipeline: **textbooks** (JBL imprint), **digital learning and test banks** (Navigate platform), and **high-stakes testing + clinical tracking** (Fisdap). This gives PSG a textbook-to-CE career-long lock-in strategy that no other competitor can replicate.

However, these products were built or acquired independently and remain architecturally separate. The gap between Navigate and Fisdap — separate platforms, separate logins, separate data models — is the structural weakness MedEdPrep can exploit. MedEdPrep's unified platform (testing + clinicals + compliance in one ecosystem), adaptive engine, and publisher-agnostic positioning are direct answers to PSG's fragmentation problem.

**Threat level:** High (market presence), but Low (competitive response likelihood in Year 1). Ascend Learning is a $2B+ company; MedEdPrep's current revenue does not register on their radar. This is a window.

## 2. Product #1: Navigate Platform (Digital Learning + Test Banks)

### What Navigate Is

Navigate is JBL's digital courseware platform. It is not primarily a testing product — it is a **textbook companion platform** that includes test banks as a feature. Think of it as JBL's answer to Pearson's MyLab or McGraw-Hill's Connect.

### Key Features

- **Digital textbook access** — eBook versions of JBL titles with highlighting, notes, search
- **Instructor test banks** — Pre-built question banks aligned to specific JBL textbooks (chapter-by-chapter)
- **Assignments and quizzes** — Instructors assign chapter-linked assessments
- **Gradebook** — Basic grade tracking within the platform
- **Course management** — Syllabus tools, pacing guides, instructor resources
- **PowerPoint slides, image banks, lesson plans** — Standard publisher ancillaries
- **Student study tools** — Flashcards, practice quizzes, glossary

### Navigate Product Tiers

Navigate is sold in multiple tiers, typically bundled with textbook purchase:

| Tier | Description | Typical Pricing |
|------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Navigate Essentials** | eBook + basic study tools | Included with textbook (~$150-180 with access code) |
| **Navigate Advantage** | Essentials + test bank + assignments + instructor tools | ~$180-220 with textbook bundle |
| **Navigate Premier** | Advantage + additional simulation/case study content | ~$220-280 with textbook bundle |
| **Navigate TestPrep** | Standalone practice exam product (NREMT-style) | ~$39-59 standalone |

*Note: Navigate pricing is notoriously opaque. Most access is bundled with textbook purchases through campus bookstores. Exact pricing varies by institution, volume, and negotiation. The figures above are estimated ranges based on retail access code prices.*

### How Programs Purchase Navigate

1. **Textbook adoption drives everything.** A program director selects the JBL textbook (e.g., AAOS Orange Book). The textbook comes bundled with a Navigate access code. Students get Navigate "for free" as part of their textbook purchase.
2. **Instructor access is complimentary** when the program adopts the JBL textbook.
3. **Test bank access** is part of the instructor portal, not a separate purchase.
4. **Students may purchase standalone** Navigate TestPrep for NREMT prep without the textbook bundle, but this is a secondary use case.

### Navigate's Limitations

- **Publisher-locked.** Navigate only works with JBL textbooks. If a program uses Brady (Pearson) or another publisher, Navigate is irrelevant.
- **Question quality varies.** Test bank items are typically written by textbook authors or contributors, not psychometricians. No IRT calibration. No item statistics. No adaptive delivery.
- **Not a standalone testing platform.** Navigate was not designed for high-stakes summative testing. It is a courseware supplement.
- **No clinical tracking.** Navigate has zero clinical/field tracking capability. That is Fisdap's domain.
- **Limited analytics.** Basic gradebook. No diagnostic reporting at the domain/objective level. No performance trending over time.
- **No mobile-first design.** The platform is functional but dated. Not designed for on-the-go student engagement.

## 3. Product #2: Fisdap (Testing + Clinical Tracking + Scheduling)

### What Fisdap Is

Fisdap (Field Internship Student Data Acquisition Project) is a **standalone platform** for EMS program management. It covers three distinct functions: high-stakes testing, clinical field tracking, and scheduling. Fisdap is the more strategically significant product for MedEdPrep to understand because it directly overlaps with MedEdPrep's testing and clinicals products.

### Fisdap Testing Products

#### Entrance Exams
- **Purpose:** Predict student success before program admission
- **Format:** Fixed-form, fixed-length standardized exam
- **Methodology:** Classical Test Theory (CTT) — NOT IRT, NOT adaptive
- **Content areas:** Reading comprehension, math/science, anatomy & physiology
- **Predictive validity claims:** Fisdap has published research claiming their entrance exam scores correlate with NREMT first-attempt pass rates. This is their strongest marketing asset.
- **Use case:** Paramedic program admissions screening. Some programs use it as a hard cutoff; others use it as one factor among many.

#### Unit Exams
- **Purpose:** Formative/summative testing during the program, by module
- **Format:** Fixed-form tests aligned to standard EMS curriculum units
- **Content:** Airway, cardiology, trauma, medical, OB/pediatric, operations (legacy NREMT domains — unclear if updated to new 2025 domain structure)
- **Customization:** Limited. Programs can select which unit exams to administer but cannot easily modify content.

#### Comprehensive Exams (Readiness Exams)
- **Purpose:** Summative assessment to predict NREMT readiness before graduation
- **Format:** Full-length fixed-form exam simulating NREMT content distribution
- **Methodology:** Classical Test Theory. Fixed number of items. No adaptation.
- **Claims:** Fisdap markets these as "readiness predictors" with published correlation data to NREMT pass rates.
- **Reporting:** Students receive a score and category-level breakdown. Programs receive aggregate data.

#### Learning Prescriptions
- **Purpose:** Post-exam remediation guidance
- **How it works:** After completing a Fisdap exam, students receive "learning prescriptions" — topic-level recommendations for further study based on areas where they scored below threshold.
- **Limitations:** Prescriptions are generic ("study more cardiology") rather than adaptive or targeted to specific knowledge gaps. They point students back to textbook content rather than into a remediation assessment loop.

### Fisdap Clinical Tracking

This is Fisdap's original product — clinical field tracking existed before the testing suite.

- **Patient encounter logging:** Students log clinical encounters (patient contacts, skills performed, procedures observed/performed)
- **Shift scheduling:** Programs schedule students into clinical and field internship rotations
- **Preceptor evaluation:** Preceptors can evaluate student performance per shift
- **Skill tracking matrix:** Tracks student progress toward required skill/patient contact minimums (aligned to CoAEMSP/CAAHEP standards)
- **Appendix G tracking:** Helps programs track compliance with CoAEMSP accreditation requirements (minimum patient contacts, skills, and team lead experiences)
- **Site management:** Track clinical site agreements, preceptor credentials

### Fisdap Scheduling

- **Shift scheduling engine:** Programs assign students to clinical rotations with conflict detection
- **Site capacity management:** Set maximum students per site per shift
- **Student preference input:** Some programs allow students to indicate scheduling preferences
- **Calendar integration:** Basic calendar views for students and coordinators

### Fisdap Pricing

Fisdap uses a **per-student, per-product pricing model**. Programs purchase access codes for each product separately.

| Product | Estimated Price Range | Notes |
|---------|----------------------|-------|
| Entrance Exam | ~$30-40 per student | One-time administration |
| Unit Exams (bundle) | ~$40-60 per student | Full set for program duration |
| Comprehensive Exam | ~$35-50 per student | Usually 1-2 administrations |
| Clinical Tracking (Scheduler + Skills Tracker) | ~$60-100 per student/year | Ongoing for clinical phase |
| Full Fisdap Bundle | ~$150-250 per student | All products combined |

*Note: Fisdap does not publicly list pricing. These are estimated ranges based on program director reports, conference discussions, and publicly available information. Volume discounts exist for larger programs. Pricing is often negotiated at the institutional level.*

**Total student cost when combined with JBL textbook + Navigate:**
A paramedic student at a JBL-adopted program could pay $400-550+ for textbook + Navigate + Fisdap bundle over their program duration. This is a significant per-student revenue stream for Ascend Learning.

## 4. The Integration Gap: Navigate + Fisdap

This is the most important section for MedEdPrep's competitive strategy.

### How They (Don't) Integrate

Navigate and Fisdap are **architecturally separate products** that were built independently and acquired at different times. Despite both living under the PSG umbrella:

| Integration Point | Current State |
|-------------------|---------------|
| Single sign-on (SSO) | Separate logins, separate accounts |
| Student data flow | No automatic data sharing between platforms |
| Test scores → Clinical tracking | Manual; no automated connection |
| Gradebook unification | Separate gradebooks in each platform |
| Reporting dashboard | No unified reporting across both products |
| Content alignment | Navigate aligned to JBL textbooks; Fisdap aligned to NREMT/CoAEMSP standards |
| API/data export | Limited; CSV exports possible but not seamless |
| Billing | Often purchased through different channels (textbook bundle vs. separate Fisdap purchase) |

### Why This Matters

A program director who uses both Navigate and Fisdap must:

1. Manage two separate platforms with two separate logins
2. Manually cross-reference student performance data between systems
3. Run two separate reporting workflows for accreditation
4. Train faculty on two separate interfaces
5. Troubleshoot two separate support channels
6. Reconcile two separate billing/purchasing flows

This fragmentation is the direct consequence of Ascend Learning's acquisition strategy — buy best-of-breed products and cross-sell them, but do not invest in deep integration because the ROI does not justify the engineering cost when you already have market dominance.

### The MedEdPrep Opportunity

MedEdPrep's entire value proposition is the **unified platform** that PSG cannot deliver without a multi-year, multi-million-dollar integration project that KKR/Ascend has shown no appetite to fund:

| PSG (Two Products) | MedEdPrep (One Platform) |
|---------------------|--------------------------|
| Navigate test bank (CTT, publisher-locked) | Adaptive testing engine (IRT-based, publisher-agnostic) |
| Fisdap entrance/unit/comprehensive exams (CTT, fixed-form) | Adaptive exams with real-time difficulty adjustment |
| Fisdap clinical tracking | MedEdPrep Clinicals (encounter logging, skills tracking) |
| Fisdap scheduling | Built into Clinicals product |
| No compliance automation | CoAssist (accreditation compliance, Appendix G auto-population) |
| No real-time engagement | Pulse (classroom engagement, live games) |
| Separate gradebooks | Unified student dashboard |

| No confidence/metacognition data | 1M+ metacognition-tagged responses |
| Generic "learning prescriptions" | Targeted remediation driven by IRT item parameters |

## 5. Market Position and School Adoption

### Navigate (via JBL Textbook Dominance)

- **JBL is the #1 EMS textbook publisher.** The AAOS "Orange Book" and Nancy Caroline series are the two most widely adopted textbook series in U.S. EMS education.
- **Estimated 50-60% of paramedic programs** use a JBL primary textbook, giving Navigate a built-in distribution channel.
- **Navigate adoption is largely passive** — programs get it "for free" with textbook adoption. Instructor engagement with Navigate's test bank features varies widely.
- **Strongest in paramedic programs.** EMT programs are more fragmented in textbook choice.

### Fisdap Adoption

- **Strongest in paramedic programs.** Fisdap's entrance exam and clinical tracking are most relevant at the paramedic level where clinical rotations are a significant program component.
- **Estimated 200-400 paramedic programs** use at least one Fisdap product (out of ~700+ accredited paramedic programs nationally).
- **Clinical tracking is the stickiest product.** Programs that use Fisdap for clinical tracking rarely switch mid-cohort because historical data migration is painful.
- **Entrance exam is the most visible product** due to Fisdap's predictive validity marketing.
- **Less penetration at EMT level.** Most EMT programs are shorter, have minimal clinical requirements, and do not need the clinical tracking infrastructure.

### Combined PSG Market Position

When you consider both Navigate (via JBL textbooks) and Fisdap together:

- **PSG touches the majority of paramedic students** at some point during their education — through the textbook, the courseware, the testing, or the clinical tracking.
- **Revenue per student is high** — $400-550+ when fully bundled.
- **Switching costs are high** — changing textbooks changes Navigate; changing clinical tracking mid-program is operationally painful.
- **But engagement per product is often shallow.** Many programs use Navigate only for the eBook, not the test bank. Many programs use Fisdap only for clinical tracking, not the exams. The "full suite" adoption rate is lower than PSG's marketing would suggest.

## 6. Strengths

### 1. Ecosystem Lock-In (Textbook → Digital → Testing → Clinical → CE)
PSG is the only entity in EMS education with a plausible end-to-end pipeline. A student encounters JBL in EMT school, uses Navigate for coursework, takes Fisdap exams, logs clinicals in Fisdap, and does CE through Recert. This lifecycle lock-in is PSG's most powerful strategic asset.

### 2. Established Brand and Institutional Trust
JBL textbooks have been the standard for decades. Program directors know the brand. Purchasing departments have existing vendor relationships. There is zero adoption risk.

### 3. Fisdap Predictive Validity Research
Fisdap has published peer-reviewed and white-paper research claiming statistically significant correlations between their entrance exam scores and NREMT first-attempt pass rates. Whether the predictive validity is as strong as marketed is debatable, but the **existence of published research** is a powerful sales tool in an industry where most competitors have no psychometric evidence at all.

### 4. Clinical Tracking Depth
Fisdap's clinical tracking product is mature. It has years of development, handles the complexity of EMS clinical rotations (multiple sites, varying shift structures, preceptor management), and tracks the specific CoAEMSP requirements that programs need for accreditation.

### 5. Financial Resources (Ascend/KKR Backing)
Ascend Learning has the resources to invest if threatened. KKR did not buy Ascend to watch it lose market share. If MedEdPrep became a genuine threat (>$5M revenue, >15% market penetration), Ascend could respond with pricing pressure, bundling, or acquisitions.

### 6. NAEMT Partnership (Recert)
The NAEMT partnership gives PSG's CE product institutional credibility and co-marketing reach. NAEMT is the largest EMS professional organization.

## 7. Weaknesses

### 1. Two Separate Products That Don't Integrate
Covered extensively in Section 4. This is the #1 exploitable weakness. PSG sells a "suite" that is actually two different products with different architectures, different UIs, different data models, and different login credentials.

### 2. Publisher-Locked (Navigate)
Navigate only works if the program uses JBL textbooks. The ~40-50% of programs using Brady (Pearson), Elsevier, or other publishers cannot use Navigate. MedEdPrep is publisher-agnostic — it works with any textbook.

### 3. Classical Test Theory Only — No Adaptive Testing
Fisdap explicitly uses Classical Test Theory for its exams. Every student gets the same items in the same order. No item response theory. No computer-adaptive testing. No real-time difficulty adjustment. This is a 1990s testing methodology being sold to a market that increasingly understands adaptive assessment.

**This is provable.** Fisdap's own documentation describes their methodology. The absence of adaptive testing is not speculation — it is stated.

### 4. Poor Student Experience
Search "Fisdap" on Reddit, EMS forums, or social media. The student sentiment is consistently negative:
- Interface feels dated
- Clinical logging is tedious
- Scheduling tools are clunky
- Exam experience is not engaging
- "Learning prescriptions" feel generic and unhelpful

This negative sentiment is documented across years of student feedback. It represents latent demand for a better product.

### 5. Slow Innovation Cadence
Fisdap's core product has not undergone a fundamental architectural change since acquisition. The clinical tracking UI, the testing methodology, and the scheduling tools are incrementally improved but not reimagined. This is typical of PE-backed education companies — KKR optimizes for margin, not innovation.

### 6. No Real-Time Engagement Layer
PSG has nothing equivalent to Pulse. No live classroom games. No real-time competitive learning. No social engagement mechanics. Their products are used by students because they are required, not because they are engaging.

### 7. Fisdap Clinical Tracking Competes with MedEdPrep Clinicals
This is both a threat and an opportunity. Fisdap's clinical tracking is the stickiest part of their product — programs are reluctant to switch. But MedEdPrep's Clinicals product, especially with CoAssist (compliance automation, Appendix G auto-population), offers capabilities Fisdap does not.

### 8. Pricing is Opaque and Expensive
The combined cost of JBL textbook + Navigate + Fisdap is $400-550+ per student. This is a significant cost burden. Programs and students are increasingly price-sensitive. MedEdPrep can compete aggressively on total cost of ownership.

### 9. No Metacognition or Confidence Data
Fisdap collects answer responses. MedEdPrep collects answer responses tagged with confidence levels (High/Medium/Low), enabling diagnostic analysis of not just what students know, but what they think they know. This metacognition dataset (1M+ tagged responses) has no equivalent at PSG.

## 9. Fisdap's Predictive Validity Claims — A Closer Look

Fisdap's strongest marketing asset is their published research on predictive validity. This deserves specific analysis.

### What They Claim
- Fisdap entrance exam scores correlate with NREMT first-attempt pass rates
- Fisdap comprehensive exam scores predict NREMT readiness
- Programs that use Fisdap entrance exams for admissions screening have higher NREMT pass rates

### What the Research Actually Shows
- Correlations are statistically significant but **moderate** (typically r = 0.3-0.5 range)
- Research is largely conducted or funded by Fisdap/Ascend (potential conflict of interest)
- **Selection bias:** Programs that screen admissions more rigorously tend to have higher pass rates regardless of the screening instrument used
- Classical Test Theory methodology means reliability and validity estimates are sample-dependent, not item-level
- No comparison studies against IRT-based alternatives (because none existed in EMS until MedEdPrep)

### MedEdPrep's Counter-Position
- MedEdPrep's adaptive engine can make the same predictive validity claims with **stronger psychometric evidence** because IRT provides item-level parameters (difficulty, discrimination, guessing) rather than test-level statistics
- MedEdPrep has 10,600+ items with 1.55M response records — a dataset orders of magnitude larger than Fisdap's published research samples
- The diagnostic dimension adds predictive power: it is not just "will this student pass?" but "what specific domains need remediation before they can pass?"
- **Strategic recommendation:** Commission an independent predictive validity study once sufficient NREMT outcome data is available. This neutralizes Fisdap's strongest talking point.

## 10. Strategic Implications

### Near-Term Strategy (Now through Q4 2026)

**DO NOT position MedEdPrep as a Fisdap replacement.** This triggers institutional inertia and forces a binary choice that programs will resolve by keeping the status quo.

Instead, position as a **complementary layer:**
- "Keep Fisdap for field logs. Use MedEdPrep for exams, adaptive practice, and diagnostics."
- "Keep your JBL textbook. MedEdPrep works with any publisher."
- This is a wedge strategy: get into programs alongside Fisdap, then expand scope over time.

### Clinical Tracking Migration Path

1. **Phase 1:** Offer CSV import from Fisdap. Programs will not switch if they have to re-enter historical data.
2. **Phase 2:** Demonstrate CoAssist's compliance automation as functionality Fisdap does not offer.
3. **Phase 3:** Once programs see unified testing + clinicals + compliance in one platform, the case for keeping Fisdap weakens.

### SEO Strategy

Target "Fisdap alternative" as a keyword. Program directors frustrated with Fisdap's interface, pricing, or limitations actively search for alternatives. This is already in the one-year plan for Q3 2026.

### Competitive Response Assessment

| Timeframe | PSG Response Likelihood | Most Likely Action | MedEdPrep Counter |
|-----------|------------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Year 1 (2026) | **Low** | Do nothing. MedEdPrep's revenue does not register at Ascend's scale. | Move fast. Acquire programs. Build case studies. |
| Year 2 (2027) | **Low-Moderate** | May notice if MedEdPrep wins programs from Fisdap. Possible pricing response. | Lock in multi-year contracts. Build switching cost through CoAssist data. |
| Year 3+ (2028+) | **Moderate** | Could invest in Navigate-Fisdap integration. Could attempt IRT/adaptive features. | By this point, MedEdPrep should have independent predictive validity research, 200+ programs, and a psychometric moat too deep to replicate quickly. |

### The KKR/PE Factor

KKR's ownership of Ascend Learning means decisions are driven by EBITDA optimization, not product innovation. PE-backed education companies:
- Optimize pricing and bundling to maximize revenue per student
- Reduce operating costs through consolidation
- Avoid large R&D investments that reduce short-term margins
- Acquire rather than build (hence buying Fisdap rather than building clinical tracking)

This PE operating model is structurally incapable of producing the kind of ground-up platform innovation that MedEdPrep represents. Ascend's response to a competitive threat would most likely be **pricing pressure or bundling** (giving away Fisdap testing for free with clinical tracking purchase), not **building adaptive testing from scratch**.

### The Recert/CE Dimension

PSG's Recert platform ($61.95/year, 200+ courses, NAEMT partnership) is a separate competitive consideration for MedEdPrep's CE strategy. Key points:
- Recert is **Format 3 only** (online, self-paced, no live instruction)
- Recert sells CE hours. MedEdPrep's CE play sells CE hours **that also generate diagnostic data** — the diagnostic-gap-closure loop described in the one-year plan
- Recert benefits from the JBL textbook ecosystem pipeline (students learn on JBL, continue CE on Recert)
- MedEdPrep's Pulse CE pathway (free game converts to paid CE) has no equivalent at PSG

## 11. Key Takeaways

1. **PSG is the 800-pound gorilla in EMS education** — not because any single product is exceptional, but because the textbook-to-CE pipeline creates ecosystem lock-in.

2. **The Navigate-Fisdap integration gap is real and exploitable.** Two separate products, two logins, two data models. MedEdPrep's unified platform is a direct answer.

3. **Fisdap uses Classical Test Theory.** This is provable and documented. MedEdPrep's IRT-based adaptive engine is a generation ahead.

4. **Fisdap's predictive validity research is their strongest card.** MedEdPrep needs to develop its own predictive validity evidence to neutralize this advantage.

5. **Student sentiment toward Fisdap is negative.** This is latent demand for a better product.

6. **Do not force a binary choice.** Position alongside Fisdap, not as a replacement. Win through demonstrated superiority, not ultimatums.

7. **Ascend/KKR will not respond quickly.** PE operating models optimize for margin, not innovation. MedEdPrep has a multi-year window to build market position before a competitive response materializes.

8. **The clinical tracking switching cost is the highest barrier.** CSV import from Fisdap and CoAssist's unique compliance capabilities are the keys to overcoming it.

9. **Publisher-agnostic positioning is an asymmetric advantage.** Navigate only works with JBL. MedEdPrep works with everyone. This matters to the 40-50% of programs not on JBL textbooks.

10. **Total cost of ownership favors MedEdPrep.** $400-550+ for JBL + Navigate + Fisdap vs. MedEdPrep's pricing. As programs face budget pressure, cost becomes a sharper differentiator.
