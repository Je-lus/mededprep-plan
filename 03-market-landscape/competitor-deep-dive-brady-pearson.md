# Competitor Deep Dive: Brady / Pearson / MyLab Brady

*Regenerated 2026-05-21. Original lost in gh-pages branch wipe. Rebuilt from project intelligence + industry knowledge.*

**Bottom line:** Brady/Pearson is the biggest *structural* competitive risk to MedEdPrep due to Pearson's scale, textbook install base, and theoretical ability to build adaptive testing into MyLab. In practice, EMS is a rounding error for Pearson (<0.5% of revenue), and their EMS-specific innovation velocity is near zero. The risk is real but distant -- measured in years, not quarters.

## 1. Company Structure

### Pearson plc (Parent)

- **Headquarters:** London, UK (operates globally; US HQ in Hoboken, NJ)
- **Revenue:** ~$4.7B USD (FY2024). Higher Education is roughly 35-40% of total revenue.
- **Market cap:** ~$8-9B (LSE: PSON)
- **Employees:** ~18,000 globally
- **CEO:** Omar Abbosh (since Jan 2024, previously Mark Sheridan as interim)
- **Strategic direction:** Aggressive pivot to digital-first, AI, and direct-to-consumer. Sold or wound down physical textbook printing. Acquired Credly (digital credentials, 2022), invested heavily in Pearson+ subscription platform.

### Brady Division

Brady is not a separate legal entity -- it is a **publishing imprint** within Pearson's Higher Education division, specifically under the Public Safety / Health Professions umbrella.

**History:**
- Robert J. Brady founded Brady Communications in the 1970s as an EMS textbook publisher
- Acquired by Prentice Hall (1984), which was later acquired by Pearson (1998)
- Brady has operated as Pearson's EMS/fire/public safety imprint for 25+ years
- The "bradybooks.com" domain redirects to Pearson's catalog pages

**Key Brady authors (the real brand):**
- **Daniel Limmer** -- Lead author of *Emergency Care* (the EMT textbook). Also runs Limmer Education (separate company, 13 employees, LC-Ready platform, "7 Things EMS" podcast). CAPCE Provider Spotlight Feb 2026.
- **Joseph Mistovich** -- Lead author of *Prehospital Emergency Care* and contributing author to multiple Brady titles
- **Edward Dickinson** -- Co-author of *Emergency Care*
- **Bryan Bledsoe** -- Lead author of *Paramedic Care: Principles & Practice* (the flagship paramedic text)
- **Robert Porter** -- Co-author of paramedic and AEMT texts
- **Richard Cherry** -- Co-author of paramedic texts

**Critical insight:** Brady's brand power comes from these authors, not from Pearson. Dan Limmer already operates independently (Limmer Education). The author relationships, not the Pearson corporate structure, are what programs trust.

### Where Brady Fits in Pearson's Portfolio

Pearson's Higher Education division (~$1.6-1.8B revenue) spans:
- STEM (largest segment -- MyLab Math alone is massive)
- Business/Economics (MyLab with Revel)
- Social Sciences
- Health Professions (Nursing, Allied Health)
- **Public Safety (EMS, Fire) -- Brady is here**

EMS education is a sliver of Health Professions, which is itself a fraction of Higher Ed. Conservative estimate: **Brady/EMS generates $20-50M annually for Pearson** (textbook sales + MyLab subscriptions combined), representing less than 0.5% of total Pearson revenue. This is the core of why Pearson is not building EMS-specific adaptive testing -- the ROI doesn't justify the engineering investment.

### Brady Textbooks (The Foundation)

Brady publishes the dominant EMS textbook line across all certification levels:

|-------|-------|---------------------|---------------------|---------|
| *Emergency Care* | EMT | 14th Edition | $170-190 (print) | Limmer, O'Keefe, Dickinson |
| *Prehospital Emergency Care* | EMT (alternative) | 12th Edition | $170-190 | Mistovich, Karren, Hafen |
| *Emergency Care and Transportation of the Sick and Injured* | EMT (J&B competitor) | N/A (this is J&B's) | -- | -- |
| *Paramedic Care: Principles & Practice* | Paramedic | 6th Edition (5 volumes) | $250-350+ (set) | Bledsoe, Porter, Cherry |

| *Emergency Medical Responder* | EMR | 11th-12th Edition | $140-160 | Le Baudour, Bergeron |

**Market share estimate:** Brady and Jones & Bartlett (Ascend Learning) split the EMS textbook market roughly 50/50, with Brady historically stronger in EMT and J&B stronger in paramedic (though both compete at all levels). Together they control ~85-90% of EMS textbook adoptions. Elsevier/Mosby has a small presence (primarily through Aehlert clinical references), and McGraw-Hill has largely exited the space.

### MyLab Brady (The Digital Platform)

MyLab Brady is a course-management and assessment platform **bundled with Brady textbook adoptions**. It is a vertical instance of Pearson's broader MyLab platform (the same technology that runs MyLab Math, MyLab Nursing, MyLab Programming, etc.).

**Core Features:**

1. **eText (Pearson+ integration):** Digital version of the Brady textbook with highlighting, notes, and mobile access. Since 2022-2023, Pearson has migrated eTexts to the Pearson+ subscription platform ($10.99/month or $49.99/4-month for single title).

2. **Test Bank / Instructor Resources:**
   - Pre-built question banks aligned chapter-by-chapter to the Brady textbook
   - Typically 40-80 questions per chapter across the textbook
   - Question types: multiple choice, true/false, fill-in-the-blank, matching, short answer
   - Instructors can select questions, randomize order, set time limits
   - **Questions are written by textbook authors/contributors** -- not psychometrically validated through IRT or any formal item analysis process
   - Exportable to LMS formats (Blackboard, Canvas, D2L/Brightspace, Moodle)

3. **Homework & Practice:**
   - Chapter-aligned practice quizzes and homework assignments
   - Auto-graded with immediate feedback
   - Performance tracked in gradebook

4. **Pre-Test / Post-Test Sequencing:**
   - Chapter pretests that "personalize" a study plan by identifying weak areas
   - Post-tests to verify learning after chapter completion
   - **This is what Pearson markets as "adaptive" -- it is NOT adaptive in any psychometric sense.** It is a simple pretest-remediate-posttest loop. No IRT. No item-level calibration. No ability estimation. The "personalization" is: you got chapter 5 questions wrong, so study chapter 5.

5. **Gradebook & Reporting:**
   - Assignment-level grade tracking
   - Class averages and item-level performance (% correct)
   - Exportable to institutional LMS
   - **No diagnostic reporting by competency domain, NREMT blueprint, or learning objective**
   - No individual student diagnostic profiles
   - No longitudinal performance tracking across courses

6. **Simulations & Media:**
   - Some anatomy and assessment simulations (primarily repackaged from Pearson's A&P/nursing MyLab products)
   - Video clips tied to textbook skills
   - Virtual scenario walkthroughs (limited, not high-fidelity)

7. **Dynamic Study Modules (DSM):**
   - Pearson's brand name for their flashcard-style spaced retrieval feature
   - Students rate confidence, get re-queued on weak items
   - Marketed as "adaptive" but operates on simple confidence-based scheduling, not psychometric models
   - Available across MyLab products, not EMS-specific

### What MyLab Brady Is NOT

- Not a standalone assessment platform (cannot use without textbook adoption)
- Not a certification exam prep tool (no NREMT-style practice tests)
- Not a competency diagnostic system (reports % correct, not competency gaps)
- Not available to individual students without institutional adoption
- Not publisher-agnostic (only works with Brady textbooks)
- Not used for comprehensive/entrance/exit exams (programs still need a separate tool for this)

## 3. Textbook Integration Model

### How It Works (The Adoption Cycle)

1. **Textbook committee at a program selects Brady** (e.g., *Emergency Care* 14th ed.) as their required text
2. **Students purchase access** -- either print textbook + MyLab access code, eText + MyLab bundle, or Pearson+ subscription
3. **Instructor sets up MyLab course** -- selects textbook, enables assignments, configures gradebook
4. **Students complete chapter-aligned work** through MyLab for the duration of the course (typically 1-2 semesters)
5. **Access expires** after the subscription period (4-month or lifetime access to that edition)

### Strength: Built-in Distribution

This model gives MyLab Brady enormous reach without direct selling effort:
- **Every Brady textbook adoption is a potential MyLab adoption.** If 1,000 programs use Brady textbooks and even 40% activate MyLab, that is 400 institutions without a single sales call.
- **Textbook adoption committees are the buyer.** The decision to use MyLab is often bundled with the textbook decision -- not a separate software procurement.
- **Instructors don't need to justify a new purchase.** MyLab is "included" or "recommended" as part of the textbook package. It doesn't appear as a separate line item.
- **LMS integration is pre-built.** Pearson has dedicated integration teams for Canvas, Blackboard, D2L. Grade passback and single sign-on work.

### Limitation: Textbook Lock-In

The same model creates significant constraints:

- **Only works with Brady textbooks.** If a program uses J&B's *Nancy Caroline's Emergency Care in the Streets* for paramedic, MyLab Brady is useless. If they switch textbooks, they lose their entire MyLab question bank, assignments, and grade history.
- **Tied to textbook editions.** When Brady releases a new edition (every 3-4 years), the MyLab course must be rebuilt. Old assignments break. Question banks change.
- **Cannot serve as a standalone assessment tool.** Programs that want comprehensive exams, entrance exams, exit exams, or adaptive practice must still buy a separate product (Platinum/EMSTesting, Fisdap, or MedEdPrep).
- **No cross-publisher curriculum support.** Many programs use Brady for EMT but J&B for paramedic. MyLab only covers the Brady courses.
- **No B2C path.** Individual students cannot buy MyLab Brady to practice for the NREMT independently. They need an instructor-created course.

## 4. Pricing Model & Licensing

### Student-Facing Pricing (as of 2024-2025)

Pearson has been transitioning to a subscription model. Current pricing structures for Brady titles:

| Access Type | Typical Price | Duration |
|------------|--------------|----------|
| Print textbook + MyLab access code | $220-280 | Semester/lifetime (edition-locked) |
| eText + MyLab bundle | $100-130 | 4-month access |
| Pearson+ single title subscription | $10.99/month | Monthly recurring |
| Pearson+ Multi subscription (all Pearson titles) | $14.99/month | Monthly recurring |
| MyLab standalone access (no eText) | $80-100 | 4-month access |
| Used textbook + MyLab access code only | $80-100 | 4-month access |

**Key pricing observations:**
- Pearson+ is the strategic direction. $10.99/month = ~$44 for a 4-month course. Significantly cheaper than legacy print bundles.
- MyLab access is effectively bundled with the textbook cost. Students rarely perceive it as a separate purchase.
- **Institutional pricing (Inclusive Access / Pearson First Day):** Many community colleges and programs negotiate Inclusive Access deals where all students automatically get eText + MyLab on the first day of class at a reduced per-student rate (typically $40-80/student). This is billed directly to the institution or added to tuition/fees.
- Programs do NOT pay a separate institutional license for MyLab. The revenue comes from student access sales.

### Comparison to MedEdPrep

| | MyLab Brady (effective cost) | MedEdPrep |
|--|---|---|
| Per-student price | $44-130/semester (varies by access type) | $26/student (institutional) |
| What's included | eText + chapter quizzes + homework + gradebook | Adaptive testing + diagnostic reporting + CoAssist + exam building |
| Textbook included? | Yes (eText) | No (publisher-agnostic) |
| Standalone assessment? | No | Yes |
| B2C available? | No | Yes (students can self-purchase) |

**Important:** MyLab Brady and MedEdPrep are not directly price-comparable because MyLab includes the textbook. The actual "assessment platform" cost embedded in MyLab is estimated at $30-50 of the total student price -- comparable to MedEdPrep's institutional rate. But MedEdPrep's assessment capabilities far exceed what MyLab provides.

## 5. Market Position & Adoption

### Estimated Adoption Numbers

Hard adoption numbers are not publicly disclosed. Based on industry analysis:

- **EMS programs in the US:** ~1,800-2,200 (EMT, AEMT, Paramedic programs combined, per NREMT/CoAEMSP data)
- **Brady textbook market share:** Estimated 45-55% of programs use at least one Brady title
- **MyLab activation rate among Brady adopters:** Estimated 30-50% (many instructors adopt the textbook but do not use MyLab, relying on their own tests or third-party tools)
- **Implied MyLab Brady active programs:** ~250-500 programs
- **Students per year through MyLab Brady:** Estimated 30,000-60,000

For comparison:
- MedEdPrep: 100+ active programs
- Platinum/EMSTesting: Estimated 500-800+ programs
- Fisdap: Estimated 400-600+ programs (primarily paramedic/clinical tracking)

### Market Dynamics

Brady's textbook market share has been **slowly eroding** over the past decade:
- J&B (*Nancy Caroline*, *Emergency Care and Transportation*) has gained share, particularly in paramedic programs
- Some programs have moved to open educational resources (OER) or custom courseware
- Pearson's aggressive digital pivot (discontinuing some print options, pushing Pearson+) has frustrated programs accustomed to traditional textbook procurement
- The EMS education market is highly price-sensitive (community colleges, fire departments, vocational programs) -- Pearson's premium pricing is a headwind

## 6. Integration with Pearson's Ed-Tech Stack

### Pearson+ (Subscription Platform)
- Pearson+ is the consumer-facing subscription for digital textbooks
- Brady eTexts are available on Pearson+ 
- Pearson+ includes study tools, flashcards, and AI tutoring features (powered by Pearson's partnership with large language models)
- As of 2024-2025, Pearson has been adding "AI Study Tool" features to Pearson+ that let students ask questions about their textbook content

### MyLab Platform (Shared Infrastructure)
- MyLab Brady runs on the same platform as MyLab Math, MyLab Nursing, MyLab Programming, etc.
- This means EMS gets the benefit of platform-wide improvements (LMS integration, mobile app, accessibility) without requiring EMS-specific engineering investment
- It also means EMS-specific features are extremely unlikely to be prioritized -- engineering resources go to the high-revenue verticals (Math, Business, Nursing)

### Mastering (Not Relevant to EMS)
- Mastering is Pearson's platform for science courses (A&P, Biology, Chemistry)
- Some overlap in paramedic prerequisite courses but no direct EMS connection

### Revel (Interactive Textbook)
- Revel is Pearson's "reading reimagined" platform that integrates assessments inline with textbook content
- **Brady titles are NOT currently on Revel** (as of 2024-2025). Revel has been deployed for higher-volume Pearson subjects. This is telling -- it suggests Pearson is not investing in bringing their most advanced content delivery technology to the EMS vertical.

### Credly (Digital Credentials)
- Pearson acquired Credly in 2022 for digital badge/credential infrastructure
- Theoretical connection to EMS certifications exists but has not been deployed for Brady/EMS programs
- This is a "watch" item -- if Pearson connects Credly badges to MyLab Brady completions, it could create a micro-credentialing play in EMS

## 7. Strengths

### 1. Textbook Distribution Lock-In
Every Brady textbook adoption is a built-in distribution channel. Programs don't need to make a separate purchasing decision for MyLab -- it comes with the text. This is an enormous structural advantage that no standalone platform can replicate.

### 2. Brand Recognition
"Brady" is synonymous with EMS education for an entire generation of EMTs and paramedics. *Emergency Care* is the EMT textbook at a plurality of programs. Author names (Limmer, Bledsoe, Mistovich) carry personal brand authority.

### 3. LMS Integration
Pearson has dedicated integration engineering teams for Canvas, Blackboard, D2L/Brightspace, and Moodle. Grade passback, single sign-on, and deep linking all work. For institutions with LMS-centric workflows, this matters.

### 4. Institutional Inertia
Switching textbooks is a multi-year decision process at most programs. Faculty have built courses around Brady content. Changing textbooks means rebuilding lectures, assignments, and lab activities. MyLab is sticky because the textbook is sticky.

### 5. Scale Infrastructure
Pearson handles millions of concurrent users across all MyLab products. Uptime, security, FERPA compliance, accessibility (Section 508) -- all handled at enterprise scale. No startup can match this operational infrastructure.

### 6. Inclusive Access / First Day Programs
Pearson's institutional deals (automatic day-one access for all students at reduced cost) remove the "will students actually buy access?" problem. This is a meaningful advantage in price-sensitive EMS programs where student non-compliance with textbook purchases is common.

## 8. Weaknesses

### 1. No Adaptive Testing (Real)
MyLab Brady's "adaptive" features are pretest/posttest chapter sequencing and confidence-based flashcards. There is:
- No IRT (Item Response Theory)
- No CAT (Computerized Adaptive Testing)
- No item-level psychometric calibration
- No ability estimation
- No difficulty adaptation based on student performance

The "adaptive" label is marketing for what is actually simple branching logic. **Pearson's EMS "adaptive testing" is actually Platinum's product** -- Pearson distributes EMSTesting through some channels but does not own or build it.

### 2. No Standalone Assessment
Programs cannot use MyLab Brady for:
- Comprehensive exams
- Entrance exams / admission testing
- Exit exams / program completion testing  
- NREMT-style practice tests
- Independent diagnostic assessment

They must buy a separate product for all of these use cases. This is the primary gap MedEdPrep fills.

### 3. No Diagnostic Reporting
MyLab reports % correct per assignment and per chapter. It does NOT provide:
- Competency-domain-level diagnostics (Primary Assessment, Medical/Trauma, Resuscitation, EMS Operations)
- NREMT blueprint mapping
- Individual student weakness identification
- Longitudinal performance tracking across a program
- Cohort-level diagnostic dashboards for program directors
- Accreditation-ready outcome data

### 4. Publisher Lock-In
Only works with Brady textbooks. Programs using J&B, Elsevier, or mixed curricula get zero value from MyLab Brady. This limits TAM to ~50% of the market at best.

### 5. No B2C Channel
Individual students cannot buy MyLab Brady to prepare for NREMT independently. No self-purchase. No direct-to-consumer marketing. No organic student acquisition. This is fundamentally an institutional product.

### 6. No CE Play
MyLab Brady serves initial education only. No continuing education, no recertification support, no career-long relationship with the learner. Once a student finishes their program, the Pearson relationship ends (unless they enter a higher-level program using another Brady text).

### 7. Innovation Velocity Is Near Zero
EMS is too small for Pearson to justify dedicated engineering investment. Feature improvements come from platform-wide MyLab upgrades (which benefit Math and Nursing more than EMS) or from textbook edition cycles (every 3-4 years). There is no evidence of EMS-specific product development at Pearson.

### 8. Question Quality Is Not Psychometrically Validated
Brady test bank questions are written by textbook authors and contributors as a supplement to the textbook. They are not:
- Calibrated through IRT
- Reviewed through psychometric analysis (difficulty, discrimination, distractor analysis)
- Validated against real student performance data at scale
- Mapped to NREMT exam blueprint weighting

They are content-valid (aligned to the textbook) but not construct-valid (measuring the competencies NREMT tests).

### 9. Textbook Edition Dependency
When a new edition releases, instructors must rebuild their MyLab courses. Question banks change. Assignment links break. This creates periodic disruption every 3-4 years that programs have no control over.

## 10. The Consolidation Risk

### What Could Happen

The most dangerous scenario for MedEdPrep is **Pearson deciding to build (or acquire) real adaptive testing and diagnostic reporting capabilities for the EMS vertical.**

Possible paths:
1. **Pearson acquires Platinum Educational Group outright.** Platinum/EMSTesting is the closest thing to an EMS-specific adaptive testing platform (though it uses CTT, not IRT). If Pearson bought Platinum and integrated EMSTesting into MyLab Brady, they would have textbook + assessment in one platform.
2. **Pearson deploys MyLab's AI features to EMS.** Pearson is investing heavily in AI across its platform (AI tutoring in Pearson+, auto-generated study plans, etc.). If they deployed these to MyLab Brady with any rigor, the "no adaptive testing" weakness could diminish.
3. **Pearson builds an NREMT prep product.** Pearson already offers test prep products in nursing (NCLEX prep through MyLab Nursing). If they built an equivalent NREMT prep product using Brady content, it would be a direct competitor to MedEdPrep's B2C channel.
4. **Pearson licenses IRT/CAT technology.** Pearson owns Pearson VUE, one of the world's largest high-stakes testing companies. They have IRT/CAT expertise internally. If someone connected the Pearson VUE psychometric team with the MyLab Brady team, the resulting product could be formidable.

### Why It Probably Won't Happen Soon

1. **Revenue priority mismatch.** EMS is <0.5% of Pearson revenue. Any engineering investment in EMS-specific features competes for resources with Math, Nursing, Business -- all of which are 10-50x larger markets.
2. **Corporate inertia.** Pearson is a $5B company going through a major strategic transformation (digital pivot, AI investment, Pearson+ migration). EMS-specific initiatives are not on the corporate radar.
3. **Acquisition integration is slow.** Even if Pearson acquired Platinum tomorrow, corporate integration takes 12-24 months minimum. Product integration (actually connecting EMSTesting to MyLab Brady in a seamless experience) takes another 12-24 months. That is a 2-4 year timeline from acquisition to competitive threat.
4. **Pearson VUE is a separate business unit.** Pearson VUE (testing centers, psychometrics) and Pearson Higher Education (textbooks, MyLab) operate as separate divisions with separate P&Ls, separate leadership, and limited cross-pollination. The idea that Pearson VUE psychometric expertise would flow to MyLab Brady requires internal alignment that does not currently exist.
5. **Author economics.** Brady's key authors (Limmer, Bledsoe, Mistovich) receive royalties from textbook sales. They have limited incentive to push for platform innovation that doesn't drive textbook adoption. Dan Limmer already runs his own separate ed-tech company (Limmer Education), which suggests he doesn't see Pearson as the right vehicle for innovation.
6. **No M&A signals.** As of May 2026, there are no public signals that Pearson is looking at EMS ed-tech acquisitions. Pearson's recent M&A has focused on workforce/skills (Credly, partnerships with enterprise training).

### Trigger Signals to Watch

Monitor for these events that would escalate the threat level:

| Signal | Implication | Response |
|--------|------------|----------|
| Pearson acquires Platinum Educational Group | Direct competitive threat in 12-24 months | Accelerate program adoption. Lock in multi-year commitments. Build switching costs through data. |
| Pearson announces "NREMT Prep" product | B2C channel competition | Accelerate B2C growth. Differentiate on adaptive engine quality and psychometric transparency. |
| Pearson deploys Revel for Brady EMS titles | Signals investment in EMS digital experience | No immediate threat, but indicates EMS is getting attention. |
| Brady publishes new edition with "AI-powered assessment" | Marketing escalation, may or may not be substantive | Evaluate actual capabilities. Likely cosmetic. |
| Pearson hires EMS-specific product manager | Internal investment signal | This would be the first concrete sign of EMS-focused engineering investment. |
| Dan Limmer / Limmer Education acquired by Pearson | Consolidation of Brady author ecosystem | Would signal Pearson trying to own the full Brady ecosystem. |

## 11. Strategic Implications for MedEdPrep

### Year 1 Priority: Speed

The Pearson risk is real but measured in years. MedEdPrep's window is NOW:
- Every program that adopts MedEdPrep creates switching costs (historical data, diagnostic baselines, trained instructors)
- Every student who uses MedEdPrep and passes NREMT generates a data point and a testimonial
- Every cohort of diagnostic data makes MedEdPrep's IRT calibration more accurate
- CoAssist adoption creates accreditation-workflow lock-in that MyLab cannot replicate

**MedEdPrep should be in 200+ programs before Pearson could even begin to react.** At 100+ programs today, 200+ is achievable within 12 months with Heather and Samantha selling.

### Positioning: Complement, Don't Compete

**Never position against the Brady textbook.** Position alongside it:
- "We love Brady. Our questions are aligned to the same NREMT blueprint. Use Brady for content delivery, use MedEdPrep for assessment and diagnostics."
- "MyLab Brady handles your chapter homework. MedEdPrep handles your comprehensive exams, exit exams, and adaptive practice."
- "If you switch textbooks next year, your MedEdPrep data and exams still work. That's the point."

This positioning avoids triggering Pearson's competitive response while capturing the assessment gap that MyLab Brady cannot fill.

### The Moat MedEdPrep Is Building

Things that take Pearson years to replicate even if they tried:

1. **10,600 psychometrically calibrated items with 1.55M response records.** This is not a question bank -- it is a calibrated measurement instrument. Writing questions is easy. Calibrating them through live student data takes years.
2. **IRT-based adaptive engine in production.** Building CAT is a specialized engineering problem. Pearson VUE could do it, but deploying it to MyLab Brady requires cross-divisional coordination that doesn't exist.
3. **Publisher-agnostic positioning.** MedEdPrep works with every textbook. Pearson structurally cannot offer this -- their model depends on publisher lock-in.
4. **B2C channel.** Students already buy MedEdPrep individually. Pearson's institutional-only model means they would need to build an entirely new go-to-market for direct-to-student.
5. **CoAssist accreditation tooling.** No competitor has this. It creates institutional switching costs that go beyond assessment.
6. **Metacognition dataset.** 1M+ confidence-tagged responses. No EMS competitor collects this data. It powers research partnerships (UIUC) and differentiates the diagnostic model.
7. **CE pathway (Pulse CE).** MedEdPrep is building a career-long relationship from student to practitioner. Pearson's relationship ends at graduation.

### Competitive Response Likelihood (Updated Assessment)

| Scenario | Probability (2 years) | Impact | MedEdPrep Readiness |
|----------|----------------------|--------|-------------------|
| Pearson does nothing | **70%** | None | Continue current strategy |
| Pearson adds AI study features to MyLab Brady | **20%** | Low -- cosmetic, not psychometric | Educate market on real vs. fake adaptive |
| Pearson acquires Platinum | **5-8%** | High -- but 2+ year integration timeline | Accelerate adoption, lock in programs |
| Pearson builds NREMT prep product | **2-3%** | Medium -- B2C competition | Differentiate on engine quality and data |
| Pearson deploys Revel + real adaptive for EMS | **<2%** | High | Would require fundamental response |

**Net assessment: Pearson is the biggest structural risk but the lowest near-term probability. The 12-18 month window identified in the competitive playbook holds. Move fast.**
