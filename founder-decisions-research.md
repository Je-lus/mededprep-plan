## Decision 1: 501(c)(3) Name

### The Question
What should the 501(c)(3) nonprofit entity be called?

### Why It Matters
The name appears on the Articles of Incorporation filed with the Georgia Secretary of State, the Form 1023 submitted to the IRS, all grant applications, and all public-facing materials. It sets the nonprofit's identity independent of MedEdPrep. The wrong name creates confusion with the for-profit entities or limits future programmatic scope. The right name signals independence, mission breadth, and credibility to grantors and state agencies.

### Options

**Option A: Mission-focused name with no MedEdPrep branding**
Examples: "Foundation for Out-of-Hospital Care," "EMS Futures Foundation," "Lifeline Education Foundation"
- Pros: Maximum perceived independence from MedEdPrep. Strongest firewall optics for IRS Form 1023. Broadest programmatic flexibility (can evolve beyond EMS into hospice, community health, etc.). Grantors and state agencies see an independent organization, not a corporate subsidiary.
- Cons: No brand recognition transfer from MedEdPrep. Must build awareness from scratch. Harder to explain the MedEdPrep connection to potential partners who know MedEdPrep but not the foundation.

**Option B: MedEdPrep-adjacent name**
Examples: "MedEdPrep Foundation," "MedEdPrep Community Foundation"
- Pros: Instant brand recognition within the EMS education market. Clear connection to MedEdPrep for partners and customers. Easier to explain the relationship.
- Cons: IRS scrutiny increases when the nonprofit shares a name with a for-profit. Looks like a "house nonprofit" created to benefit the for-profit. Grantors may question independence. Limits the nonprofit's ability to be perceived as serving a mission broader than MedEdPrep's commercial interests. Could trigger additional Form 1023 questions about private benefit.

**Option C: Hybrid name**
Examples: "MedEd Access Foundation," "Out-of-Hospital Education Foundation"
- Pros: Suggests the mission space without directly invoking MedEdPrep's brand. Moderate brand proximity. Reasonable independence optics.
- Cons: "MedEd" prefix may still trigger IRS association concerns. Less clean than a fully independent name.

### Precedent
- **NASM Foundation** (tied to for-profit NASM) exists but is not a strong model -- it is closely identified with NASM's commercial interests.
- **Gary Sinise Foundation** and **Firehouse Subs Public Safety Foundation** demonstrate that mission-first names build stronger independent identities.
- In healthcare education, the most credible foundations have names that describe their mission, not their parent organization: "Health Resources in Action," "National Health Foundation."

### Recommendation
**Option A: A mission-focused name with no MedEdPrep branding.** The 501(c)(3) formation design document explicitly warns against "shell nonprofit" perception. A name like "Foundation for Out-of-Hospital Care" or similar signals genuine independence to the IRS, to grantors, and to the public. The three program areas (EMS education access, workforce development, end-of-life care) span a broader mission than MedEdPrep's commercial products -- the name should reflect that breadth.

### Urgency
**Moderate -- needed before Articles of Incorporation are filed.** The 30-day action plan has this on the critical path for Week 1-2. Board recruitment can proceed in parallel, but the name must be settled before Georgia SOS filing. Not blocking anything else today, but it will block filing within 1-2 weeks.

### Reversibility
**Hard to reverse once filed.** Georgia allows name amendments to Articles of Incorporation, but it requires a board resolution, a new filing with the SOS, re-publication in the legal organ newspaper, and notification to the IRS. The EIN does not change, but all governance documents, bank accounts, contracts, and grant applications must be updated. Pick the right name now.

## Decision 2: Board Recruitment

### The Question
Confirm 3 independent directors to proceed with 501(c)(3) formation. Who are the initial board members?

### Why It Matters
The nonprofit cannot be filed until at least 3 independent directors are identified (4 total with the founder). Board composition is the single largest factor the IRS evaluates for governance quality on Form 1023. The 30-day action plan identifies board recruitment as the critical path item -- nothing else proceeds until this is resolved. Beyond IRS approval, the board members' expertise determines grant competitiveness (Perkins V, HRSA, and private foundation applications all require demonstrating governance capacity).

### Options

**Option A: File with minimum viable board (1 founder + 2 independent = 3 directors)**
- Pros: Fastest path to filing. Easier to find 2 people than 3. Gets the clock started on Form 1023 processing (3-6 months).
- Cons: 67% independence ratio -- exactly meets the 2/3 threshold with zero margin. If one independent director's status changes (e.g., they receive a payment from MedEdPrep exceeding $10K), independence drops below threshold. IRS may view a 3-person board with concerns about adequate governance.

**Option B: File with 4 directors (1 founder + 3 independent)**
- Pros: 75% independence ratio. Comfortable margin. Matches the recommendation in the formation design document. Demonstrates stronger governance to IRS. Covers 3 of the 4 expertise gaps (legal/governance, finance/grants, EMS education/public safety). Adequate for initial grant applications.
- Cons: Requires finding 3 qualified independent people, which takes more time. Delays filing by days-to-weeks depending on recruitment speed.

**Option C: File with 5 directors (1 founder + 4 independent)**
- Pros: 80% independence -- exceeds best practices from day one. All 4 expertise gaps covered (legal, finance, EMS, community/hospice). Strongest possible Form 1023 presentation. Strongest grant competitiveness.
- Cons: Finding 4 qualified, independent people takes the most time. The community/hospice seat (Seat 5) may be the hardest to fill before Company D matures. Delays filing further.

### Precedent
Most small nonprofits file with 3-5 directors. IRS approval is routine at 3+. The formation design document recommends filing with 4 if possible, recruiting the 5th within the first year.

### Recommendation
**Option B: File with 4 directors (1 founder + 3 independent).** The 75% independence ratio is comfortable, the formation design document recommends it, and it covers the three critical expertise gaps (legal/governance, finance/grants, EMS education). Recruit the 5th (community/hospice) within 6 months. Do not delay filing to wait for a perfect 5-person board.

**Where to look (per the formation design document):**
- Seat 2 (Legal/Governance): Georgia Center for Nonprofits board matching program, local attorneys with nonprofit experience
- Seat 3 (Finance/Grants): CPAs with nonprofit clients, retired finance professionals, grant administrators
- Seat 4 (EMS/Public Safety): EMS educators at non-MedEdPrep programs, retired EMS directors, NAEMSE members in Georgia

### Urgency
**Blocking now.** This is the single item blocking 501(c)(3) formation. The 30-day action plan cannot proceed past Week 2 without confirmed board members. Every day of delay pushes back the Form 1023 filing, which pushes back the determination letter, which pushes back grant eligibility.

### Reversibility
**Moderately reversible.** Directors can be added or removed per the bylaws. The initial board composition sets the tone for the IRS application, but the board evolves over time. It is easier to add directors later than to remove a problematic one. Choose carefully but do not let perfection delay progress.

## Decision 3: Certification Body Brand

### Why It Matters

Using "MedEdPrep" in the certification body's name would immediately raise NCCA auditor concerns about independence. The brand must signal credibility, independence, and psychometric rigor to state EMS boards, program directors, and candidates. It is also the name that will appear on certificates, state regulations (if recognized), and NCCA accreditation documents for decades.
