# RightSite Health Financial Scale and Growth

Research date: 2026-06-19  
Company disambiguation: This report covers RightSite Health, the EMS-initiated telehealth / ER-diversion / patient-navigation company using `rightsitehealth.com`, with filings under RightSite Health, Inc. and operating contracts under RightSite Health Physicians PLLC. It does not cover unrelated "RightSite" real-estate/site-selection companies or the forensic toxicologist H. Chip Walls.

## Executive view

RightSite appears to be a venture-backed, commercially active digital-health services company, not a pre-revenue experiment. The strongest financial-scale evidence is SEC Form D data showing three equity offerings totaling about $33.65M sold/offered across 2023, 2024, and 2025, with a $20.0M 2025 round. That corroborates the commonly cited ~$33.6M figure. Sources: [S1], [S2], [S3], corroborated by Tracxn/Elion snippets and LinkedIn-Crunchbase funding feed [S4], [S5], [S6]. Confidence: High for the Form D total; Medium for round labels because most third-party databases are partly gated and inconsistent.

I found no reliable disclosed annual revenue or valuation. One lead/database page estimates about $3.5M in annual revenue, but this is **SINGLE SOURCE - unverified** and appears model-derived rather than company-reported. SEC filings decline to disclose revenue range. Sources: [S3], [S24]. Confidence: Low for any revenue estimate; High that no public audited/company-reported revenue was found.

The company is likely able to pay a small-five-figure vendor if the work is important to growth, payer billing, operations, data, compliance, or EMS partnerships. The caveat is that RightSite's model is performance/value-driven and often "no cost" to EMS agencies/cities, so budgets are probably scrutinized and vendor spend likely needs a clear ROI tie. Sources: [S12], [S13], [S14], [S15], [S18], [S19]. Confidence: Medium-high.

Only one accessible third-party lead database estimated annual revenue at $3,507,755, revenue per employee at $86,000, and total funding at $20.0M. ### Revenue model

RightSite appears to monetize primarily through participating/contracted health plans and patient insurance, not through EMS agencies or city contracts:

- Public Columbus, GA health-services contract: RightSite offers services to the EMS agency at no charge; RightSite is paid by the patient's insurance when applicable; uninsured patients are accepted and not billed. Source: [S12]. Confidence: High for that contract.
- Jacksonville interrogatory response: RightSite says it provides services to San Antonio Fire Department and participating health plans, is paid by contracted health plans, and the city does not pay RightSite for clinical services. It also says Jacksonville does not pay for clinical services. Source: [S13]. Confidence: High for RightSite's stated model in that response.
- San Antonio coverage: city contract through Dec. 31, 2027 at no cost to the city, with expenses covered by patient insurance. Source: [S14]. Confidence: High for San Antonio contract economics.
- Texas Association of Health Plans sponsor page: RightSite says it generates direct MLR savings, requires no upfront cost for health plans, and operates as a credentialed provider, not a vendor. Source: [S15]. Confidence: Medium-high because it is company promotional material hosted by a health-plan association.
- HC9 CEO interview: Rubin says RightSite is paid only after health plans measure value and that no one pays upfront; plans pay after they save. Source: [S8]. Confidence: Medium because it is CEO/investor interview material, but it is consistent with contracts.
- A current/recent job posting for an Insurance and Billing Specialist references insurance verification, revenue cycle alignment, coding accuracy, clean claims, and "case-rate and other billing scenarios," supporting a provider-style billing/revenue-cycle operation. Source: [S18]. Confidence: Medium.

### Per-diversion / savings economics

RightSite-specific per-case revenue was not found. I found savings/economic claims, but not fee schedules or per-member-per-month rates.

- HC9 interview: Rubin says avoiding an ambulance run saves the EMS system about $1,000 per avoidable run. **SINGLE SOURCE - unverified.** Source: [S8]. Confidence: Medium-low.
- HC9 interview: Rubin says ED care costs "eight to ten times more than necessary" for many non-emergency 911 callers. **SINGLE SOURCE - unverified.** Source: [S8]. Confidence: Medium-low.
- RightSite's own EMS page claims 95% of low-acuity patients accept RightSite when offered, 85% are redirected without ambulance transport, and 80% have their barrier to care resolved. Source: [S16]. Confidence: Medium; company-reported.
- RightSite's health-plan page claims 93 out of 100 low-acuity patients accept when offered, 84 out of 100 are redirected without ambulance, and 80 out of 100 have their barrier resolved. Source: [S17]. Confidence: Medium; company-reported.
- CareSource press release says that since launching in Jan. 2024, the Georgia program helped 95% of 911 callers avoid an ambulance trip, diverted 84% of eligible CareSource members away from the ER, and produced a 9.5/10 satisfaction score. Source: [S19]. Confidence: Medium-high because it is a named payer partner press release.
- New Braunfels city release says communities already using RightSite have seen 93% patient acceptance and about 84% eligible-case redirection to more appropriate non-emergency care settings. Source: [S20]. Confidence: Medium-high for the cited rollout claims.

Partner/market signals from company site: RightSite's EMS page lists partner testimonials from Fayette County Fire/EMS, Beaumont Fire & EMS, Henry County 911 Communications, and Grady EMS. Source: [S16]. Confidence: Medium; company site, not independently verified line-by-line.

Hiring velocity / GTM buildout: A Jan. 8, 2026 EMS Partnerships Texas role was posted for sales/business development, targeting fire departments, ambulance services, 911 communications centers, and PSAP directors, with new-business revenue responsibility and Texas airport proximity including Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, and Austin. ## Ability to pay a small-five-figure vendor

Assessment: likely yes, if the vendor maps to a real operating or revenue priority.

Reasons:

- Capital: ~$33.65M raised by SEC filings, including a recent ~$20M 2025 financing. Sources: [S1], [S2], [S3]. Confidence: High.
- Institutional backing: HC9 and Polaris involvement are well-supported; ARTIS and Virtue are supported by third-party/portfolio-source evidence. Sources: [S8], [S10], [S11], [S33]. Confidence: Medium-high overall.
- Commercial operations: multiple payer/municipal/EMS contracts and public launches show active deployment across Georgia, San Antonio, New Braunfels, Cy-Fair/Harris County, and Jacksonville. Sources: [S13], [S14], [S19], [S20], [S23]. Confidence: Medium-high.
- Revenue-cycle infrastructure: job postings indicate active insurance verification, claims, coding, payer, and case-rate workflows. Source: [S18]. Confidence: Medium.

Caveats:

- Revenue is not public and may still be early relative to funding. Source: [S3]. Confidence: High.
- City/EMS contracts are often "no cost" to the agency, so the company must collect from health plans/insurance or value-based arrangements. Sources: [S12], [S13], [S14]. Confidence: High.
- Their model emphasizes measured savings/no upfront cost, so non-essential vendors may face tighter ROI scrutiny than a traditional SaaS buyer. Sources: [S8], [S15]. Confidence: Medium.

Practical vendor-read: RightSite does not look scrappy/pre-revenue. It looks like a recently funded, scaling, venture-backed healthcare services company with a small but real operating team. A $15K-$50K vendor engagement is plausible if it directly supports sales expansion, implementation, payer billing, reporting/analytics, security/compliance, marketing demand generation, or operational scale.
