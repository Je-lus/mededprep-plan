# IACBE JetPack Research: The Program-Side Experience

**Date:** 2026-06-01
**Purpose:** Understand what IACBE-accredited business schools experience when using JetPack, to prepare for CoAEMSP's July 2026 meeting about mandatory JetPack adoption for EMS programs.

---

## Executive Summary

IACBE launched JetPack as its accreditation management system in **September-October 2025**. The rollout followed a structured sequence: overview webinar (September 11), user training (September 29), and credential distribution (September 22). Each member institution gets **two full-access users** ("Member Lead" and "Member Contributor") plus optional read-only observers.

**The key finding:** There is virtually zero public complaint or negative feedback from IACBE program directors about JetPack. This is either because (a) the transition was genuinely smooth, (b) it's too early and the full burden hasn't hit yet, (c) accreditation staff don't air grievances publicly, or (d) IACBE's relatively small scale (~230 institutions) made it manageable. The absence of noise is itself useful intelligence for the July meeting.

**Total rollout time:** Approximately 3 weeks from announcement to live system (Sep 11 to late Sep 2025). This is remarkably fast, suggesting the accreditor-side configuration was done before involving member institutions. Programs were given ~2 weeks between receiving credentials and the training session.

**Key implication for CoAEMSP:** IACBE's rollout was compact. If CoAEMSP follows a similar pattern, EMS programs could see a similarly compressed timeline. Programs should expect a few weeks between "here's your login" and "start submitting."

## 2. What Programs See: The Portal Experience

### User Roles & Access

From IACBE's published documentation:

- **Member Lead (primary contact):** Has voting privileges, receives all IACBE correspondence and invoices, full access to JetPack.
- **Member Contributor (alternate contact):** Equal portal access to Member Lead, should be "well versed in accreditation."
- **Member Observer:** Read-only access.
- **Financial Contact:** CC'd on billing, may or may not have portal access.

Each institution designates exactly **two full-access users** -- these are the "go to" people for all accreditation matters. They are tasked with entering all accreditation documents (annual reporting, interim reporting, self-study).

### What Programs Submit Through JetPack

Based on IACBE's compliance documentation:

1. **Annual reporting** (ongoing compliance)
2. **Interim Quality Assurance Reports (IQAR)** -- midway through accreditation period
3. **Outcomes Assessment Plans (OAP)** -- structured assessment templates
4. **Self-Study documents** (draft at 120 days, final at 60 days before site visit)
5. **Changes in Programs/Locations**
6. **Changes in Contact Information** (President/CEO, Provost, other contacts)
7. **Changes in Academic Business Unit Name**
8. **Web Path Notification Form**
9. **Public Statements of Membership**

### What Changed from Before JetPack

**Before:** IACBE used a combination of:
- Web forms on iacbe.org (some still visible, e.g., the IQAR submission page still has a basic upload form)
- Email submissions to reports@iacbe.org
- MS Word templates uploaded via web forms
- A legacy system (not publicly named)

**After:** All of the above consolidated into JetPack's portal. Key differences:
- Centralized document repository (no more emailing files)
- Structured data entry (standards-aligned fields, not freeform uploads)
- Contact changes managed directly within JetPack
- Self-study built and edited online with collaborative access
- Prior-year data carries forward (not starting from scratch each cycle)

**Notable:** As of June 2026, some IACBE compliance pages still show traditional web-form submission (IQAR page still has a basic file upload form with CAPTCHA). This suggests IACBE may be running a hybrid model during transition, or that certain forms haven't been fully migrated into JetPack yet. This is relevant intelligence -- it means even IACBE's "reference implementation" may not be 100% complete after 9 months.

## 3. Complaints, Pain Points, and Friction

### Public Complaints Found: None

After extensive searching across:
- Reddit (including r/MBA, higher education subreddits)
- LinkedIn (IACBE-related posts, Virtual Atlantic's page, Dr. Laurie Yates' profile)
- Software review sites (Capterra, Software Advice, SoftwareSuggest, SourceForge)
- Google search (multiple query variations)
- Higher education forums

**Zero negative feedback found from IACBE program directors about JetPack.** This is the single most important finding.

### Why This Matters

Several interpretations:
1. **IACBE is small.** ~230 institutions, ~2,000 programs. Manageable scale for hand-holding.
2. **The accreditation culture is non-public.** Program directors rarely air accreditation tool complaints publicly. This is institutional back-office work.
3. **Timing.** Only ~9 months since launch. The real stress test comes when self-study submissions go through JetPack at scale.
4. **IACBE's supportive relationship.** IACBE is known for "partnering" with programs rather than being adversarial. Quotes like "IACBE's Supportive Accreditation Process Brings Greater Benefits to Schools" are prominent on their site. This culture may suppress public complaints.
5. **It might actually work well.** JetPack's reviews (from the accreditor side) are uniformly positive (4/4 reviews on Capterra are 5.0/5.0, 2/2 on SoftwareSuggest are 5/5).

## 5. Complementary Tools

### IACBE's Ecosystem

IACBE does not appear to officially recommend specific complementary tools alongside JetPack. However:

- **Peregrine Global Services:** Long-standing IACBE partner for assessment (ETS Major Field Test alternative). Peregrine was a sponsor at ACAM 2026.
- **Bungee Enterprise Survey Management:** Virtual Atlantic's own survey tool that integrates with JetPack. Used for data collection that feeds into the accreditation process.
- **CapSource:** IACBE partnership for project-based experiential learning (student case study competition).

### Third-Party IACBE Tools (Not From Virtual Atlantic)

**Kramah Ki-IACBE Software** is a third-party tool specifically designed for institutions pursuing IACBE accreditation. It provides:
- Automated Assurance of Learning (AoL) reporting
- CO-PO-PLO mapping
- Evidence management structured to IACBE Self-Study Report format
- Faculty qualification tracking (AQ, PQ, IP, SP classification)

**Key insight:** The existence of Kramah's Ki-IACBE tool (which is institution-side, not accreditor-side) suggests that JetPack alone may not fully meet all institution-side needs. Kramah's tool fills gaps in AoL cycle management, rubric-based assessment tracking, and automated report generation that institutions need internally before submitting to JetPack. This is analogous to how a program might use its own internal tools to prepare data before uploading to an accreditor's portal.

**Implication for CoAEMSP:** EMS programs will likely still need their own internal tools for data collection, outcomes tracking, and report preparation. JetPack is the submission and management layer, not a replacement for institutional data infrastructure. This is where CoAssist can position itself.

IACBE continues to offer regular trainings that now reference JetPack:

## 8. What This Means for the July CoAEMSP Meeting

### Questions to Ask CoAEMSP

Based on the IACBE research, these are high-value questions for the July meeting:

1. **Rollout timeline:** IACBE did it in ~3 weeks. What's CoAEMSP's timeline? Will there be a longer runway for EMS programs, which are often smaller and less resourced than business schools?

2. **User access model:** IACBE gives 2 full-access users per institution. How many will CoAEMSP provide? EMS programs often have a single program director doing everything -- will one user license be enough?

3. **Parallel systems:** AACP is running 3 concurrent systems during migration. Will CoAEMSP run their current submission process alongside JetPack during transition?

4. **What moves into JetPack:** Which submissions (Annual Reports, Self-Study Reports, site visit documentation) go into JetPack first? IACBE and CSI both did phased rollouts.

5. **Training & support:** IACBE offered live Zoom training sessions. What will CoAEMSP provide for ~600+ EMS programs?

6. **Cost pass-through:** Will programs bear any cost for JetPack? IACBE appears to absorb the cost as part of membership. One Capterra reviewer noted JetPack's cost "may seem prohibitive up front" for accreditors.

7. **Internal tool compatibility:** JetPack is a submission/management layer. Programs still need their own data infrastructure. Does CoAEMSP anticipate programs using complementary tools (like CoAssist) alongside JetPack?

### Strategic Positioning for CoAssist

The IACBE experience reveals a clear **division of labor** in the accreditation tech stack:

- **JetPack = the accreditor's portal.** It manages submissions, tracks compliance, coordinates reviews, and generates reports for the accrediting body.
- **The gap = program-side preparation.** JetPack doesn't help programs collect their data, run their assessments, track their outcomes, or prepare their documentation. It's where you submit, not where you prepare.

This is exactly where **CoAssist can differentiate**:
- CoAssist helps programs **prepare** what goes into JetPack
- CoAssist automates the **data collection and analysis** that JetPack receives
- CoAssist is the program's tool; JetPack is the accreditor's tool
- They are complementary, not competitive

**The Kramah Ki-IACBE analogy is perfect:** Just as IACBE business schools use Kramah's tool to prepare their AoL reports and self-study evidence before submitting through JetPack, EMS programs could use CoAssist to prepare their accreditation materials before submitting through CoAEMSP's JetPack instance.

## 9. Gaps and Limitations of This Research

### What We Could NOT Find

1. **No program-side portal screenshots or documentation.** The entity-facing JetPack experience is completely hidden behind logins.
2. **No program director complaints or feedback.** Zero forum posts, zero Reddit threads, zero LinkedIn gripes. Either it's fine or nobody talks about it publicly.
3. **No IACBE-specific JetPack user guide.** All documentation is behind the member login.
4. **No data on actual adoption metrics.** We don't know what percentage of IACBE members are actively using JetPack vs. still submitting via legacy methods.
5. **The IACBE YouTube webinar** exists but requires watching the full recording to extract portal details -- could not transcribe here.
6. **No CoAEMSP-specific JetPack announcement found** in public web materials. The ACCREDITCON announcement was verbal/conference-only.
