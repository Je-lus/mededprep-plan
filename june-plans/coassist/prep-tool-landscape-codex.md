# Third-Party Accreditation Prep Tool Landscape

Date researched: 2026-06-01

Scope: Kramah Ki-IACBE, JetPack-adjacent tools, and the broader market for program-side accreditation preparation, evidence management, outcomes assessment, and self-study software.

## 1. Executive Summary

- Kramah Ki-IACBE is a real, public product from Kramah Software for institutions pursuing IACBE accreditation. It is positioned as a program-side prep and compliance tool: Assurance of Learning reporting, outcome mapping, evidence management, faculty qualification tracking, dashboards, and IACBE-aligned self-study support.
- No public evidence was found that Ki-IACBE integrates directly with JetPack, exports into JetPack, or is endorsed by IACBE or Virtual Atlantic. Public Kramah materials describe IACBE self-study/evidence preparation, not JetPack submission.
- JetPack remains primarily accreditor-side infrastructure. The clearest JetPack-adjacent program-side examples are ACPE PHARMS, AACP AAMS/Survey System transition materials, CSI's school-facing JetPack rollout, and NAAB's Accreditation Management System. These are mostly accreditor-provided portals, not independent third-party prep products.
- The broader accreditation prep software market is crowded at the general higher-education/institutional-effectiveness layer, but sparse for narrow program-specific prep tools. Most incumbents are enterprise, quote-only, and built around assessment, outcomes, evidence repositories, program review, and self-study workflows. Transparent pricing is unusual.
- CoAssist has a plausible open lane if it stays clearly program-side, accreditor-specific, lightweight, and free: "the place programs prepare and maintain evidence year-round before submitting through the accreditor portal."

## 3. Other JetPack Ecosystem Tools

### JetPack by Virtual Atlantic

JetPack is made by Virtual Atlantic / VA, Inc. It is positioned as accreditation management software for accreditors, not primarily as internal prep software for programs.

Public JetPack features include:

- Standards and criteria management
- Self-study submission
- Annual reporting
- Site visit management

- Commission/board decision workflows
- Documentation and evidence handling
- Data standardization
- Benchmark reporting

- Role-based access and audit trails

Virtual Atlantic's public messaging explicitly recognizes that accreditors and accredited entities have different needs. That supports the strategic distinction: JetPack is the official process/portal layer; programs still need operational and evidence-preparation systems upstream.

### AACP and ACPE pharmacy tools

AACP's Assessment and Accreditation Management System (AAMS) was developed by AACP in collaboration with ACPE to assist pharmacy schools with assessment and accreditation activities. AACP states that beginning July 1, 2025, institutions no longer use AAMS to submit accreditation data; ACPE's PHARMS is the expected reporting system for 2025 and later. AAMS remains available through the end of 2026 for transition.

Important details:

- AAMS purpose: assist schools and colleges of pharmacy with assessment and accreditation-related activities.
- AACP's system guide references AAMS, Survey System 1.0, and Jetpack Survey System 2.0 as concurrent systems during the 2025-2026 transition.
- AACP states no AACP data will populate automatically into PHARMS; institutions are expected to export data from AACP systems for import into PHARMS.
- This is direct evidence that even where accreditor systems exist, programs still need to export/import, prepare, and reconcile data.

ACPE's PHARMS is the Pharmacy Accreditation Report Management System. ACPE states it is designed to help colleges/schools prepare their self-study report and document how the program addresses ACPE standards. ACPE also states PHARMS is intended for creation and submission of the final report and is not meant to be used for data collection or as a peer review tool of the report.

Strategic relevance: ACPE explicitly distinguishes the final report/submission system from data collection. That is the exact gap CoAssist can occupy for CoAEMSP programs.

### CSI accreditation

Christian Schools International announced JetPack in February 2023. CSI described JetPack as a way to streamline, simplify, and automate accreditation and annual reporting for schools. Phase one focused on annual reporting improvements; phase two was planned to include site visit documentation, self-study, school profile, school improvement plan, and site visit report materials.

CSI's announcement is a useful JetPack program-facing reference because it lists concrete benefits to schools:

- Accepting all file types for evidence upload
- Carrying forward prior-year reporting information
- Easier authorization of additional school users
- Integrating professional development reporting into staff roster workflows
- Simplified disaster procedure log
- Uploading supporting evidence directly into substantive change forms
- Consolidating accreditation documents, templates, forms, self-study, school profile, improvement plan, and site visit report in one platform

This is still an accreditor-provided portal experience, not an independent prep tool.

### NAAB accreditation management system

NAAB's site says annual reports are submitted through NAAB's Accreditation Management System (AMS), which facilitates and streamlines program reporting and volunteer review. Search results and Virtual Atlantic's LinkedIn product page list NAAB as a JetPack customer, but NAAB's public AMS page does not visibly name JetPack in the accessible content found.

NAAB also publishes accreditation fees, which are separate from software pricing. For example, 2026 fees include $3,100 APR fee, site visit fees, and annual fees by enrollment tier. These are accreditation fees, not prep-tool software fees.

### IACBE and JetPack

IACBE uses JetPack as its accreditation management system. Publicly visible evidence from prior research indicates IACBE launched JetPack around September 2025, with member training and a member-facing portal. No public program-side user guide or portal screenshots were found.

Kramah Ki-IACBE is the only IACBE-specific independent third-party prep product found in this research. It is not described publicly as an official IACBE or JetPack companion.

### Other third-party tools that prepare data for JetPack submission

Not found in a direct, explicit sense.

No public vendor was found saying: "we prepare data specifically for JetPack submission," "JetPack export," "JetPack connector," or "JetPack API integration" for AACP, ACPE, CSI, NAAB, IACBE, or CoAEMSP.

Adjacent evidence:

- AACP says institutions must export AACP system data for import into PHARMS.
- LinkedIn/Virtual Atlantic marketing says JetPack can integrate with Bungee Enterprise Survey Management or other third-party tools.
- JetPack comparison/listing pages reference API access, but official implementation details were not found publicly.

Conclusion: The JetPack complementary-tool market is not publicly mature. Tools exist around assessment, surveys, outcomes, and evidence, but explicit JetPack-focused prep/export tools are scarce or invisible.

## 4. Broader Accreditation Prep Tool Landscape

The landscape has two main layers:

1. General higher-ed institutional effectiveness platforms: support assessment, outcomes, curriculum mapping, evidence repositories, strategic planning, program review, and accreditation self-study across many accreditors.
2. Program/discipline-specific tools: support specific accreditors or professional education domains such as ABET, IACBE, ACPE, CCNE/ACEN, NAAC/NBA, health professions, and teacher education.

## 5. Market Analysis

### How sparse or crowded is the market?

Crowded:

- General higher-ed assessment/accreditation/institutional effectiveness software is crowded. Watermark, Weave, Anthology, HelioCampus, Nuventive, AEFIS, Creatrix, Kuali-adjacent tools, and others all address evidence, outcomes, assessment, program review, and accreditation readiness.
- Health professions has multiple operational platforms that support accreditation readiness indirectly: Enflux, Medtrics, CORE ELMS, eMedley, and others.
- India/global accreditation tools for NAAC/NBA/ABET/AACSB/IACBE are also visible, with Kramah and Creatrix especially prominent.

Sparse:

- Explicit program-side prep tools for one specific accreditor are much rarer.
- ABET has at least dal.ai and ABET-supporting OBE tools, but many programs still appear to use generic assessment platforms or spreadsheets.
- IACBE-specific independent software search produced Kramah Ki-IACBE as the only clear product.
- No clear CoAEMSP/CAAHEP/EMS-specific prep tool was found in this research pass.
- No clear "JetPack export/prep" product category was found.

Conclusion: the generic category is crowded, but the accreditor-specific program prep category remains sparse.

## 6. Strategic Implications for CoAssist

### The core opening

JetPack and similar accreditor portals solve the accreditor's process problem: receiving standardized submissions, tracking reviews, managing site visits, and preserving official records.

Programs still have the upstream problem:

- Collect data all year
- Track students, graduates, outcomes, surveys, skills, clinicals, and employer feedback
- Know what is missing before the deadline
- Turn messy operational data into clean accreditation evidence
- Maintain continuity despite staff turnover
- Prepare documents before entering/uploading them into the official portal

CoAssist can own that upstream layer.

### What CoAssist can learn from Kramah

- Accreditors' language matters. Kramah's strongest pages mirror each accreditor's vocabulary: AoL, PLO/SLO, AQ/PQ/IP/SP for IACBE; CO-PO mapping and Criterion/SAR logic for ABET/NBA.
- The product should make the accreditor's evidence structure visible in the tool. Kramah's evidence folders aligned to IACBE self-study format are a strong pattern.
- The pitch should be readiness, not only submission. "Year-round audit readiness" is a repeated market theme.
- Dashboards and gap analysis are table stakes in this category.
- Faculty/staff burden reduction is a major buying argument, even when the buyer is leadership.
- Vendor-hosted testimonials are not enough for market credibility; CoAssist should try to generate transparent case studies or public examples early.

### What CoAssist can learn from dal.ai and AtlasOA

- Transparent pricing is differentiating because incumbents hide pricing.
- Narrow specificity can beat generic breadth. dal.ai's ABET-only positioning is clearer than generic accreditation software.
- "Built for small teams, no consultant required" is a strong wedge against enterprise platforms.
- Easy export and data ownership matter. AtlasOA emphasizes CSV/SQL exports and self-hosting; CoAssist can emphasize clean exports and no lock-in even if it remains hosted.

### What CoAssist can learn from ACPE PHARMS

ACPE explicitly says PHARMS is for creating/submitting the final report and is not meant for data collection or peer review. This is a nearly perfect precedent for CoAssist messaging:

"The official portal is where you submit. CoAssist is where your program collects, checks, and prepares the evidence before submission."

### Product opportunities

High-value CoAssist features based on market patterns:

- CoAEMSP standards/evidence map
- Accreditation readiness checklist and gap dashboard
- Evidence repository organized exactly like CoAEMSP/CAAHEP expectations
- Annual report/self-study export bundles
- Survey collection and outcome calculations
- Graduate and employer survey workflows
- Clinical/field internship evidence mapping
- Personnel/faculty credential tracking
- Medical director/program director task workflows
- Prior-year carryforward
- Audit trail and change history
- "Submission packet" export for JetPack upload
- Plain CSV/PDF/Word exports so the tool is useful even without JetPack API access
- Optional API/connector later if Virtual Atlantic or CoAEMSP cooperates

### Positioning recommendation

Use a complementary, not adversarial, position:

"JetPack is the official CoAEMSP portal. CoAssist is the free program-side prep workspace that helps EMS programs keep their accreditation evidence ready year-round and export cleaner submission materials when CoAEMSP asks for them."

Avoid claiming JetPack integration until there is an actual integration.

### Market risk

- If CoAEMSP's JetPack implementation includes robust program-side data collection, checklists, carryforward, surveys, and export/import workflows, CoAssist's overlap increases.
- Enterprise vendors could add CoAEMSP templates, but the market size may be too narrow for them to prioritize.
- Free pricing is a strong wedge, but institutions may still prefer an official or vendor-backed system if CoAEMSP endorses one.
- The biggest adoption risk is double entry. CoAssist should minimize it through exports, copy-ready text, generated tables, and eventually API integration if possible.

### Overall assessment

The market is not empty, but the precise CoAssist lane is under-served. The strongest evidence is not the absence of accreditation software generally; there is plenty. The evidence is the absence of public, accreditor-specific, program-side tools for CoAEMSP-like workflows, plus the repeated distinction across JetPack, ACPE PHARMS, and Kramah between official submission systems and prep/data systems.
