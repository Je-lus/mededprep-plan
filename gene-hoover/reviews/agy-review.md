# Agy Review - Gene Hoover Offer + JD

This hiring package establishes an authentic, high-respect foundation for a foundational educator, but requires several crucial structural and legal refinements—chiefly around pre-existing intellectual property, wage-and-hour timekeeping boundaries, and Program Director regulatory protection—to eliminate candidate hesitation and insulate MedEdPrep from legal exposure.

---

## 1. Coherence

The offer letter and position description fit together cleanly in their broad architecture. The reporting relationship (Jeramey Ricks, CEO), employment classification (part-time, hourly, non-exempt at $40.00/hour), primary campus location (Carrollton, GA), and tripartite division of responsibilities (Education Delivery, Program Administration, Outreach Duties) are well aligned. The deliberate regulatory scoping ("programs under this position's direction") is scrupulously maintained across both documents, avoiding any inadvertent collision with the company's separate CAPCE-accredited activities.

However, several subtle disconnects and operational omissions create friction:

1. **Remote Work Disparity:** Clause 7 of the offer letter explicitly notes that *"Administrative work may be performed remotely by arrangement."* In contrast, the subheader of the position description lists only *"Location: Carrollton, Georgia campus, with travel to clinical and partner-agency sites."* Because curriculum authoring, clinical tracking, student remediation reviews, and state reporting are primarily desk work, the job description should explicitly reflect this remote administrative flexibility.
2. **"Other Duties" Bounding:** The offer letter's catch-all in Clause 1 (*"perform such other duties as the company may reasonably assign from time to time"*) is completely unbounded. By contrast, the job description's catch-all ties duties directly *"in support of its educational mission."* For a non-exempt, part-time employee, leaving "other duties" open-ended in the primary legal document invites operational scope creep. The offer letter should mirror the job description's educational mission limitation.
3. **Absence of an Hours Range or Baseline:** While the flexible schedule is an intentional accommodation of Gene's availability (Constraint #1), neither document specifies an anticipated baseline or weekly range (e.g., "anticipated at 10 to 20 hours per week, varying with cohort schedules"). Without a baseline or scheduling notice protocol, Gene cannot predict income or reserve time against his outside clinical shifts, and MedEdPrep cannot easily budget monthly labor costs.
4. **Referenced Enclosures:** The offer letter mentions *"the enclosed position description"* in paragraph 1, but in Clause 10 notes that *"As a condition of employment, you will execute MedEdPrep's Confidentiality and Non-Disclosure Agreement (the 'NDA')."* It does not clarify whether the NDA is enclosed or provided separately. Signing an offer conditioned on an unseen document is a classic friction point; the letter should state that the NDA is enclosed and executed concurrently.

## 2. How It Reads to the Candidate

Gene Hoover is an established critical-care paramedic educator (NRP, CCP-C/FP-C) who helped build MedEdPrep's educational architecture from scratch. He is a peer, an expert, and a clinician with an established professional identity.

### Tone, Respect, and Warmth
The personal touch in this letter is exceptional:
- The opening acknowledgment (*"You have shaped our education from its first outline, and there is no one better suited to lead its delivery"*) and the closing tribute (*"Gene, this program exists because of the standard you hold it to"*) are genuine, collegial, and deeply respectful. They recognize his equity of effort in building the company.
- The closing signature from Jeramey Ricks including full clinical credentials (`NRP, CCP-C, FP-C`) reinforces a peer-to-peer, clinician-led partnership rather than a cold corporate hierarchy.
- Clause 9 explicitly validates his outside clinical practice, which is vital for any active paramedic educator whose credibility depends on maintaining sharp street skills.

### What Will Give Gene Pause
Despite the warm framing, four specific provisions will cause an experienced professional of Gene's caliber to hesitate before signing:

1. **Intellectual Property Overreach on Prior & Concurrent Works (The Biggest Red Flag):** 
   Clause 11 assigns all course materials, scenarios, and curricula created within the scope of employment to MedEdPrep as works made for hire. But Gene has already been developing materials for MedEdPrep, and he possesses a lifetime of clinical slide decks, simulation scenarios, ventilator algorithms, and ECG rhythm strips developed prior to this hire or used in his outside teaching. 
   Furthermore, Clause 10 demands that upon termination, he must *"return or permanently destroy all MedEdPrep data, materials, and copies in your possession or control."* 
   Gene will immediately wonder: *If I incorporate my favorite ventilator scenario or critical-care lecture into a MedEdPrep class, does MedEdPrep now own it? If I leave, am I legally barred from using my own materials? Do I have to delete my own files?* Without an explicit prior-works carve-out, this clause reads as predatory to an experienced educator.
2. **Ambiguous "Conflict of Interest" Boundaries:** 
   Clause 9 permits outside work that does not *"create a conflict of interest with MedEdPrep."* In EMS education, veteran educators frequently teach for multiple entities—local technical colleges, hospital CE programs, or regional AHA/NAEMT training centers (e.g., ACLS, PALS, PHTLS). Because "conflict of interest" is undefined, Gene may fear that his existing teaching engagements or future clinical CE gigs could be retroactively labeled a breach of loyalty.
3. **The "Unpaid Administrative Trap":** 
   The job description details heavy administrative duties: administering enrollment, student remediation, faculty coordination, Medical Director review cycles, and clinical tracking. In EMS training, clinical tracking and student remediation can quickly swallow 20+ hours a week. At $40/hour non-exempt, Gene will immediately ask himself: *Am I getting paid to answer student emails at 9:00 PM, or track down missing preceptor evaluations on Sunday afternoons? Or will I be expected to absorb administrative overhead as "part of being the Director"?* The offer must reassure him that all time spent on MedEdPrep business is compensable, logged time.
4. **Personal Regulatory Liability as State Program Director:** 
   Under Georgia OEMS&T rules, the designated Program Director is held personally accountable by the state for program integrity, records retention, and regulatory compliance. Taking on state-designated Program Director duties for $40/hour without explicit contractual confirmation of company administrative backing and regulatory indemnification exposes Gene's personal EMS license to institutional risk.

## 3. Legal & HR Red Flags (Georgia W-2, Hourly, At-Will)

### A. FLSA Non-Exempt & Wage-and-Hour Mechanics
- **Classification:** Classifying the role as hourly non-exempt is legally prudent. It avoids the catastrophic misclassification traps common to "Director" titles under the FLSA white-collar administrative/academic exemptions.
- **The Off-the-Clock Hazard:** Under the FLSA (29 U.S.C. § 201 et seq.), an employer must pay non-exempt employees for all hours they are "suffered or permitted" to work. High-level educational roles invite off-the-clock work: checking emails on mobile devices, grading quizzes at home, or fielding preceptor calls. 
  - *Fix:* The offer letter must include mandatory timekeeping language, an explicit prohibition against off-the-clock work, and an affirmative statement that all preparation, student correspondence, and administrative work must be accurately logged and will be paid.
- **Overtime Authorization:** While non-exempt employees must be paid overtime if they work over 40 hours in a workweek, employers are legally entitled to require advance written authorization for overtime hours. Without an explicit overtime pre-approval requirement, an hourly employee could rack up unbudgeted overtime during intensive cohort weeks.
- **Pay Frequency:** O.C.G.A. § 34-7-2 requires Georgia employers to pay wages at least semi-monthly or monthly (or bi-weekly). Specifying the regular pay schedule (e.g., "paid bi-weekly") ensures compliance and eliminates ambiguity.

### B. At-Will Employment & Integration
- **At-Will Language:** Clause 3 contains solid at-will language, but the phrase *"although we will each endeavor to provide reasonable notice"* introduces mild contractual ambiguity. Under Georgia law (O.C.G.A. § 34-7-1), employment without a definite term is strictly at-will. To preserve goodwill without diluting the at-will presumption, clarify that advance notice is an informal professional courtesy, not an enforceable obligation.
- **Missing Integration & Modification Clause:** The offer letter completely lacks an integration (merger) clause. It must explicitly state that the offer letter, along with the enclosed Job Description and NDA, constitutes the entire agreement between the parties, supersedes all prior discussions, and cannot be modified except in a written agreement signed by the CEO.

### C. Intellectual Property & Prior Works Carve-Out
- Under 17 U.S.C. § 101, works created by an employee within the scope of employment are statutory works made for hire, and Clause 11's present assignment (*"hereby assign"*) correctly closes the gap identified in *Board of Trustees of Leland Stanford Junior Univ. v. Roche Molecular Systems, Inc.*
- However, the complete absence of a **Prior Inventions / Pre-Existing Materials Carve-Out** is a severe deficiency:
  1. Gene helped build the curriculum *before* his W-2 start date.
  2. Gene owns pre-existing slides, clinical cases, and instructional frameworks.
  3. The contract must clarify that:
     - Pre-existing materials remain Gene's sole property.
     - To the extent Gene incorporates pre-existing materials into MedEdPrep courseware, he grants MedEdPrep a non-exclusive, perpetual, royalty-free license to use and modify them for MedEdPrep's educational programs.
     - Materials Gene creates outside of MedEdPrep working hours and without MedEdPrep confidential information remain his property.

### D. Confidentiality, BYOD, and Student Records
- **NDA Mechanics:** Clause 10 conditions employment on executing an NDA. If the NDA is separate, it must be attached and executed simultaneously.
- **BYOD Data Destruction vs. Deletion:** Clause 8 requires personal devices to be encrypted, and Clause 10 requires the employee upon termination to *"permanently destroy all MedEdPrep data, materials, and copies."* On a personal computer or phone containing personal backups and mixed personal files, "permanently destroy" can be interpreted as requiring a full device wipe. The clause should instead require the employee to *"return all physical property and securely delete all MedEdPrep proprietary files and student data from personal devices."*
- **Student Records Privacy:** In EMS education, student records contain criminal history checks, drug screens, immunizations, and clinical evaluation metrics. The clause should specify that student records must be accessed and stored exclusively through MedEdPrep's secure institutional platforms (e.g., Google Workspace, LMS) rather than stored locally on personal hardware.

### E. Outside Activities & Permitted Scope
- Under Georgia's Restrictive Covenants Act (O.C.G.A. § 13-8-50 et seq.), restrictions on outside work during employment must be reasonable.
- To protect Gene's livelihood and avoid disputes, the clause should explicitly state that active field EMS practice, flight paramedic duties, hospital clinical work, and standardized provider courses (e.g., ACLS, PALS, ITLS, PHTLS) taught for other entities are expressly permitted, provided they do not conflict with scheduled MedEdPrep teaching obligations or use MedEdPrep proprietary curriculum.

### F. Travel Reimbursement & Commuting
- Clause 6 reimburses personal vehicle mileage at the IRS rate for outreach and program business.
- Under IRS regulations, ordinary commuting between an employee's personal residence and their primary work location (Carrollton campus) is personal and non-reimbursable.
- The agreement should clarify that routine commuting between home and the Carrollton campus is not reimbursable, but travel between campus and clinical/field affiliate sites or direct travel to outreach partner locations is fully reimbursable. A standard 30-day submission window should also be specified.

### G. Program Director Institutional Support & Regulatory Indemnification
- Georgia OEMS&T rules impose specific personal obligations on designated Program Directors (compliance audits, instructor qualification tracking, curriculum adherence, and student records).
- If the company faces a regulatory audit or compliance complaint, the Program Director is personally scrutinized by the state licensing authority.
- The offer letter should explicitly commit that MedEdPrep will provide the necessary institutional and administrative resources for compliance, and will defend and indemnify the Program Director against any third-party claims or regulatory enforcement actions arising out of the good-faith performance of his duties.

### H. Acceptance Deadline & E-Signature
- Line 166 currently reads: *"To accept, please sign below and return a copy at your convenience."*
- Open-ended offers create legal uncertainty. Standard commercial and employment practice requires a definite acceptance deadline (e.g., *"by [Date], after which this offer will expire unless extended in writing"*), along with express confirmation that electronic signatures (or scanned PDF copies) are fully binding.

## 4. Director Title vs. Hourly Reality: Handling the Tension

The juxtaposition of an executive title (**"Director of Education"**) with a **part-time, $40.00/hour, non-exempt, flexible schedule** creates a noticeable conceptual tension. In corporate or academic settings, a "Director" is typically an exempt salaried officer commanding $90,000–$140,000+, with substantial budget authority, hiring/firing power, and full institutional accountability.

### Why the Title is Justified
In small healthcare training programs, this dual reality is both practical and necessary:
1. **Regulatory & External Standing:** When Gene interfaces with Georgia OEMS&T inspectors, hospital education managers, and fire/EMS chiefs to secure clinical affiliation agreements, the title "Director of Education" provides essential institutional stature. A weaker title (such as "Lead Instructor" or "Program Coordinator") would undermine his authority in the field.
2. **True Programmatic Ownership:** Gene is indeed directing the educational delivery, designing stations, reviewing examinations, and guiding faculty. The title accurately reflects his subject-matter leadership.

### Where the Dissonance Becomes Dangerous
The tension becomes problematic when the job description uses heavy, enterprise-level verbs that imply 24/7 unassisted institutional liability:
- *"The Director of Education owns the delivery..."* (JD Line 63)
- *"Own regulatory compliance for the programs under this position's direction..."* (JD Line 83)
- *"Administer student enrollment, progression, remediation, and completion decisions."* (JD Line 85)

Telling a $40/hour part-time employee that they "own" regulatory compliance creates two serious risks:
1. **FLSA Off-the-Clock Exposure:** An employee who "owns" compliance feels personally responsible for solving emergencies at all hours. If an audit notice arrives or an urgent student clinical incident occurs on an unscheduled day, Gene will feel pressured to work off-the-clock, generating wage-and-hour liability.
2. **Compensation Resentment:** If Gene feels he carries the entire legal and operational weight of MedEdPrep's education division on his shoulders, an hourly rate of $40/hour will begin to feel inadequate for the perceived risk.

### The Solution: Keep the Title, Refine the Verbs
Do not demote the title or alter the part-time hourly structure. Instead:
- Shift the framing in the Job Description from absolute ownership to operational leadership: replace *"owns"* with *"leads"* and add *"in coordination with executive leadership."*
- Reaffirm in the offer letter that administrative and regulatory responsibilities are performed within mutually agreed scheduled hours, backed by company administrative resources.

**Verdict:** SHIP-WITH-TWEAKS

1. **Clause 11 (Work Product & IP):** Add an explicit Prior Works carve-out and cross-license to guarantee Gene retains ownership of his pre-existing teaching assets and scenarios while granting MedEdPrep a perpetual operating license.
2. **Clause 5 (Compensation & FLSA Compliance):** Add explicit timekeeping requirements mandating that all administrative, prep, and student correspondence hours be logged and paid, strictly prohibiting off-the-clock work, and requiring advance written overtime approval.
3. **Clause 1 & 9 (Program Director Support & Outside Activities):** Provide company indemnification and administrative backing for Gene's state Program Director regulatory duties, and establish a clear safe harbor for his outside flight/field clinical shifts and routine CE teaching.
