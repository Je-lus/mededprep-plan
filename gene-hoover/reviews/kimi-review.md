# Kimi Review - Gene Hoover Offer + JD

Overall take: this is an unusually well-constructed pair of documents for a company this size — the at-will, non-exempt/overtime, and confidentiality language is competent, the tone is genuinely personal without being sloppy, and the two documents track each other closely. The remaining risks are a small set of specific gaps (timekeeping/travel-time pay for an hourly role, an unseen NDA that "controls," and no pre-existing-IP carve-out) plus a few wording-level fixes; none of them requires restructuring either document.

## 1. Coherence

The framing hangs together well. The offer letter's clause 1 (delivery + program administration + Program Director designation + reports to CEO) matches the position description's Purpose section almost exactly. The JD header line — "Reports to: Chief Executive Officer · Location: Carrollton, Georgia campus, with travel to clinical and partner-agency sites · Status: Part-time, hourly, non-exempt" — is consistent with clauses 1, 3, 5, and 7 of the letter. The letter's outreach clause 2 is actually *more* candidate-protective than the JD ("you may be asked," "coordinated with you in advance," "reasonable notice"), while the JD lists the same travel as a flat responsibility — the letter is the document being signed, so this ordering favors you, but a careful candidate could read the JD as harder-edged than the letter. Minor.

The one genuine internal contradiction is the "other duties" language:

- Letter clause 1: "such other duties as the company may reasonably assign from time to time" — unbounded.
- JD General section: "other duties as needed at the direction of the company **in support of its educational mission**" — bounded by the mission.

The signed document (the letter) contains the broader grant. If a dispute ever arose over assigned work, the letter controls what he signed. Align the letter to the JD's mission-bounded version (wording fix #7 below).

Small mismatches worth noting but not necessarily changing: the letter says Gene "will be primarily responsible for the delivery" while the JD Purpose says the Director "owns the delivery" — trivial. The JD lists qualifications ("Meets Georgia OEMS&T program-director eligibility requirements") that the letter never converts into a continuing condition of employment — that's not a contradiction, but it is a coherence gap between "this role requires X" and "employment depends on keeping X" (fix #6).

## 2. How it reads to the candidate

The opening paragraph is genuinely good: "You have shaped our education from its first outline, and there is no one better suited to lead its delivery" is specific, earned praise — not boilerplate — and the closing ("this program exists because of the standard you hold it to") matches it. For a candidate who already helped build the program, this reads as recognition, not flattery. The personal-warm/legal-cold/personal-warm sandwich works because the legal middle is written in plain English rather than statute-speak.

Things that would give an experienced educator pause:

- **No stated hours.** The letter says part-time, flexible, mutual agreement — but never gives even an anticipated range. "Director of Education" with no hour expectation invites the question: is this 8 hours a week or 35? For someone holding other clinical roles (which clause 9 acknowledges), the hours question determines whether the offer is even schedulable. This is the single biggest candidate-experience gap.
- **Addressed to his MedEdPrep email.** The addressee block is "Gene Hoover / Gene@mededprep.com" — an offer of employment sent to a company address for a role he hasn't accepted yet reads as presumptuous, and a signed acceptance that lives only inside the company's own email system is bad hygiene for both parties. Use his personal email or a street address.
- **"Return a copy at your convenience."** No deadline, no destination. For a document establishing at-will employment, wages, and IP assignment, "at your convenience" is oddly casual — and there's no offer expiration, which is slightly risky for the company too (an open-ended offer of a specific hourly rate).
- **The JD Qualifications read thin next to the role's gravity.** "Instructor credentials." — which ones? "Experience in the delivery of education content." — awkward phrasing ("education content" instead of "educational content"), and no level or setting. "Bachelor's degree." — no field, no "or equivalent experience." For a document a state reviewer might later read, this section is noticeably weaker than the rest of the JD.

## 3. Legal/HR red flags

Beyond the five deliberate omissions, in rough order of importance:

1. **No timekeeping clause — the real gap for hourly non-exempt.** The letter gets non-exempt classification and overtime right ("one and one-half times your regular rate for hours worked over forty in a workweek"), which most small-company letters botch. But it never says hours must be recorded or that off-the-clock work is prohibited. For a flexible-schedule, partly-remote ("Administrative work may be performed remotely by arrangement") hourly employee, that is exactly the fact pattern that produces wage claims. One sentence fixes it.
2. **Outreach travel time is unaddressed.** Clause 2 creates travel obligations and clause 6 reimburses mileage, but neither says whether travel time to partner agencies counts as compensable hours. Under the FLSA, travel between worksites during the day generally is compensable for non-exempt employees. Silence here invites either a dispute or quiet non-compliance. State that it counts.
3. **The NDA "controls" but isn't attached.** Clause 10 makes the NDA a condition of employment and gives it priority over the letter ("in the event of any conflict regarding confidentiality, the NDA controls"), but the letter doesn't enclose it. Gene is being asked to sign a letter subordinating itself to a document he hasn't seen. At minimum, enclose the NDA with the offer and say so; ideally have both signed the same day.
4. **IP clause has no carve-out for pre-existing / outside materials.** Clause 11's scope is actually well-drafted — "within the scope of your employment," plus the general-skills savings sentence is good. But this is a part-time educator with an existing career who will almost certainly bring scenarios, exam items, and teaching materials he created before (and outside) this job. The first time one of his pre-existing scenarios ends up in a MedEdPrep course pack, the assignment clause arguably sweeps it in. A background-IP carve-out with a license-back is standard for exactly this situation and costs the company nothing it needs.
5. **No contingency on required credentials.** The JD requires NRP, CCP-C/FP-C, instructor credentials, and OEMS&T program-director eligibility; the letter never conditions the offer or continued employment on maintaining them. At-will means you can still terminate, but a one-line contingency removes any argument that the role could be performed without the credentials.
6. **BYOD clause is one sentence with real operational ambiguity.** "Any device you use for MedEdPrep work must be password-protected and encrypted" — encrypted how (full-disk? at rest?), and is the company really requiring a part-time hourly employee to encrypt a personal device without offering a company one? Clause 8's first sentence covers specialized tools, but a laptop isn't "specialized." Either offer a device on request or accept that this clause will be honored in the breach.
7. **Outside-activities clause is disclosure-only with no destination.** It's appropriately light for a part-time role (disclosure, not pre-approval — good), but "you agree to disclose" to whom, in what form? "Disclose in writing to the CEO" suffices.
8. **No acceptance deadline** (noted above) — also a company-side risk: an unexpired offer of a specific rate.
9. Minor: the employer's signature block has no date line (only the acceptance block dates signatures); dating both is cleaner. And the letterhead gives the CEO's email and website but no company mailing address anywhere in either document — unusual for a document that may end up in a regulatory file.

## 4. Title-vs-terms tension

Handled better than expected, mostly because the JD does the work: the Purpose section defines the Director's scope as delivery plus "the administration that keeps the programs under this position's direction in good standing," outreach is explicitly *secondary*, and the letter softens it further with consent-based language ("you may be asked," "coordinated with you in advance"). The documents never pretend the role is full-time, and clause 9's explicit blessing of outside roles converts the potential "grand title, modest terms" awkwardness into candor: everyone knows what this is.

Two residual friction points:

- The JD's General "other duties" clause ("Perform other duties as needed at the direction of the company") sits under a part-time hourly header. Because he's hourly and paid for all hours worked (clause 5 says exactly that), scope creep is paid creep — the risk is low, but the *letter's* unbounded version should still be pulled into line with the JD's mission-bounded one (fix #7).
- The tonal shift. Honestly, it works — the warmth is in the right places (first paragraph, last paragraph) and the legal middle never cosplays as friendship. The one line I'd touch is the closer: "I am looking forward to building it with you for a long time." Against clause 3's "at any time, with or without cause and with or without notice," "for a long time" is the only sentence in either document that could be quoted back as implying duration. The explicit "Nothing in this letter creates a contract of employment for any fixed period" makes the legal risk negligible, but the optics of promising longevity two paragraphs after disclaiming it are avoidable (fix #8).

## 5. Concrete wording improvements

**1. Add timekeeping to the compensation clause (offer letter, clause 5).**

> **Current:** "Your hours will be flexible and scheduled by mutual agreement."
>
> **Proposed:** "Your hours will be flexible and scheduled by mutual agreement. You will record all hours worked in MedEdPrep's timekeeping system each week; MedEdPrep does not permit off-the-clock work, and time spent on any assigned duty — including outreach travel time — counts as hours worked."

**2. Bound the outreach travel obligation with compensability (offer letter, clause 2).**

> **Current:** "Outreach assignments will be scheduled with reasonable notice and with regard to your primary teaching calendar."
>
> **Proposed:** "Outreach assignments will be scheduled with reasonable notice and with regard to your primary teaching calendar. Travel time to and from outreach sites counts as hours worked and is paid like other duties, in addition to the mileage reimbursement described below."

**3. State an anticipated hours range (offer letter, clause 3 or 5).**

> **Current:** "This is a part-time, hourly, W-2 position." (with no hours anywhere in the letter)
>
> **Proposed (add a sentence):** "We anticipate the role will average approximately 15–20 hours per week, varying with the course calendar; this is an estimate, not a guarantee of hours in any week." *(Use whatever range is actually intended — the point is that some number must exist.)*

**4. Enclose the NDA (offer letter, clause 10).**

> **Current:** "As a condition of employment, you will execute MedEdPrep's Confidentiality and Non-Disclosure Agreement (the "NDA")."
>
> **Proposed:** "A copy of MedEdPrep's Confidentiality and Non-Disclosure Agreement (the "NDA") is enclosed with this letter; your execution of it is a condition of employment."

**5. Add a background-IP carve-out (offer letter, clause 11).**

> **Current:** "Nothing in this engagement restricts your use of the general skills, methods, and knowledge you develop."
>
> **Proposed:** "Nothing in this engagement restricts your use of the general skills, methods, and knowledge you develop. Materials you created before your employment with MedEdPrep, or create wholly outside it, remain yours; if you incorporate any such material into a MedEdPrep deliverable, you grant MedEdPrep a non-exclusive, royalty-free license to use it as part of that deliverable."

**6. Condition the role on required credentials (offer letter, clause 1).**

> **Current:** "You will hold the Program Director designation for MedEdPrep's state-designated programs."
>
> **Proposed:** "You will hold the Program Director designation for MedEdPrep's state-designated programs; continued employment in this position is contingent on your maintaining the licensure, certifications, and Georgia OEMS&T program-director eligibility the position requires."

**7. Align "other duties" with the JD (offer letter, clause 1).**

> **Current:** "You will also perform such other duties as the company may reasonably assign from time to time."
>
> **Proposed:** "You will also perform such other duties in support of the company's educational mission as the company may reasonably assign from time to time."

**8. Soften the longevity promise (offer letter, closing paragraph).**

> **Current:** "Gene, this program exists because of the standard you hold it to. I am looking forward to building it with you for a long time."
>
> **Proposed:** "Gene, this program exists because of the standard you hold it to. I am looking forward to building it with you."

**9. Give the acceptance real logistics (offer letter).**

> **Current:** "To accept, please sign below and return a copy at your convenience."
>
> **Proposed:** "To accept, please sign below and return a copy to me by [acceptance date]; this offer remains open until that date."

**10. Fix the addressee block (offer letter).**

> **Current:** "Gene Hoover / Gene@mededprep.com"
>
> **Proposed:** "Gene Hoover" with his personal email or street address — not the company address for the role he hasn't accepted.

**11. Tighten the vague JD qualifications (position description).**

> **Current:** "Instructor credentials." / "Experience in the delivery of education content." / "Bachelor's degree."
>
> **Proposed:** "Current Georgia EMS instructor credential, and AHA instructor credentials appropriate to MedEdPrep's course offerings." / "Demonstrated experience delivering EMS education to initial-certification and continuing-education audiences." / "Bachelor's degree, or equivalent experience in EMS education."

**12. Add a disclosure destination (offer letter, clause 9).**

> **Current:** "you agree to disclose any outside role that could reasonably create such a conflict."
>
> **Proposed:** "you agree to disclose in writing to the CEO any outside role that could reasonably create such a conflict."

**Verdict:** SHIP-WITH-TWEAKS

1. Add the timekeeping sentence and make outreach travel time expressly compensable (fixes the one real wage-and-hour exposure in the letter — improvements #1 and #2).
2. Enclose the NDA with the letter, since the letter says it controls (improvement #4).
3. Add the pre-existing/outside-materials carve-out to the IP clause, so Gene's existing body of teaching material isn't arguably captured (improvement #5).
